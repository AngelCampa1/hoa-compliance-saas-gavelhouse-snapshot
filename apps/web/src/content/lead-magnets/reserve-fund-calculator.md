## What This Calculator Measures

A reserve fund is money set aside for major repairs and replacements - roofs, parking lots, elevators, pool resurfacing, building exteriors. These expenses are predictable in the sense that every component has a useful life. You know the roof will need replacement. You just need to plan for when and how much.

The percent-funded method compares your current reserve balance to your "fully funded" balance - the theoretical amount you should have saved by now given the age and remaining life of all your components. A fully funded association has 100% of what it needs on hand to replace every component on schedule. That's the ideal. Reality is usually worse.

This calculator walks you through the math step by step. You don't need accounting software or a reserve study professional to run it. You need your most recent financial statement and basic information about your community's major components.

## Step 1: Component Inventory

List every major component your association is responsible for maintaining or replacing. Focus on items that cost $5,000+ to repair or replace. Small maintenance items (light bulbs, cleaning supplies) come from the operating budget, not reserves.

### Component Inventory Template

| Component               | Useful Life (years) | Current Age (years) | Remaining Life (years) | Replacement Cost (current $) |
| ----------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Roof (building A)       | 25                  | 12                  | 13                     | $180,000                     |
| Roof (building B)       | 25                  | 12                  | 13                     | $180,000                     |
| Parking lot resurfacing | 15                  | 8                   | 7                      | $45,000                      |
| Exterior paint          | 10                  | 6                   | 4                      | $35,000                      |
| Pool resurfacing        | 12                  | 9                   | 3                      | $25,000                      |
| HVAC system (clubhouse) | 20                  | 14                  | 6                      | $28,000                      |
| Elevator modernization  | 25                  | 18                  | 7                      | $95,000                      |
| Fencing/gates           | 20                  | 10                  | 10                     | $22,000                      |

**Where to get replacement costs:** Get quotes from contractors for the top 3-4 most expensive items. For others, your property manager or a quick online search for "[component] replacement cost per square foot" gives you a reasonable estimate. These don't need to be precise - you're looking for order-of-magnitude accuracy.

**Where to get useful life estimates:** Industry reference tables are published by organizations like the Community Associations Institute (CAI). Your previous reserve study (if you have one) lists useful life estimates. General rules of thumb: asphalt shingle roofs 20-30 years, flat roofs 15-25 years, asphalt paving 15-20 years, exterior paint 7-12 years, pool plaster 8-12 years.

## Step 2: Calculate the Fully Funded Balance

For each component, calculate what your fund "should" have right now based on how much of the component's useful life has been consumed.

**Per-component funded balance = (Current Age / Useful Life) x Replacement Cost**

Using the roof example:

- Current Age: 12 years
- Useful Life: 25 years
- Replacement Cost: $180,000
- Funded Balance Should Be: (12 / 25) x $180,000 = **$86,400**

The logic: if the roof lasts 25 years and costs $180,000 to replace, you should be saving $7,200 per year for that roof. After 12 years, you should have $86,400 set aside for it.

### Fully Funded Balance Calculation

| Component      | Current Age | Useful Life | Replacement Cost | Should Have Saved |
| -------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ----------------- |
| Roof A         | 12          | 25          | $180,000         | $86,400           |
| Roof B         | 12          | 25          | $180,000         | $86,400           |
| Parking lot    | 8           | 15          | $45,000          | $24,000           |
| Exterior paint | 6           | 10          | $35,000          | $21,000           |
| Pool           | 9           | 12          | $25,000          | $18,750           |
| HVAC           | 14          | 20          | $28,000          | $19,600           |
| Elevator       | 18          | 25          | $95,000          | $68,400           |
| Fencing        | 10          | 20          | $22,000          | $11,000           |
| **TOTAL**      |             |             | **$610,000**     | **$335,550**      |

Your **Ideal Fully Funded Balance** is the sum of the "Should Have Saved" column. In this example: $335,550.

## Step 3: Calculate Percent Funded

Now compare your actual reserve balance to the ideal:

**Percent Funded = (Actual Reserve Balance / Ideal Fully Funded Balance) x 100**

Pull your current reserve fund balance from your most recent financial statement. This is the money in your reserve account(s), not your operating account.

Example:

- Actual Reserve Balance: $185,000
- Ideal Fully Funded Balance: $335,550
- Percent Funded: ($185,000 / $335,550) x 100 = **55.1%**

### What Your Score Means

| Percent Funded | Rating        | What It Means                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **70-100%**    | Strong        | Your fund is in good shape. Maintain current contribution levels and adjust for inflation.                                                                                       |
| **50-69%**     | Fair          | You can handle near-term replacements but may struggle if multiple components need work in the same year. Consider increasing annual contributions by 10-20%.                    |
| **30-49%**     | Below Average | A special assessment is likely within 3-5 years unless contributions increase significantly. Run scenarios for your earliest-due components.                                     |
| **0-29%**      | Critical      | The fund cannot cover near-term replacements. A special assessment is almost certain. Commission a professional reserve study immediately and present options to the membership. |

## Step 4: Annual Contribution Analysis

Once you know your percent funded, calculate what you should be contributing each year to reach and maintain healthy funding.

### Method A: Straight-Line Contribution per Component

For each component, the annual contribution needed is:

**Annual Contribution = Replacement Cost / Useful Life**

| Component      | Replacement Cost | Useful Life | Annual Contribution |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ----------- | ------------------- |
| Roof A         | $180,000         | 25          | $7,200              |
| Roof B         | $180,000         | 25          | $7,200              |
| Parking lot    | $45,000          | 15          | $3,000              |
| Exterior paint | $35,000          | 10          | $3,500              |
| Pool           | $25,000          | 12          | $2,083              |
| HVAC           | $28,000          | 20          | $1,400              |
| Elevator       | $95,000          | 25          | $3,800              |
| Fencing        | $22,000          | 20          | $1,100              |
| **TOTAL**      |                  |             | **$29,283/year**    |

Divide by the number of units in your community to get the per-unit monthly reserve contribution:

- 50 units: $29,283 / 50 / 12 = **$48.81/unit/month**
- 100 units: $29,283 / 100 / 12 = **$24.40/unit/month**

### Method B: Catch-Up Contribution

If you're below 70% funded, you need to catch up. The catch-up amount depends on how quickly you want to reach target funding.

**Annual Catch-Up = (Ideal Balance - Actual Balance) / Years to Catch Up**

Using our example:

- Gap: $335,550 - $185,000 = $150,550
- 5-year catch-up: $150,550 / 5 = $30,110/year additional
- 10-year catch-up: $150,550 / 10 = $15,055/year additional

Add the catch-up amount to the straight-line contribution:

- 5-year plan: $29,283 + $30,110 = $59,393/year total
- 10-year plan: $29,283 + $15,055 = $44,338/year total

Present both options to the board and membership. The 5-year plan means higher assessments now. The 10-year plan means lower monthly costs but more risk if a major expense hits early.

## Step 5: Cash Flow Projection

The percent-funded number tells you where you are today. A cash flow projection tells you whether you'll have enough when expenses actually hit.

Create a 30-year projection with these columns:

| Year | Beginning Balance | Contributions | Interest Earned | Expenditures    | Ending Balance |
| ---- | ----------------- | ------------- | --------------- | --------------- | -------------- |
| 2026 | $185,000          | $29,283       | $3,700          | $0              | $217,983       |
| 2027 | $217,983          | $29,283       | $4,360          | $0              | $251,626       |
| 2028 | $251,626          | $29,283       | $5,033          | -$25,000 (pool) | $260,942       |
| ...  |                   |               |                 |                 |                |

Fill in expenditure years based on when each component's remaining life runs out. If the balance goes negative in any year, you either need higher contributions or a special assessment in that year.

**Interest rate assumption:** Use 2-3% for money market or CD rates on reserve funds. Don't assume stock market returns - reserve funds should be in low-risk, liquid investments.

**Inflation adjustment:** Add 2-3% per year to replacement costs. A roof that costs $180,000 today will cost more in 13 years. Many boards skip this adjustment and then face sticker shock when the bill arrives.

## Limitations of the Self-Assessment

This calculator gives you a solid working estimate, but it has limits:

- **Replacement costs are estimates.** Without contractor quotes or engineering assessments, you might be off by 15-25%.
- **Useful life varies.** Climate, maintenance quality, and materials all affect how long components last. A roof in Phoenix ages differently than one in Seattle.
- **Hidden components.** You may have infrastructure (plumbing, electrical, structural elements) that isn't visible and doesn't appear on your inventory.
- **No condition assessment.** A professional reserve study includes a physical inspection that may reveal components need replacement sooner or later than the standard useful life suggests.

If your percent-funded score is below 50%, or if you have components with replacement costs over $200,000, invest in a professional reserve study. The $3,000-$8,000 cost pays for itself by giving the board defensible numbers when the time comes to raise assessments or levy a special assessment. Some states actually require a professional study at regular intervals - check your state's HOA statutes.

## Why a Standardized Meeting Agenda Protects Your Board

Running an HOA board meeting without a structured agenda is one of the fastest ways to generate liability exposure. Homeowners can challenge votes made without proper notice. Vendors can dispute contracts approved during meetings where quorum was never confirmed. State regulators in Florida, California, and Texas increasingly scrutinize whether boards followed their own governing documents.

A written agenda does three legally important things: it anchors the meeting to the notice posted in advance (most states require the agenda be published with meeting notice), it establishes the order of business so quorum is confirmed before any votes are taken, and it creates a traceable structure that your meeting minutes can follow verbatim. When a dispute arises months later, the agenda plus minutes is the board's primary documentary defense.

## The Essential Agenda Sections and Their Order

A compliant HOA board meeting agenda should follow this sequence:

1. **Call to Order** - The presiding officer calls the meeting to order and states the date, time, and location.
2. **Quorum Confirmation** - The secretary confirms whether a quorum of board members is present. If not, the meeting must adjourn.
3. **Approval of Prior Meeting Minutes** - Board votes to approve (or correct and approve) the minutes from the previous meeting.
4. **Treasurer's Report** - Financial update including operating account balance, budget-to-actual variance, outstanding receivables, and reserve fund status.
5. **Reserve Fund Update** - Separate line item reporting current reserve balance, funded percentage vs. reserve study target, and any upcoming expenditures from reserves.
6. **Old Business** - Action items carried from prior meetings, with status updates and votes as needed.
7. **New Business** - New motions, vendor proposals, policy changes, and board decisions.
8. **Open Forum** - Homeowners address the board (typically 2-3 minutes per speaker, no board action during this segment).
9. **Executive Session** (if required) - Board moves to closed session for qualifying topics only.
10. **Adjournment** - Presiding officer adjourns and the secretary records the time.

## How to Handle Financial Reports Properly

The treasurer's report is the most scrutinized section of any board meeting. It should cover: operating account balance, month-to-date and year-to-date actual vs. budgeted income and expenses, accounts receivable aging (total delinquencies, not individual owner names), and current reserve fund balance.

The reserve fund update deserves its own line item - not a footnote inside the treasurer's report. Report the current balance, the target balance from your reserve study, and the funded percentage. If reserves are below 70% funded, the board should note what the current funding plan is. This signals to homeowners and any future buyers that the board takes its fiduciary duty seriously.

Never commingle operating and reserve fund figures. Reporting them as a single "total cash" number obscures whether reserves are being drawn down to cover operating shortfalls - a compliance violation in most states.

## Executive Session - Rules and When to Use It

Executive session is not a tool for avoiding uncomfortable discussions with homeowners. It is a legally defined exception for four categories: pending or threatened litigation, personnel matters (including management company performance reviews), delinquent owner hearings, and contract negotiations where disclosure would harm the association's bargaining position.

To enter executive session correctly: the board votes to enter in open session, notes the general subject category in the regular minutes, and then reconvenes in closed session. A separate set of executive session minutes is kept by the secretary and stored confidentially - not distributed with regular meeting minutes.

Improper use of executive session (discussing general policy, vendor complaints, or neighbor disputes) is a common source of homeowner legal challenges.

## Robert's Rules Basics for HOA Boards

Most governing documents reference Robert's Rules of Order as the default parliamentary authority. In practice, small HOA boards benefit from a simplified version:

- **Making a motion** - "I move that we approve the vendor contract for $4,200."
- **Seconding** - Another board member seconds to confirm the motion merits discussion.
- **Discussion** - Only board members speak during formal deliberation.
- **Vote** - The presiding officer calls the vote and records yeas, nays, and abstentions.
- **Recording** - The secretary records the motion text and exact vote count in the minutes.

Abstentions are not votes and do not count toward the majority needed for passage. A board member with a financial conflict of interest must recuse and should not remain in the room for that vote.

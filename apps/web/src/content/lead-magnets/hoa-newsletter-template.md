This template gives your board a complete newsletter structure you can fill in, customize, and send each month or quarter. Each section includes placeholder text showing the format - replace the bracketed items with your community's specifics.

---

## SECTION 1: NEWSLETTER HEADER (FREE PREVIEW)

```text
[COMMUNITY NAME] HOMEOWNERS ASSOCIATION
Community Newsletter - [Month Year]

Published by the Board of Directors
[Community Name] HOA | [City, State]
[Website or contact email]
```

**Formatting notes:** Put the community name and date prominently at the top. Include a contact email so homeowners know how to reach the board without hunting for information. If you have a community website, include the URL here - this is where you post the digital version.

---

## SECTION 2: FROM THE BOARD (FREE PREVIEW)

```text
FROM THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

Dear [Community Name] Homeowners,

[Opening line summarizing the month's main topic - a project completion,
upcoming vote, or seasonal reminder. Keep to 1-2 sentences.]

This month's newsletter covers:
• [Topic 1 - e.g., Reserve fund status update]
• [Topic 2 - e.g., Pool opening date and rules]
• [Topic 3 - e.g., Parking enforcement reminder]

[Optional: one sentence on the most recent board meeting outcome.]

The Board of Directors
[Community Name] HOA
```

**Formatting notes:** The "From the Board" section sets the tone for the issue. Keep it factual and brief - three bullet points is enough. Homeowners will read the sections below for details. Do not use this space for editorial opinions or enforcement language.

---

## SECTION 3: BOARD DECISIONS AND ACTIONS

```text
BOARD DECISIONS - [Month Year]

The board met on [Date]. Decisions made at that meeting:

• APPROVED: [Description of action, e.g., "Vendor contract with XYZ Landscaping
  for $18,400 annually, beginning [Date]"]
• APPROVED: [Description of action]
• TABLED: [Item tabled and reason, e.g., "Pool resurfacing bid - board requested
  two additional quotes before deciding"]
• DISCUSSED (no vote): [Topic discussed without a formal decision]

Next board meeting: [Date], [Time], [Location]
[Note whether homeowners may attend the open portion]
```

**Formatting notes:** This is the most important section. Every board decision should appear here within days of the meeting, and in the newsletter as permanent distribution. Do not copy the full minutes - write one sentence per decision in plain language. For items tabled or deferred, say so and explain why. Homeowners should never be surprised at the annual meeting by decisions that were already made.

---

## SECTION 4: FINANCIAL SUMMARY

```text
FINANCIAL SUMMARY - As of [Date]

Operating Fund
  Current balance:     $[Amount]
  Budget YTD:          $[Amount]
  Actual YTD:          $[Amount]
  Variance:            $[Amount over/under]

Reserve Fund
  Current balance:     $[Amount]
  Funded percentage:   [X]%
  Last reserve study:  [Year]

[Optional note: 1-2 sentences explaining any significant variance or
upcoming expense. Example: "The operating fund is $2,100 under budget
for the year due to lower-than-expected landscaping costs in Q1."]

Note: Full financial statements are available to homeowners upon request
in accordance with [State] law and our governing documents.
```

**Formatting notes:** Use a simple table format, not a full balance sheet. The numbers homeowners care about: operating balance, reserve balance, and whether you are on track with the budget. If you are over budget by a significant amount, explain why - silence breeds rumors. The note about full statements being available is good transparency practice and satisfies many states' open records requirements.

## SECTION 5: MAINTENANCE AND PROJECTS

```text
MAINTENANCE UPDATES

COMPLETED:
• [Project name] - [Brief description and outcome, e.g., "East parking lot
  crack sealing completed March 15. No further work needed until 2028."]

IN PROGRESS:
• [Project name] - [Status and expected completion, e.g., "Pool deck
  resurfacing begins May 15. Pool will be closed May 15-17."]

UPCOMING (Next 90 Days):
• [Project name] - [Planned start date and brief description]
• [Project name] - [Planned start date and brief description]

Questions about maintenance? Contact [Name] at [email] or attend the
next board meeting.
```

**Formatting notes:** Homeowners notice physical work happening around the community. Pre-empting questions with a structured update section prevents dozens of individual inquiries to board members. Include any project that will affect parking, pool access, gate operation, or daily routine - and give at least two weeks' notice before work begins.

---

## SECTION 6: RULE REMINDERS

```text
COMMUNITY REMINDER - [Month's Focus Topic]

[Rule or requirement in plain language. Example:]

TRASH AND RECYCLING CONTAINERS
Per Section [X.X] of our Rules and Regulations: containers must be stored
out of street view except on collection day. Containers may be placed at
the curb after [time] the evening before collection and must be returned
to storage by [time] the evening of collection day.

Questions about this requirement? Contact [Name] at [email].
```

**Formatting notes:** Choose one or two rules per issue and rotate through the year. Frame rule reminders as information, not enforcement threats. Cite the governing document section number - it signals that the rule is not a board preference but a document requirement. Save violation notices for direct, individual communication; they never belong in a community newsletter.

## SECTION 7: UPCOMING EVENTS AND DATES

```text
MARK YOUR CALENDAR

[DATE] - Board Meeting, [Time], [Location]
           Open to homeowners. Agenda posted at [location/website]
           [X] days in advance per our bylaws.

[DATE] - [Community Event, e.g., Annual Pool Opening, Welcome Spring
          Cleanup, Holiday Lighting Contest deadline]

[DATE] - [Annual Meeting - include date as soon as it is confirmed]

[DATE] - Assessment due date (if a non-standard date applies)
```

**Formatting notes:** Include the annual meeting date in every newsletter once it is set, even months in advance. The more notice homeowners have, the better attendance you will get. For regular board meetings, note that homeowners can attend - this satisfies the open meeting requirement many states impose and builds community trust.

## SECTION 8: BOARD CONTACT DIRECTORY

```text
YOUR BOARD OF DIRECTORS

President:      [Name] | [email]
Vice President: [Name] | [email]
Secretary:      [Name] | [email]
Treasurer:      [Name] | [email]
Member at Large: [Name] | [email]

General inquiries: [boardemail@community.com]
Maintenance emergencies: [emergency contact or after-hours number]
Management company (if applicable): [Name], [phone], [email]

[Community Website]: [URL]
```

**Formatting notes:** Update this section any time there is a board member change. Include a general board email address so homeowners have one contact point regardless of which board member is best suited for their question. For maintenance emergencies - burst pipes, gate failures, security issues - include an after-hours contact or process. Homeowners who cannot find contact information will post publicly in community Facebook groups instead.

## USING THIS TEMPLATE

**For email delivery:** Copy each section into your email system (Gmail, Mailchimp, or your HOA software's communication tool). Use the section headers as subject-line subheadings. Keep the total email readable in under three minutes.

**For printed delivery:** Import into any word processor. The section structure formats cleanly in two-column layouts if you want a more polished printed look. Standard letter-size paper at 11pt body text fits four sections per page.

**Archive every issue:** Store a dated copy in your board records with the distribution date and method documented. This archive is your evidence of consistent communication if a dispute ever goes to mediation.

**Legal notice reminder:** This newsletter template covers routine communication. It does not satisfy the individual written notice requirements for assessment increases, annual meeting notice, or proposed rule changes in most states. Always use individual delivery - mail or documented email - for legally required notices.

## Why budget preparation delays happen and how a checklist prevents them

Every year, HOA boards face the same budget preparation crunch. The budget needs to be ready before the homeowner distribution deadline. The distribution deadline is 14-30 days before the board vote, depending on governing documents and state law. The board vote needs to happen before the budget year begins. And yet, year after year, boards find themselves scrambling in the final weeks.

The reason is not lack of effort. It is lack of structure. Budget preparation for a self-managed HOA involves collecting data from multiple sources, making judgment calls on expense projections that require context from the prior year, calculating a reserve contribution rate that matches the reserve study recommendations, reviewing the draft with the full board, and then executing the homeowner distribution in the format required by governing documents.

Without a checklist that assigns each task to a phase and tracks completion, some tasks always get pushed to the last minute - and the last-minute rush produces errors that have consequences. An incorrect reserve contribution rate. A homeowner distribution that went out with the wrong effective date. A board vote on a budget that was never properly distributed to homeowners.

This checklist covers the full budget preparation process from 90 days out to adoption day. The phases are designed to match the reality of how volunteer board members work - not in concentrated blocks, but in small windows of time spread across several months.

## Phase 1: Data gathering (90+ days before budget year)

The budget draft depends on accurate data about current and projected costs. Gathering that data is the first phase, and it should start 90 days before the new budget year begins.

**Pull the current year's actual expenses by category.** You need the year-to-date actuals for every expense category, compared to the current year budget. This comparison tells you where the budget was accurate and where it was off. Categories where actuals exceed budget will likely need higher allocations in the next year.

**Request vendor contracts and renewal quotes.** Landscaping, insurance, pool maintenance, elevator maintenance, gate system maintenance, trash collection - any service with an annual contract is going to reprice at renewal. Contact vendors 90 days out to confirm renewal rates. Insurance renewals often come in 30-45 days before the policy expiration, which can be too late if your budget has already been distributed to homeowners.

**Collect capital repair and maintenance estimates.** If any significant repairs or maintenance projects are planned for the coming year, get written estimates from contractors. Unplanned capital expenditures are the most common source of budget overruns.

**Pull the reserve study funding plan.** Your reserve study includes a funding plan that specifies the recommended reserve contribution for each year. If your most recent study is from several years ago, the contribution recommendation may be out of date. Check the study's currency and note the recommended annual contribution.

**Review delinquency rates and collection history.** If the community has a meaningful delinquency rate, the budget should account for it. Assuming 100% assessment collection when 5% of units are routinely delinquent understates the real revenue available for expenses.

**Confirm unit count and assessment structure.** Verify that the total unit count and assessment breakdown (if different tiers apply to different unit types) are current. Changes in the ownership roster or assessment structure need to be reflected in the budget.

## Phase 2: Expense projection (60 days before budget year)

With data in hand, the treasurer can build the expense projections that form the draft budget.

**Project each expense category.** For recurring expenses with known renewal rates, use the vendor's confirmed renewal price. For categories without firm quotes, apply a conservative inflation factor (typically 3-7% depending on category) to the current year actuals.

**Flag one-time items separately.** A roof replacement, parking lot resurfacing, or clubhouse renovation is not a recurring operating expense. These items belong either in the capital budget (funded from reserves) or as a separately disclosed item in the operating budget if there is no reserve funding. Mixing one-time capital items into the regular operating budget makes year-over-year comparison misleading.

**Calculate the reserve contribution.** The reserve contribution should be the amount your reserve study recommends for the coming year. That number is in the funding plan section of the study - typically expressed as an annual dollar amount or as a percentage of total assessments. If the community is currently underfunded, the study may recommend a "catch-up" contribution rate that is higher than the regular ongoing rate.

For communities using Gavelhouse, reserve balances stay visible alongside operating activity. Boards should use their reserve study or spreadsheet to calculate percent-funded ratio and funding trajectory before explaining reserve contributions to homeowners.

**Calculate the per-unit assessment.** Total projected expenses plus reserve contribution, divided by the number of units (adjusted for any differential assessment structure), equals the monthly assessment. If the result represents a significant increase over the current year, the board needs to decide whether to phase it in over multiple years (if governing documents permit) or absorb it in a single year.

**Build contingency into operating expenses.** Most experienced HOA boards include a contingency line of 5-10% of projected operating expenses. Unexpected repairs, delinquency-related shortfalls, and minor capital items that do not reach the reserve threshold all land in this line.

## Phase 3: Draft review and revision (45 days before budget year)

The draft budget should be reviewed by the full board, not just the treasurer. Other board members have visibility into upcoming issues - the president may know about a pending vendor negotiation, the maintenance committee member may have flagged a repair that is not yet reflected in the projections.

**Circulate the draft to all board members at least two weeks before the review meeting.** Board members need time to read the document and ask questions before the meeting. A budget review that happens at the same meeting where the draft is first distributed is not a real review.

**Review against the prior year budget and actuals side by side.** The three-column comparison - prior year budget, prior year actual, proposed budget - is the most useful format for budget review. It shows where projections were accurate, where they were off, and why the new budget differs from the prior year.

**Document the board's reasoning for significant changes.** If the assessment is increasing by more than the prior year, or if a reserve contribution line is being added or significantly changed, the minutes should reflect the board's rationale. This documentation matters if homeowners challenge the increase.

**Confirm the reserve contribution calculation against the reserve study.** The board should verify that the reserve contribution in the draft budget matches the recommendation in the most recent reserve study. If the board is deviating from the study's recommendation (funding less than recommended), that decision should be documented explicitly.

## Phase 4: Homeowner distribution and board adoption

**Distribute the proposed budget to homeowners within the required notice period.** Check your governing documents and state law for the required timeline. Most require 14-30 days of advance distribution before the board vote. The distribution should include the proposed budget, the effective date, and any explanation of significant changes.

**Keep the distribution format accessible.** A PDF attachment works for homeowners who check email regularly. For homeowners who have opted for paper communications, mail the distribution. Track the distribution in your communications system so you have delivery records.

**Hold the board meeting and vote on the budget.** The vote should be in the meeting agenda, and the motion should specify the effective date and total monthly assessment rate. Record the vote in the meeting minutes with specificity: the proposed amount, the vote count, and the effective date.

**Update the assessment schedule in your accounting system.** Once the budget is adopted, the new assessment rate should be reflected in your accounting system immediately. Any homeowners on autopay should receive advance notice of the rate change.

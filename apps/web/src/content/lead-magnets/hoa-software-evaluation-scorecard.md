## Why Standard Software Reviews Don't Help HOA Boards

Software review sites rank HOA tools based on feature counts, enterprise scalability, and property management capabilities. That's useful if you're a property management company overseeing 200 communities. It's mostly irrelevant if you're a volunteer treasurer managing one 80-unit condo association on evenings and weekends.

Self-managed HOA boards have different priorities. You need fund accounting that separates operating and reserve funds correctly. You need an interface simple enough that the next treasurer (who might have zero accounting background) can pick up without training. You need pricing that doesn't punish small communities with per-unit fees that add up to more than your software budget allows.

This scorecard is designed for that context. Rate each tool you're considering on six categories, weight them based on your board's priorities, and compare the totals.

**Scoring scale:**

- **5** - Excellent for self-managed communities
- **4** - Good, minor compromises
- **3** - Adequate but has gaps
- **2** - Weak, requires workarounds
- **1** - Not suitable for volunteer boards

## Category 1: Accounting and Fund Management (Weight: High)

HOA accounting is not the same as small business accounting. The single biggest requirement is proper fund accounting - keeping operating funds, reserve funds, and any special assessment funds separate with full audit trails. QuickBooks and generic accounting tools get this wrong because they're not built for it.

**What to evaluate:**

- **Fund separation:** Does the software natively support multiple funds (operating, reserve, special assessment) with separate reporting?
- **Assessment tracking:** Can it generate and track assessments per unit, including partial payments, late fees, and payment plans?
- **Bank reconciliation:** Can you reconcile against bank statements within the software?
- **Budget vs. actual reporting:** Can you run a report showing budgeted vs. actual spending by category and fund?
- **Accounts payable:** Can you track vendor invoices and payments?
- **Year-end reporting:** Does it generate the reports your CPA needs for the annual review or tax filing (Form 1120-H)?
- **Reserve fund tracking:** Can you track reserve contributions and expenditures separately from operating?

**Score 5 if:** Native HOA fund accounting, assessment tracking per unit, reserve tracking built in, generates CPA-ready reports.

**Score 1 if:** Generic accounting with no fund separation, no assessment tracking, manual workarounds needed for basic HOA reporting.

## Category 2: Compliance and Governance Features (Weight: Medium-High)

HOA boards have legal obligations that generic project management or accounting tools don't address: meeting notice requirements, document retention, voting procedures, and architectural review workflows.

**What to evaluate:**

- **Document storage:** Can you store and organize governing documents (CC&Rs, bylaws, rules, meeting minutes, financial statements) with version history?
- **Meeting management:** Does it help with meeting notices, agenda creation, minutes recording, and action item tracking?
- **Violation tracking:** Can you document violations, send notices, and track resolution?
- **Architectural review:** Can homeowners submit modification requests and the board track approval workflows?
- **Election/voting:** Does it support online voting with proper authentication?
- **Audit trail:** Are changes to financial records logged with timestamps and user attribution?

**Score 5 if:** Built-in HOA governance workflows (violations, architectural review, voting), document storage with versioning, full audit trail.

**Score 1 if:** No governance features, document storage limited to file uploads with no organization, no audit logging.

## Category 3: Ease of Use for Volunteers (Weight: High)

This is the category that separates HOA-specific tools from property management platforms. Your treasurer changes every 1-3 years. Your board members have day jobs. Nobody has time for a 40-hour certification course.

**What to evaluate:**

- **Time to competency:** How long does it take a new board member (with no prior experience with the tool) to perform basic tasks? Measure in hours, not days.
- **Interface clarity:** Are common tasks (record a payment, generate a financial report, send a notice) findable in 2-3 clicks?
- **Onboarding materials:** Are there video tutorials, help docs, or guided walkthroughs?
- **Mobile access:** Can board members review reports or approve items from their phone?
- **Role-based access:** Can you give the treasurer full accounting access while giving other board members view-only access to reports?
- **Handoff process:** When a board member's term ends, how easy is it to transfer access to their replacement?

**Score 5 if:** New users can perform basic tasks within 1-2 hours, clean interface, strong help resources, easy role transitions.

**Score 1 if:** Steep learning curve, enterprise-style interface designed for property managers, no role separation, difficult account transitions.

## Category 4: Homeowner Portal (Weight: Medium)

A homeowner-facing portal reduces the number of emails, calls, and texts your board has to field. Good ones let homeowners pay assessments, view documents, submit requests, and check their account balance.

**What to evaluate:**

- **Online payment:** Can homeowners pay assessments via ACH or credit card? What's the payment processing fee? Who pays it (homeowner or HOA)?
- **Account view:** Can homeowners see their balance, payment history, and any outstanding amounts?
- **Document access:** Can homeowners view CC&Rs, meeting minutes, financial summaries, and community rules?
- **Communication:** Can you send announcements, newsletters, or targeted messages through the portal?
- **Maintenance requests:** Can homeowners submit and track requests?
- **Privacy controls:** Can you control what financial information homeowners see (their own vs. the association's)?

**Score 5 if:** Full self-service portal with payments, documents, communication, and request tracking. Low or no processing fees for ACH.

**Score 1 if:** No homeowner-facing features, or portal is so limited that homeowners still call the treasurer for basic information.

## Category 5: Pricing Model (Weight: High)

HOA software pricing falls into three models: per-unit/month, flat monthly rate, and free/open source. Per-unit pricing punishes larger communities. Flat rates are more predictable.

**What to evaluate:**

- **Pricing structure:** Per unit, flat rate, or tiered? What's the actual monthly cost for your community's size?
- **Hidden fees:** Setup fees, data migration fees, training fees, payment processing surcharges?
- **Payment processing costs:** ACH fees ($0.25-$2.00 per transaction) and credit card fees (2.5-3.5%) add up. Who absorbs them?
- **Contract length:** Monthly vs. annual? Cancellation penalties?
- **Free tier:** Is there a usable free version for small communities?
- **Scaling:** What happens to your price if your community adds units or merges with another association?

**Score 5 if:** Flat monthly rate under $50 for communities up to 200 units, no setup fees, monthly commitment, reasonable processing fees.

**Score 1 if:** Per-unit pricing that exceeds $100/month for a 100-unit community, annual contract with cancellation penalties, high processing fees.

Calculate the **total annual cost** including software subscription plus estimated payment processing fees. A tool that costs $30/month but charges $2 per ACH payment to a 100-unit community costs $30/mo + $200/mo in processing = $2,760/year, not $360/year.

## Category 6: Data Migration and Support (Weight: Medium)

Switching software is the biggest barrier to adoption. If you're coming from QuickBooks, spreadsheets, or another platform, you need to know how painful the transition will be.

**What to evaluate:**

- **Import capability:** Can you import your homeowner directory, assessment ledger, and financial history? What formats are supported (CSV, QBO, Excel)?
- **Migration assistance:** Does the vendor help with data migration, or is it self-service?
- **Support channels:** Email, phone, chat? Response time?
- **Support hours:** Are they available when volunteers work on HOA tasks (evenings and weekends)?
- **Community/knowledge base:** Active user forum, FAQ, or knowledge base?
- **Data export:** If you leave, can you export all your data in a standard format?

**Score 5 if:** Assisted migration, import from major accounting tools, responsive support with evening availability, full data export.

**Score 1 if:** No migration help, manual data entry required, support only during business hours, data locked in with no export.

Before signing up for any platform, run a real-world test. Enter 10 homeowner records, post a few payments, generate a financial report, and have a board member who wasn't involved in the evaluation try the same tasks. The tool that scores highest on paper isn't always the one that works best in practice for your specific board.

  - term: Fiduciary duty
    definition: >-
      A legal obligation to act in the best interests of another party. HOA
      board members owe fiduciary duties to the association and its members,
      requiring them to put the community's interests above their own and to
      exercise reasonable care in every decision they make.
  - term: Duty of care
    definition: >-
      The obligation to make informed, reasonable decisions - attending
      meetings, reviewing financial reports, consulting professionals when
      appropriate, and acting as a reasonably prudent person would in the same
      circumstances. Rubber-stamping budgets without review or missing board
      meetings can constitute a breach of the duty of care.
  - term: Duty of loyalty
    definition: >-
      The obligation to act in the community's interest rather than personal or
      third-party interests. Board members must disclose conflicts of interest,
      recuse themselves from votes where they have a personal stake, and never
      use their position to benefit themselves or associates at the community's
      expense.
  - term: Business judgment rule
    definition: >-
      A legal doctrine that protects board members from personal liability for
      decisions made in good faith, with adequate information, and without a
      conflict of interest. The rule requires a proper process - not a perfect
      outcome. Courts will not substitute their judgment for a board's
      reasonable decision, but the protection is lost when statutory
      requirements are ignored or a conflict of interest is undisclosed.
  - term: D&O insurance
    definition: >-
      Directors and Officers liability insurance covers legal costs and damages
      arising from claims that board members breached their fiduciary duties.
      D&O insurance is distinct from the HOA's general liability policy and is
      specifically designed to protect individual board members. Without it, a
      successful lawsuit against the board can reach personal assets.

## What Fiduciary Duty Actually Means for Volunteer Board Members

When you accepted a position on an HOA board, you accepted a legal status that most volunteers don't realize exists. Under the law, HOA board members are fiduciaries - a word borrowed from corporate governance that carries real weight. A fiduciary must act in the best interests of the people they serve (the homeowners), not in their own interests, and must do so with the care that a reasonably prudent person would exercise in the same position.

That standard does not care that you are a volunteer. It does not care that you received no training before taking office. It does not care that you inherited a mess from the previous board. If a homeowner sues your board for breach of fiduciary duty and the court finds that you failed to meet the standard, personal assets are on the table.

The good news is that the business judgment rule - the same doctrine that protects corporate directors - also protects HOA board members. If you made a decision in good faith, with reasonable information, and without a personal conflict of interest, courts will generally leave that decision alone. The rule is about process, not perfection. What strips away the protection is failing to follow statutory requirements or acting without any reasonable basis.

This checklist covers every area where boards routinely create liability exposure, and what compliance looks like at each step.

## Section 1 - Duty of Care

The duty of care requires board members to be informed, attentive, and deliberate. Inaction is not a defense.

**Meeting attendance and preparation**

- [ ] Attend all scheduled board meetings; if you must miss one, review the minutes before the next meeting
- [ ] Read the agenda and supporting materials before each meeting - do not vote on matters you have not reviewed
- [ ] Ensure a quorum is confirmed before taking any binding vote
- [ ] Approve meeting minutes from the prior meeting at each regular meeting

**Financial oversight**

- [ ] Review the monthly financial statement (balance sheet, income statement, reserve account balance) before each board meeting
- [ ] Compare actual spending to the approved budget line by line - flag variances above 10% for discussion
- [ ] Verify that the reserve fund balance matches the most recent bank statement for that account
- [ ] Confirm that reserve contributions are being deposited monthly, not annually
- [ ] Verify that the annual budget was distributed to all homeowners within the timeframe required by your governing documents

**Professional consultation**

- [ ] Commission a professional reserve study on the schedule required by your state (commonly every 3-5 years)
- [ ] Engage HOA legal counsel before amending governing documents, entering major contracts, or responding to litigation
- [ ] Have financial statements reviewed or audited by a CPA on the schedule required by your documents or state law
- [ ] Obtain at least two competitive bids for contracts above the threshold set in your governing documents

**Documentation**

- [ ] Record all board decisions in meeting minutes, including the specific motion, who moved and seconded, and the vote count
- [ ] Document the information the board reviewed before each major decision (studies, proposals, professional advice)
- [ ] Retain minutes, financial records, and contracts for the period required by your state (commonly 7 years)

## Section 2 - Duty of Loyalty

The duty of loyalty requires board members to put the community first. Conflicts of interest are not prohibited - undisclosed conflicts are.

**Conflict of interest disclosure**

- [ ] Disclose in writing any personal, financial, or family relationship with a vendor, contractor, or party to a transaction before the board votes
- [ ] Recuse yourself from the vote when you have a disclosed conflict - leave the room if your governing documents require it
- [ ] Document disclosures and recusals in the meeting minutes
- [ ] Review the board roster annually and ask each member to sign a conflict of interest disclosure form

**Vendor and contractor relationships**

- [ ] Verify that no board member or immediate family member has a financial interest in any vendor currently under contract with the association
- [ ] Use a competitive bidding process for contracts above your governing documents' threshold - do not sole-source without documented justification
- [ ] Review vendor contracts for automatic renewal clauses and calendar renewal dates before they pass

**Confidentiality**

- [ ] Treat information discussed in executive session (litigation, personnel matters, collection actions) as confidential
- [ ] Do not share homeowner financial information, collection status, or dispute details with other homeowners outside the board
- [ ] Do not use your board position to gain advance information about community decisions that would benefit you personally

## Section 3 - Reserve Fund Compliance

Reserve fund mismanagement is the most common source of board member liability. This section applies regardless of your state's specific statutory requirements.

**Fund separation (mandatory everywhere)**

- [ ] Maintain the reserve fund in a separate bank account from the operating fund - this is not optional
- [ ] Verify that no operating expenses have ever been paid from the reserve account without a board resolution authorizing the transfer
- [ ] Confirm the reserve account title identifies it as the reserve fund
- [ ] Obtain board approval documented in minutes before any transfer from reserve to operating funds

**Reserve study and funding**

- [ ] Locate your most recent reserve study - if you cannot find one, this is an immediate priority
- [ ] Confirm the study was conducted within your state's required update interval
- [ ] Confirm the study was conducted by a credentialed professional if your state requires one
- [ ] Compare the current reserve fund balance to the study's recommended fully funded or baseline funded amount
- [ ] Document in minutes the board's decision on reserve contribution levels, including the reasoning if you deviated from the study's recommendation
- [ ] Schedule the next professional reserve study update before the current one expires

**Annual disclosure**

- [ ] Distribute the required reserve fund disclosure to all homeowners with the annual budget (required in California, Hawaii, Washington, and several other states)
- [ ] If your state does not mandate disclosure, include reserve fund status in the annual report to homeowners anyway - it creates a record that homeowners were informed

## Section 4 - Insurance Requirements

D&O insurance is the primary protection for individual board members. Gaps in coverage create direct personal exposure.

**D&O (Directors and Officers) insurance**

- [ ] Confirm the association carries an active D&O policy - general liability does not cover board member fiduciary duty claims
- [ ] Verify that the policy covers all current board members, including recently elected members
- [ ] Review the policy's coverage limit - minimum recommended is $1 million per occurrence for communities under 100 units, $2 million+ for larger communities
- [ ] Confirm the policy does not have exclusions that would apply to the association's most likely risk areas (reserve fund disputes, assessment collection disputes, vendor contract disputes)
- [ ] Note the policy renewal date and calendar it 60 days in advance to allow time to shop coverage

**Other required policies**

- [ ] Confirm the association carries general liability insurance in the amounts required by your governing documents and state law
- [ ] Confirm property insurance covers common area structures at replacement cost (not actual cash value)
- [ ] Confirm workers' compensation coverage if the association has employees
- [ ] Confirm fidelity/crime coverage (employee dishonesty bond) to protect against embezzlement - required by Fannie Mae and FHA for condominiums

**Annual insurance review**

- [ ] Review all policies at renewal - do not auto-renew without confirming coverage limits remain adequate for current replacement costs and community size
- [ ] Document in minutes that the board reviewed coverage at renewal and approved the renewal terms

## Section 5 - Record-Keeping Obligations

Complete, organized records are the primary evidence that the board exercised its fiduciary duties. Missing records are evidence that it didn't.

**Governing documents**

- [ ] Maintain official copies of the declaration, bylaws, CC&Rs, all recorded amendments, and rules and regulations
- [ ] Ensure at least two board members and the management company (if any) have access to these documents
- [ ] Record all amendments with the county recorder within the timeframe required by your state

**Financial records**

- [ ] Retain general ledgers, bank statements, invoices, and payment records for at least 7 years (check your state for the specific requirement)
- [ ] Retain annual financial statements and any audit or review reports permanently
- [ ] Retain reserve studies permanently - they are evidence of the board's compliance with funding obligations

**Meeting records**

- [ ] Retain all board meeting and annual meeting minutes permanently
- [ ] Minutes must record the date, location, members present and absent, all motions (exact language), vote results, and any dissents
- [ ] Distribute draft minutes to board members for review within 30 days of each meeting; approve at the next meeting

**Homeowner records**

- [ ] Maintain a current homeowner roster with contact information
- [ ] Retain documentation of all covenant violation notices and resolutions
- [ ] Retain documentation of all architectural review applications and board decisions

## How to Use This Guide

This reference covers all 50 states and the District of Columbia. For each jurisdiction, we list: whether a reserve study is mandated, the relevant statute, study frequency requirements, funding rules, penalties for non-compliance, and any post-Surfside legislative changes.

States fall into four categories:

- **Mandate states** require reserve studies by statute
- **Disclosure states** require reserve fund information in financial reports but do not mandate a formal study
- **Permissive states** allow or encourage reserve studies but do not require them
- **Silent states** have no specific reserve fund provisions in their HOA statutes

Regardless of state law, all associations are subject to Fannie Mae's reserve allocation requirement (currently 10%, rising to 15% for Full Review loan applications on or after January 4, 2027 under Lender Letter LL-2026-03) if units are financed with conventional mortgages. Note also that the Limited Review process for condo projects is retired as of August 3, 2026 - all condo loan applications must use Full Review from that date.

## Mandate States (Reserve Study Required)

### California

- **Statute:** Civil Code 5550-5560
- **Study frequency:** Every 3 years (visual inspection every year, full study every 3 years)
- **Funding requirement:** Must include a 30-year funding plan. Boards must disclose percent-funded status annually.
- **Penalties:** $100-500 per day for non-compliance
- **Post-Surfside changes:** SB 900 (2024), AB 2114 (2024), SB 410 (2025) strengthened requirements. AB 2050 pending.

### Colorado

- **Statute:** Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act 38-33.3-209.5
- **Study frequency:** Recommended every 5 years; required for communities with annual assessments above a threshold
- **Funding requirement:** Must disclose reserve balance and percent-funded status
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** No major post-Surfside legislation enacted

### Delaware

- **Statute:** Delaware Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act 81-318
- **Study frequency:** At least every 5 years
- **Funding requirement:** Reserve study must include a funding plan
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** No major post-Surfside legislation enacted

### Florida

- **Statute:** Chapter 718 (condos), Chapter 720 (HOAs)
- **Study frequency:** SIRS every 10 years for condos/co-ops 3+ stories; milestone inspections at 30 years (25 near coast)
- **Funding requirement:** Reserve waiver banned for structural components covered by SIRS. Deferred maintenance threshold: $25,000. §718.111(14) requires separate accounting of condo reserves and operating funds (joint investment permitted only with separate accounting); §720.303 imposes the same separate-accounting duty on HOAs.
- **Penalties:** DBPR Division of Condominiums can fine condo and co-op boards up to $5,000 per violation under §718.501(1)(d)(6) and Florida Admin Code Chapter 61B-21. HOAs are not under DBPR jurisdiction; HOA enforcement is via member civil action.
- **Post-Surfside changes:** SB 4-D (2022), HB 913 (2025). Most comprehensive post-Surfside reforms in the country.

### Hawaii

- **Statute:** HRS 514B-148
- **Study frequency:** As required by statute; 30-year horizon under Act 296
- **Funding requirement:** Minimum 50% funded requirement under Act 296 (2025)
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** Act 62 (2022), Act 296 (2025). Significant strengthening of requirements.

### Maryland

- **Statute:** Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2 (annual budget reserves) and §11B-112.3 (reserve study) for HOAs; §11-109.4 for condominium reserve studies
- **Study frequency:** Reserve study required under HB 107 (2022); funding plan and budget alignment required under HB 292 (2025)
- **Funding requirement:** Mandatory funding under HB 292 (2025, Chapter 519, effective October 1, 2025). Associations must adopt a funding plan in consultation with the reserve study author and deposit reserve contributions per the most recent reserve study's recommended amount by the end of each fiscal year.
- **Penalties:** Enforcement runs through the Maryland Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division under the Maryland Condominium Act and Homeowners Association Act. No specific reserve-fund per-violation statutory penalty is set in §11B-112.2 or §11B-112.3.
- **Post-Surfside changes:** HB 107 (2022, Chapter 664), reserve study mandate; HB 292 (2025, Chapter 519), mandatory funding plan and budget alignment.

### Nevada

- **Statute:** NRS 116.31152
- **Study frequency:** At least every 5 years
- **Funding requirement:** Must include a 30-year plan with annual contribution schedule
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** SB 56 pending to strengthen existing requirements

### Oregon

- **Statute:** ORS 100.175
- **Study frequency:** At least every 3-5 years (varies by community type)
- **Funding requirement:** Must include a funding plan
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** No major post-Surfside legislation enacted

### Tennessee

- **Statute:** Tennessee Community Association Act
- **Study frequency:** As required by governing documents or statute
- **Funding requirement:** Study required but funding levels NOT mandated
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** SB 863 (2023). Weakest post-Surfside reform - study required, funding not.

### Utah

- **Statute:** Utah Community Association Act 57-8a
- **Study frequency:** At least every 6 years
- **Funding requirement:** Must disclose reserve balance
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** No major post-Surfside legislation enacted

### Virginia

- **Statute:** Virginia Property Owners' Association Act 55.1-1826
- **Study frequency:** At least every 5 years
- **Funding requirement:** Must include component analysis and funding plan
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** HB 1209 (2024) formalized definitions and strengthened board authority

### Washington

- **Statute:** RCW 64.34.382 (condos), 64.90 (common interest)
- **Study frequency:** At least every 3 years
- **Funding requirement:** Must include a 30-year funding plan
- **Penalties:** Fiduciary duty exposure
- **Post-Surfside changes:** No major post-Surfside legislation enacted

## Disclosure States (No Study Required, But Reserve Disclosures Mandated)

### Connecticut

- **Statute:** Common Interest Ownership Act 47-261
- **Requirement:** Must disclose reserve fund balance in annual financial reports
- **Post-Surfside changes:** SB 212 pending to mandate reserve studies

### Illinois

- **Statute:** Condo Property Act 765 ILCS 605
- **Requirement:** Must disclose reserve fund status; no formal study required
- **Post-Surfside changes:** HB 2563 / SB 1703 pending to require studies and minimum funding

### New Jersey

- **Statute:** Condominium Act N.J.S.A. 46:8B
- **Requirement:** Must disclose reserve fund balance
- **Post-Surfside changes:** S2760 (2024), S3992 (2025) established baseline funding rules. Balance can never drop below $0.

### New York

- **Statute:** Real Property Law, General Business Law
- **Requirement:** Must disclose reserve fund information in offering plans and annual reports
- **Post-Surfside changes:** S7600 / A8945 pending to require reserve studies for larger associations

### Pennsylvania

- **Statute:** Uniform Planned Community Act 68 Pa.C.S. 5304
- **Requirement:** Must include reserve information in public offering statement
- **Post-Surfside changes:** No major post-Surfside legislation enacted

## Permissive States (Reserves Encouraged, Not Mandated)

The following states have HOA statutes that reference reserve funds in some capacity (authorizing boards to maintain them, suggesting best practices, or allowing governing documents to require them) but do not mandate reserve studies or specific funding levels: Arizona, Georgia, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Texas.

In these states, fiduciary duty is the primary enforcement mechanism. Board members who knowingly allow reserves to become critically underfunded face potential personal liability, even without a state mandate.

## Silent States (No Specific Reserve Provisions)

The following states have limited or no specific reserve fund provisions in their HOA statutes: Alabama, Alaska, Arkansas, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Mississippi, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Mexico, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Vermont, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming, and the District of Columbia.

In silent states, boards are still subject to general fiduciary duty obligations and Fannie Mae's reserve allocation requirement.

## The Fannie Mae Floor

Regardless of state law, Fannie Mae requires associations to allocate at least 10% of their annual budget to reserves. Lender Letter LL-2026-03 sets two deadlines: the Limited Review process for condo projects is retired August 3, 2026, and the minimum allocation rises to 15% for Full Review loan applications dated on or after January 4, 2027. Associations below the 15% threshold are classified as non-warrantable, meaning conventional mortgages cannot be used to purchase units.

For boards in silent or permissive states, Fannie Mae's requirement is effectively the binding constraint. Even with no state mandate, failing to meet the 15% threshold for applications on or after January 4, 2027 freezes unit sales for buyers who need conventional financing.

## Tracking Compliance Across Changing Laws

State legislation is evolving rapidly. Five or more states have pending bills as of early 2026. Boards cannot rely on a one-time review of their state's requirements.

Gavelhouse can support a cleaner reserve audit trail by keeping operating and reserve activity separated. Boards must still monitor state requirements, Fannie Mae thresholds, and legislative changes outside the product, then prepare required disclosures with CPA or counsel review where needed.

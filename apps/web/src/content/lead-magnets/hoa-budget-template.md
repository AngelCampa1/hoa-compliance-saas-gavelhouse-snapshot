## What This Template Covers

A well-structured HOA annual budget does two things a generic spreadsheet template cannot: it tracks operating and reserve funds separately from the first entry, and it produces the reserve allocation percentage figure that lenders ask for on condo questionnaires.

This template is structured around fund accounting principles. Every dollar of assessment income is allocated at collection - split between the operating fund and the reserve fund based on the board-approved allocation percentage. Operating expenses are posted against the operating fund. Capital expenditures are posted against the reserve fund. The two funds never mix.

## How to Use This Template

**Step 1: Enter your unit count and current monthly assessment.** The template calculates total annual assessment income automatically.

**Step 2: Set your operating/reserve split percentage.** Start with your current reserve study's recommended annual contribution. Divide by total annual income to get the required reserve allocation percentage. If you don't have a reserve study, start at 15% to meet the upcoming Fannie Mae threshold, then commission a reserve study to refine the number.

**Step 3: Enter operating expense estimates line by line.** Use last year's actual spending as your baseline. Adjust for known cost increases (contractor bids, insurance renewals, management fee contracts).

**Step 4: Enter reserve expenditures from your reserve study schedule.** List every component with its expected replacement year and estimated cost. Pull annual contribution figures directly from the reserve study.

**Step 5: Verify the reserve allocation percentage.** Section 5 shows the calculated percentage. Confirm it meets the Fannie Mae threshold. If not, adjust the reserve contribution line until it does - and adjust the operating portion accordingly, which may require raising total assessments.

**Step 6: Present to the board for approval.** The budget must be approved by board resolution at a properly noticed board meeting before the start of the fiscal year. Most state HOA statutes require budget adoption by a specific date and distribution to homeowners within a specified period after adoption.

## The Reserve Allocation Percentage: Why It Matters Beyond the Budget

Boards completing this template often discover for the first time that their reserve contribution is well below 10% of total assessments. This is not just a budget problem - it is a financing problem for every unit in the community.

When a buyer's lender sends a condo questionnaire and asks for the reserve allocation percentage, the answer comes from your budget and financial statements. If the answer is 6%, the buyer's conventional Fannie Mae loan will be denied. Not because of the buyer's finances. Because of the board's budget decisions.

Fixing this requires raising the reserve contribution in the budget - which typically means raising total assessments. This is the correct and necessary action. The Fannie Mae deadline of January 4, 2027 for the 15% Full Review threshold gives boards a defined window to make this adjustment.

## Why Board Transitions Fail

The typical HOA board transition goes like this: the outgoing member says they are stepping down, the board scrambles to find a replacement, and the new member inherits a box of binders, a vague verbal briefing, and login credentials scribbled on a Post-it note. Financial records may be incomplete. Vendor contracts are in someone's email. The reserve study schedule exists only in the outgoing member's calendar.

With 80% of current board members aged 60 or older and 33-38% citing volunteer recruitment as a primary stressor, transitions are happening more frequently and with less preparation. This checklist turns an ad hoc scramble into a structured process that protects the community through leadership changes.

## Phase 1: Financial Access Transfer (Complete Within 7 Days)

### Bank Account Signatories

- [ ] Obtain a board resolution authorizing signatory changes (required by most banks)
- [ ] Visit the bank with outgoing and incoming members to update authorized signatories
- [ ] Update online banking access: add new member, remove outgoing member
- [ ] Verify that at least two current board members have signatory authority
- [ ] Confirm the outgoing member's debit card (if any) is deactivated
- [ ] Document all account numbers, bank contact information, and access procedures

### Accounting and Financial Software

- [ ] Transfer login credentials for HOA accounting software
- [ ] Walk through the current chart of accounts, fund structure, and open items
- [ ] Verify the incoming member can access all financial reports
- [ ] Review current month's reconciliation status
- [ ] Identify any pending transactions, scheduled payments, or auto-debits
- [ ] Export a complete financial backup before removing outgoing member's access
- [ ] Remove outgoing member's access after transfer is confirmed

### Assessment and Dues Collection

- [ ] Transfer access to the payment processing platform (if separate from accounting software)
- [ ] Review the current delinquency list and any accounts in collections
- [ ] Hand off any pending assessment disputes or payment plan agreements
- [ ] Document the assessment schedule, due dates, and late fee policies

## Phase 2: Document and Record Transfer (Complete Within 14 Days)

### Governing Documents

- [ ] Confirm the incoming member has copies of: articles of incorporation, bylaws, CC&Rs, all amendments, rules and regulations, architectural guidelines
- [ ] Identify where the official copies are stored (physical and digital)
- [ ] Transfer access to the document management system or shared drive

### Financial Records

- [ ] Transfer all financial statements for the current and prior fiscal year
- [ ] Hand off the current year's budget and reserve fund contribution schedule
- [ ] Provide the most recent reserve study and any updates
- [ ] Transfer tax returns for the past 3 years
- [ ] Hand off audit reports or financial reviews (if applicable)

### Meeting Records

- [ ] Transfer all board meeting minutes from at least the past 2 years
- [ ] Hand off annual meeting minutes and any special meeting records
- [ ] Provide the current meeting schedule and notification procedures

### Homeowner Records

- [ ] Transfer the homeowner directory and contact information
- [ ] Hand off any pending violation cases, architectural review applications, or homeowner complaints
- [ ] Identify any homeowners with special circumstances (payment plans, accommodations, pending legal matters)

## Phase 3: Compliance and Schedule Transfer (Complete Within 21 Days)

### Reserve Study Status

- [ ] Review the current reserve fund balance and percent-funded status
- [ ] Confirm the date of the most recent reserve study
- [ ] Identify when the next reserve study is due (most states: every 3-5 years)
- [ ] Review whether the community meets Fannie Mae's reserve allocation threshold (currently 10%, rising to 15% for Full Review applications on or after January 4, 2027 under LL-2026-03; also note Limited Review is retired August 3, 2026)
- [ ] Document any reserve study recommendations that have not yet been acted on

### State Compliance Deadlines

- [ ] Identify all state-specific HOA filing requirements and deadlines
- [ ] Check whether your state has enacted post-Surfside reserve legislation
- [ ] Review annual report filing dates with the secretary of state (if required)
- [ ] Confirm the status of any required reserve fund disclosures

### Insurance Policies

- [ ] Transfer the insurance binder and policy documents
- [ ] Review coverage levels: general liability, D&O (directors and officers), property, umbrella
- [ ] Confirm the D&O policy covers the incoming board member
- [ ] Note the renewal dates for each policy
- [ ] Update the insurance agent's contact information with the new board roster

### Vendor Contracts

- [ ] List all active vendor contracts: landscaping, maintenance, cleaning, pest control, legal, accounting, insurance, management services
- [ ] For each vendor: contract term, renewal date, contact person, payment schedule
- [ ] Transfer any vendor login portals or service request systems
- [ ] Notify vendors of the board member change and updated contact information

## Phase 4: Pending Matters Briefing (Complete Within 30 Days)

### Legal Matters

- [ ] Brief the incoming member on any pending litigation or legal disputes
- [ ] Transfer attorney contact information and retainer agreement details
- [ ] Review any pending collection actions against delinquent homeowners
- [ ] Identify any open insurance claims

### Capital Projects

- [ ] Review the status of any in-progress or planned capital projects
- [ ] Transfer contractor bids, proposals, and project timelines
- [ ] Identify projects approved by the board but not yet started
- [ ] Review the reserve study's schedule for upcoming major replacements

### Communication Plan

- [ ] Notify homeowners of the board member change (newsletter, email, portal announcement)
- [ ] Update the community website or portal with new board member information
- [ ] Update the management company (if applicable) with new contact information

## Phase 5: System Verification (Complete Within 45 Days)

### Software and System Access

- [ ] Verify the incoming member can access all software systems independently
- [ ] Confirm the outgoing member's access has been revoked for all systems
- [ ] Test that automated processes (assessment generation, email notifications, report scheduling) are still functioning
- [ ] Ensure at least two board members have admin-level access to every system

### Knowledge Gaps

- [ ] Schedule a 30-day check-in between the outgoing and incoming member to address questions that arise
- [ ] Document any issues or gaps discovered during the first month
- [ ] Update this checklist with any community-specific items that were missed

## Making Transitions Routine

This checklist should not live in a drawer until someone resigns. Review and update it at least once per year, even when no transition is imminent. Staggered board terms mean partial turnover happens by design, and having the checklist ready before you need it is the difference between a smooth transition and a governance gap.

When financial records, compliance status, vendor contracts, and meeting history live in dedicated HOA software rather than personal files, most of this checklist becomes trivial. The incoming member inherits a working system with complete history by getting login credentials. The knowledge transfer happens through system access, not a multi-hour briefing.

We built Gavelhouse so that the association's financial records, reserve balances, and supported board records persist through any board member transition. The system belongs to the community, not to any individual volunteer. When the treasurer changes, the financial history does not reset. The new treasurer's onboarding is logging in and reviewing the dashboard.

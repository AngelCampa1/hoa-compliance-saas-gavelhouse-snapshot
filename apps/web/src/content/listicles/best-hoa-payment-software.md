## The difference between paying dues and tracking compliance

Most HOA payment tools solve the same narrow problem: accepting online payments from homeowners and recording them in a general ledger. ACH processing, credit card acceptance, automatic reminders, delinquency reports, these are table stakes across the category.

The harder question is what happens after dues are collected. For self-managed boards with reserve fund obligations, dues collection that does not integrate with reserve fund accounting pushes compliance work onto manual spreadsheets and year-end adjustments.

State reserve fund disclosure requirements are specific. They do not ask for your checking account balance. They ask for your reserve fund balance as a percentage of fully funded reserve adequacy, a number that requires connecting collected reserve assessments to a reserve study's projected targets. Most HOA payment software cannot produce that number without manual calculation.

## Why reserve fund integration matters at payment time

When a homeowner pays their monthly assessment, the operating and reserve allocation decision happens at that moment. If your HOA collects $300/month per unit and $75 is designated as a reserve contribution, that $75 should go to the reserve fund, tracked separately from operating funds, at the point of receipt.

Most HOA payment tools record the full payment to a single account and leave fund allocation to manual journal entries later. That manual step is where commingling risk accumulates. Boards that skip it, or do not know they need to do it, end up with undifferentiated ledger balances that fail reserve disclosure requirements.

Gavelhouse allocates dues to the correct fund at the point of collection. PayHOA, ClickPay, HOALife, TownSq, and Condo Control all require manual processes for that allocation.

## What to look for in HOA payment software

The pricing model matters. PayHOA and Gavelhouse use flat monthly tiers regardless of unit count. TownSq and ClickPay price per transaction or per unit. For growing communities, flat pricing becomes cheaper above 50-75 units.

If reserve fund compliance is a legal or fiduciary requirement, confirm the tool separates operating and reserve funds at the ledger level, not just in account labels. Ask whether it produces reserve adequacy reports tied to a reserve study, most do not.

Payment method fees are easy to overlook. ACH runs $0.25-$1.00 per transaction; credit card processing runs 2-3%. For a $300/month assessment, that difference is real and falls on the HOA or homeowners depending on how fee rules are configured.

Finally, check whether signup is self-serve. PayHOA, Gavelhouse, and TownSq allow self-serve signup with published pricing. ClickPay and Condo Control require a sales conversation. If the board needs to move fast, that friction matters.

## Why Illinois HOA Software Selection Has a Deconversion Lens

Illinois is unusual among states because of 765 ILCS 605/15 — the condominium deconversion statute. A 75% supermajority of unit owners (or higher if the declaration sets a higher threshold) can approve a bulk sale of the entire association to a single buyer, typically a developer who plans to convert the building to rental or redevelop it.

Deconversion offers arrive without warning. When one does, the association needs to produce a complete picture of ownership, financials, reserves, and pending obligations within the buyer's diligence timeline. Boards using software that does not produce clean exports end up reconstructing the picture by hand under deal pressure.

This is not a hypothetical. Chicago has been one of the most active deconversion markets in the country since the statute took shape, with both successful and contested deconversions playing out in Lincoln Park, Lakeview, the South Loop, and the suburbs.

For non-condominium HOAs in Illinois, the Common Interest Community Association Act (765 ILCS 160) creates the parallel governance framework with reserve fund, fiduciary duty, and disclosure obligations.

## What Illinois Boards Should Look For

**Clean ownership-and-financial export.** For condo associations, deconversion readiness means the software can produce a unit-by-unit ownership list, a transaction history, current fund balances, and outstanding receivables in a format counsel and a buyer's diligence team can work with directly.

**Fund accounting at the ledger level.** Both 765 ILCS 605 and 765 ILCS 160 require operating and reserve fund separation. Software that allows commingling creates audit and dispute risk.

**Reserve fund separation and records.** Industry-standard reserve study cycles run three to five years. The board or a reserve specialist performs the adequacy review; software that keeps operating and reserve funds separated at the ledger level — with a full audit log — supplies the accurate records that review depends on.

**Document retention.** Meeting minutes, financial disclosures, and notices need to be retained and producible. Owners — and in deconversion situations, buyers — have statutory rights to specific documents.

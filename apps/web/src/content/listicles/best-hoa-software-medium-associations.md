## Why 51–200 Units Is the Hardest Size Band

The 51-200 unit range is where HOA software economics break for most boards. Below 50 units, free or near-free tools cover the workload. Above 200 units, full-service property management becomes economically viable. In between, boards face a software market that was mostly built for either end of the spectrum.

The two structural problems mid-size boards run into:

**The price-cliff.** Per-unit pricing — typical for incumbent platforms — runs $1.50-$3 per unit per month. At 50 units that is manageable. At 150 units it is $225-$450/month, before any add-on modules. Flat-tier pricing at $27/month (Y80OFF annual) for the entire 51-200 band changes the math by a factor of four to nine.

**The compliance threshold.** At this size, reserve balances commonly cross $100,000 and annual operating budgets cross $200,000. That is the size at which commingling operating and reserve funds stops being a theoretical risk and starts showing up on state audits as a documented finding. Software that does not enforce fund separation creates exactly the documentation gap that turns a routine audit into a personal-liability question for board members.

## What Mid-Size Boards Should Look For

**Flat pricing within the 51-200 band.** Per-unit pricing is the wrong shape for this size. Look for software that charges the same at 75 units as at 175.

**Fund accounting at the ledger level.** Operating and reserve funds need separate ledgers. QuickBooks and most general-purpose accounting tools allow commingling. Purpose-built HOA fund accounting prevents it.

**Audit-logged fund records.** Mid-size associations almost always have reserve balances large enough to attract auditor attention. Software that logs every fund movement with user, timestamp, and before/after values gives the board the documentation that supports the annual adequacy review most state statutes require.

**Volunteer-friendly workflows.** Mid-size boards are still volunteer boards. Software that requires a daily login or accountant-level skills to operate will not stick.

## The Five Best Options

### 1. Gavelhouse — Best for Reserve Compliance

We built Gavelhouse because the mid-size band was the worst-served part of the market. Per-unit pricing punished growth; QuickBooks-based stacks created commingling risk; full-suite condo platforms charged for features volunteer boards never used.

The Growth tier at $27/month billed annually with Y80OFF (80% off your first year) covers the entire 51-200 unit band. Fund accounting enforces operating and reserve separation at the database level — you cannot post a reserve expense to the operating account by accident. A full audit log captures every transaction with user, timestamp, and before/after values. Clean financial exports give the treasurer accurate, separated records to use when the board does its annual adequacy review.

The trade-offs: Gavelhouse is newer (launched 2026) and does not yet have a mobile app. If your board needs amenity booking or visitor management for a condo high-rise, it is not the right fit.

### 2. PayHOA — Best for Dues and Violations

PayHOA has the most mature dues-collection and homeowner-portal experience in the segment. Online assessment payment via ACH or card, payment history, document storage, and violation management with photo evidence all work cleanly.

At 100 units the typical PayHOA price lands around $99/month, and at 200 units closer to $199/month. The bands stay flat within ranges, which is more predictable than pure per-unit pricing but still meaningfully more expensive than flat-tier alternatives.

The gap: PayHOA does not separate operating and reserve funds at the ledger level, and it does not include reserve compliance tracking. For mid-size condo associations under reserve-mandate statutes, that is the same gap QuickBooks creates.

### 3. Condo Control — Best for Full-Suite Condo Operations

Condo Control covers the full operational surface area of a mid-size condo association: amenity booking, visitor management, parcel tracking, document storage, basic financials, and homeowner communication.

At 51-200 units, pricing typically lands in the $80-$200/month range depending on modules selected. The financial module produces reports suitable for annual meeting disclosures, but it does not include dedicated reserve compliance tracking against reserve study targets.

For a 120-unit condo with amenities (gym, pool, party room) and a part-time concierge, Condo Control covers more of the workload than any other tool in this comparison. For a 120-unit single-family-home HOA with no amenities, most of the platform is unused.

## The Other Two

### 4. HOALife

HOALife is built around violation tracking. The inspection workflows, photo management, and notice templates handle the full enforcement cycle. Accounting runs through QuickBooks integration, which means HOALife inherits QuickBooks's commingling problem.

For a mid-size HOA where CC&R enforcement is the biggest weekly workload and accounting is already in QuickBooks, HOALife is a strong fit at $45-$80/month. It is not a complete solution for reserve-compliance-driven boards.

### 5. MoneyMinder

MoneyMinder is genuine fund accounting software at flat annual pricing (~$159/year). It does not include a homeowner portal, dues collection, or violation tracking. It is the best "treasurer-only" tool in the segment.

For a board where the treasurer wants real fund accounting and the rest of the board handles dues collection through a bank portal and violations on paper, MoneyMinder fits. For a board that wants one platform for all of it, it does not.

## Methodology

This list is based on public pricing pages, product documentation, and the structural fit between platform capabilities and the specific needs of 51-200 unit associations. Pricing was verified as of April 2026; per-unit and per-band pricing on competitor platforms can change. We did not include platforms that are exclusively for 500+ unit portfolio operators (Vantaca, AppFolio, CINC) because their pricing models and feature sets are oriented to a different buyer.

The right choice for any specific board depends on which compliance pressures are most acute and which operational workflows dominate the volunteer board's time. Reserve-compliance-driven condo boards should start with Gavelhouse. Dues-and-violation-driven planned communities should start with PayHOA. Operationally complex condo associations should evaluate Condo Control.

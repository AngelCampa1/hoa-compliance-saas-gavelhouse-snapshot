## Why Washington HOA Software Has to Handle Reserve Studies

Washington has three overlapping statutes governing common interest communities. RCW 64.34 covers condominiums created before July 1, 2018. RCW 64.38 covers HOAs created in roughly the same era. RCW 64.90 — the Washington Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act — governs all common interest communities created on or after July 1, 2018, and applies optionally to older communities.

All three converge on one requirement: a reserve study, updated at least every three years, with annual board review of funding adequacy.

For software selection, that requirement is the central filter. Tools that produce reserve compliance reports against study targets — current reserve balance vs. funded vs. fully-funded thresholds, by component — make the annual review a routine task. Tools that do not produce those reports leave the volunteer board to reconstruct them by hand from bank statements and the reserve study spreadsheet.

The communities most affected: high-rise condos in Seattle and Bellevue with significant deferred-maintenance components (elevators, roofs, mechanical systems), waterfront condos in Tacoma and Bellingham with corrosion exposure, and master-planned HOAs in Spokane and Vancouver with shared roads and amenities.

## What to Look For in Washington HOA Software

**Reserve study integration.** The software needs to ingest reserve study targets and track current balances against them. Bonus: component-level tracking that mirrors how Washington reserve studies are typically structured.

**Fund accounting at the ledger level.** Operating and reserve funds need separate ledgers. RCW 64.38.025 and RCW 64.90.305 both require separate accounting for reserve funds.

**Annual disclosure reporting.** Washington requires associations to provide annual financial disclosures to members. Software that generates clean balance sheets and reserve adequacy reports in a format non-accountant board members can present saves the treasurer hours of manual work each year.

**Board meeting and minutes documentation.** RCW 64.38 and 64.90 both require open meetings with notice and minutes. Document storage that retains meeting minutes and notices supports the audit trail.

## Making the Right Choice for Your Washington Community

Start with which statute governs the community: RCW 64.34 (older condo), RCW 64.38 (older HOA), or RCW 64.90 (post-2018). The reserve study and fund separation requirements are similar across all three, so the software shortlist converges.

For boards where reserve compliance is the central concern — particularly Bellevue high-rises and Seattle mid-rise condos with substantial deferred-maintenance components — Gavelhouse is the closest fit to the RCW reserve adequacy workflow. For dues-and-violation-driven planned communities, PayHOA covers the day-to-day. For amenity-rich high-rises, Condo Control covers the operational breadth.

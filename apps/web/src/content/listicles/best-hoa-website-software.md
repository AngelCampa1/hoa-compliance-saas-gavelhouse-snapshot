Most HOA boards that search for "HOA website" are actually looking for something more specific than a website. They need a homeowner portal where residents can pay dues, access documents, and view their account. The public website is secondary to the authenticated portal experience that makes day-to-day community operations run.

Understanding this distinction is the first step in choosing the right tool. A general website builder gives you a professional webpage. HOA management software gives you the portal functionality that community operations actually require - and most platforms include a public-facing website component alongside it.

## What an HOA website needs vs. what a generic website builder provides

A generic website builder - Wix, Squarespace, Weebly - can produce a professional-looking community website with contact forms, event calendars, and announcement posts. For a community that needs only a public information page with no authenticated access, that is sufficient.

Where general builders fall short is everything that requires authentication and data:

**Homeowner account access.** Homeowners need to see their balance, payment history, and upcoming charges. This requires authenticated access tied to their homeowner record. No general website builder provides this without significant custom development.

**Online payment processing.** Collecting dues online requires payment processing integrated with your accounting records. A Wix "storefront" can accept payments, but those payments are disconnected from your HOA ledger, creating manual reconciliation work.

**Document access controls.** Governing documents may be publicly accessible, but meeting minutes, financial statements, and reserve studies are typically restricted to homeowners. Controlling access by homeowner record requires an authentication layer that general builders do not provide.

**Violation notices and communication history.** Homeowners receiving violation notices need authenticated access to view their notice history and respond. This is a portal workflow, not a website feature.

**Role-based access.** Board members should see more than homeowners. A platform that controls what different user types can access requires role management that generic builders do not handle.

## The right framing: portal first, website second

For most HOA boards, the right approach is to choose an HOA management platform that includes a homeowner portal - and treat any public website features as a bonus. The portal is where day-to-day community management happens. The public website is informational.

If you need a highly customized public community website - custom design, detailed neighborhood information, marketing for prospective residents - a general website builder can supplement your HOA management platform's portal. Running them as separate tools is common and manageable.

## The 5 best HOA website and portal options

### 1. Gavelhouse - Best integrated portal for compliance-conscious communities

Gavelhouse's homeowner portal is the authenticated interface through which residents access account information and make payments. It is designed around the financial data that is core to the platform; community documents, reserve fund reports, financial statements, and board communications should remain in the board's existing website or records process today.

The portal is mobile-accessible without a separate app download. Board members should keep document hosting, meeting minutes, and financial statement publication in their existing website or records process today.

What Gavelhouse's portal is not is a public community marketing website. It does not include a public-facing community homepage with a custom domain, neighborhood photos, and community news for prospective residents. For that, communities should use a general website builder or their existing public site.

**Best for:** Communities that prioritize integrated portal functionality - dues payment, document access, account management - tied to financial and compliance data.

### 2. PayHOA - Best for combined public website and portal in one platform

PayHOA comes closest to providing both a public-facing community website and a full homeowner portal in a single platform. The community website component lets you share announcements, event calendars, and community information publicly. The homeowner portal gives residents authenticated access to their accounts, documents, and communications.

This combination is the strongest argument for PayHOA in a website/portal comparison. Communities that want one platform to serve both public visitors and authenticated homeowners without managing two separate systems get the most value here.

The financial portal visibility reflects PayHOA's general ledger approach - there is no fund-separated reserve compliance view, and the financial data accessible through the portal does not include the compliance tracking that purpose-built tools provide.

**Best for:** Communities that want both a public community site and a homeowner portal in a single subscription without managing separate tools.

### 3. HOALife - Best for violation-focused portal workflow

HOALife's portal centers on violation notices and communications. Homeowners can log in to see their violation history, respond to notices, and access community documents. The portal workflow for violations is more streamlined than most alternatives - notices are delivered through the portal with documented acknowledgment.

The financial portal functionality is limited by HOALife's QuickBooks dependency. Homeowners cannot see real-time account balances tied to a fund-separated ledger; financial information visible through the portal reflects QuickBooks data with all its fund accounting limitations.

**Best for:** Communities where violation management and documented notice delivery are the primary portal use cases.

### 4. Wix - Best for standalone public community websites

Wix is a legitimate choice for communities that genuinely only need a public information website - no portal, no payment processing, no document access controls. If your community is managed by a management company that handles all homeowner portal needs, and you want a professionally designed public presence to share community information, Wix provides that at low cost with good design tools.

The clear line: Wix cannot replace a homeowner portal. Do not try to use Wix to collect dues, manage documents with access controls, or provide homeowners with account access. You need a purpose-built HOA platform for those requirements.

**Best for:** Communities that only need a public information page and handle all portal and financial functions through a separate platform.

### 5. Weebly - Best for very basic public community websites

Weebly offers a simpler version of what Wix provides - drag-and-drop website creation at low cost. For communities with the simplest possible requirements (a page with community contact information, basic rules summary, and possibly a community calendar), Weebly gets the job done with minimal configuration.

Like Wix, Weebly cannot replace portal functionality. The e-commerce workaround for dues collection creates more administrative friction than it solves. For any community with more than a handful of homeowners, a purpose-built HOA platform covers the portal needs that Weebly cannot.

**Best for:** The simplest possible public information sites for very small communities with no portal requirements.

## Choosing the right approach for your community

The practical recommendation depends on what your community actually needs:

**If you need homeowner payment access:** Start with Gavelhouse or PayHOA. If you need document access or a public website in the same portal, PayHOA's public website component is more developed today.

**If you need both a professional public website and a full portal:** PayHOA handles both in one platform. Alternatively, pair Gavelhouse or HOALife with a Wix public site - the two tools serve different audiences and can coexist cleanly.

**If you only need a public information page:** Wix or Weebly at low monthly cost. But be honest about whether your homeowners will eventually need portal access - starting with a general builder that you will need to supplement later creates more migration work.

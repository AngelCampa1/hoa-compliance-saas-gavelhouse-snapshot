## Why QuickBooks Is the Wrong Default for HOA Accounting

QuickBooks is the most widely deployed HOA accounting tool in the United States. It is also the most common source of commingling findings in CPA reviews and state audits of common interest realty associations.

The mechanism is structural. QuickBooks is general-purpose small business accounting software. It has one general ledger, one chart of accounts, one bank reconciliation workflow. To simulate fund separation, treasurers use class tracking (separate "Operating" and "Reserve" classes), separate sub-accounts under cash and equity, or in some cases separate QuickBooks company files entirely.

All three approaches depend on the treasurer maintaining the discipline manually. Posting a reserve expense to the operating class by accident produces no error. Issuing a check from the operating bank account for a reserve expense — when the funds should have flowed through an inter-fund transfer first — produces no error. The simulated separation is exactly that: simulated.

State statutes increasingly require reserve fund separation as a substantive obligation, not a presentation convention. The AICPA Audit Guide for Common Interest Realty Associations treats fund accounting as the appropriate model for CIRA financials. Self-managed boards that stay on QuickBooks past a certain size are accumulating audit and liability exposure that purpose-built fund accounting structurally prevents.

## What Self-Managed Boards Should Look For

**Fund accounting at the database level.** Operating and reserve funds as separate accounting entities, with inter-fund transfers as explicit, audited transactions.

**Audit-logged fund records.** Every transaction recorded with user, timestamp, and before/after values. The annual adequacy review most state statutes require starts from clean, separated records — not from reconstructing months of bank statements.

**Owner-friendly outputs.** Annual disclosures, balance sheet by fund, budget vs. actual reports, owner statements — generated in formats non-accountant board members can read and present.

**Flat pricing.** Per-unit pricing is the wrong shape for self-managed boards. Flat tiers by community size make budgeting predictable.

**Reasonable scope.** A self-managed board does not need property-management-grade software. Tools built for management companies (Vantaca, AppFolio, CINC) over-feature the workflow and over-price for the size.

## The Five Best Options for Self-Managed Boards

### 1. Gavelhouse — Best All-in-One for Self-Managed Boards

We built Gavelhouse because the gap between "QuickBooks plus assorted add-ons" and "purpose-built HOA fund-accounting platform" was wider than any single tool covered. Fund accounting enforces operating and reserve fund separation at the database level — you cannot post a reserve expense to the operating account by accident. A full audit log captures every transaction with user, timestamp, and before/after values. Clean financial exports give the board accurate, separated records for annual reporting.

At $10–$50/month billed annually with Y80OFF (80% off your first year), the cost stays predictable from a 30-home association to a 200-home community. The trade-off: Gavelhouse launched in 2026 and does not yet have a mobile app. For boards that need amenity booking or visitor management, dedicated condo platforms cover more of the operational surface area.

### 2. PayHOA — Best for Dues-First Boards

PayHOA''s strength is the homeowner-facing workflow: online dues collection via ACH and card, payment history portal, document storage, violation management with photo evidence. For a board where the dominant pain is collecting dues and enforcing CC&Rs, PayHOA covers the daily workload at $50-$300/month depending on size.

The accounting side is the gap. Operating and reserve funds share a single ledger. For a 50-home association where reserve balances are modest, the gap may be acceptable. For a 150-home association with $200K+ in reserves, it is the same exposure QuickBooks creates.

### 3. MoneyMinder — Best for Treasurer-Only Workflows

MoneyMinder is genuine fund accounting at ~$159/year. The fund separation model works correctly; budget vs. actual reporting is clean; bank reconciliation is straightforward. It is the right tool when the board structure is "treasurer handles accounting, the rest of the board handles dues collection through a bank portal and violations through paper or email."

The gap: no homeowner portal, no dues collection, no violation tracking. The board will use multiple tools.

## The Other Two

### 4. HOALife

HOALife covers violation tracking well, with QuickBooks integration handling accounting. For a community where violation enforcement is the dominant workload and the existing accounting setup is QuickBooks, HOALife adds the violation workflow without forcing an accounting migration. The QuickBooks dependency carries the same commingling concern that affects any QuickBooks-based stack.

### 5. Condo Control

Condo Control is a full-suite condo operations platform. Amenity booking, visitor management, parcel tracking, and document storage are mature. The financial features are lighter than the operational features. For a self-managed condo board with shared amenities and a desire for operational breadth, Condo Control fits, with a separate accounting tool handling the reserve compliance side.

## Why We Excluded QuickBooks from the List

QuickBooks is widely used; it is also widely identified as a source of commingling exposure for HOAs. Including it on a "best HOA accounting software" list would imply that it is fit-for-purpose for the use case. It is not. Self-managed boards using QuickBooks today should treat migration to purpose-built fund accounting as an upgrade path rather than a feature wishlist item.

## Methodology

This list is based on the structural fit between platform capabilities and the specific needs of self-managed HOA boards in the 30-200 unit range. Pricing was verified against public pricing pages as of April 2026. We did not include national property management platforms (Vantaca, AppFolio, CINC) because their pricing models and feature sets are oriented to management companies rather than self-managed volunteer boards.

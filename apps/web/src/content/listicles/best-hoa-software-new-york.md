## Why New York HOA Software Has Two Compliance Layers

New York is the unusual state where common-interest housing covers two distinct legal structures with different software needs. Condominiums are governed by Real Property Law Article 9-B, with unit-level ownership and standard condo-style governance. Cooperatives are governed by the Business Corporation Law, with shareholders owning shares of a corporation that owns the building, plus proprietary leases granting occupancy rights to specific units.

Software built for condo associations does not handle co-op share tracking and proprietary leases. Software built for co-ops does not always handle the unit-level financial separation that condos need. Boards that pick the wrong tool end up with workflow friction that compounds over years.

On top of that, New York layers cybersecurity compliance through 23 NYCRR Part 500. Associations that meet the covered-entity threshold — typically larger buildings or those processing significant volumes of owner financial data — face written-policy, access-control, MFA, and breach-notification requirements. Even associations not directly covered face de facto pressure through their banking partners and management companies.

The personal-liability exposure for New York condo and co-op board members is materially higher than most states. The audit trail produced by the board's software is the operative defense in any dispute.

## What to Look For in New York Software

**Audit log at the transaction level.** Every fund movement should be captured with user, timestamp, and before/after values. This is the single most important control under both fiduciary-duty doctrine and 23 NYCRR 500.

**Fund accounting at the ledger level.** Operating and reserve funds need separate ledgers. New York condo and co-op disputes routinely surface commingling claims; ledger-level separation is the structural defense.

**Role-based access and MFA.** 23 NYCRR 500.7 requires access controls, and 500.12 requires MFA. Software with both built in (rather than bolted on through a third-party SSO) reduces compliance gap risk.

**Document retention.** Meeting minutes, notices, and financial disclosures need to be retained and retrievable. New York condo and co-op disputes routinely turn on whether the board can produce specific documents from years prior.

## The Five Best Options for New York Boards

### 1. Gavelhouse — Best for Audit-Trail Compliance

We built Gavelhouse because no existing tool combined fund accounting with the audit-log depth that New York's compliance environment requires. Every transaction is captured with user, timestamp, and before/after values. Fund separation is enforced at the database level, not as a presentation-layer convention.

The trade-off: Gavelhouse does not yet handle co-op share tracking and proprietary leases. For NY condo associations, the fit is direct. For NY co-ops, Gavelhouse covers the financial side but not the share-corporation governance side.

### 2. BuildingLink — Best for Staffed Manhattan and Brooklyn Buildings

BuildingLink is the dominant platform for staffed New York buildings. Doormen, package tracking, visitor management, key tracking, and resident communication are all native to the platform. For a high-rise with a full-time concierge and 200+ units, BuildingLink is the operational baseline most NYC buildings standardize on.

The financial side is lighter than the operational side. Operating and reserve fund separation is not enforced at the ledger level, and most BuildingLink buildings pair the platform with a management company's accounting stack.

### 3. Condo Control — Best for Mid-Size Condos With Amenities

Condo Control covers amenity booking, visitor management, parcel tracking, and basic financials for mid-size condo associations. Pricing in the $80-$200/month range works for buildings between 50 and 200 units with shared amenities like gyms, pools, or party rooms.

Like BuildingLink, Condo Control does not enforce operating and reserve fund separation at the ledger level, and the audit-log depth is operational rather than financial-transaction granular.

## The Other Two

### 4. PayHOA

PayHOA fits New York planned communities outside the dense urban core — Long Island, Westchester, the Hudson Valley — where the workload looks more like a typical suburban HOA than a Manhattan high-rise. Dues collection, homeowner portals, and violation management are mature.

### 5. AppFolio

AppFolio is the platform New York management companies typically use to run portfolios of associations. Per-unit pricing with a $400+/month minimum makes it the wrong shape for self-managed boards, but for a management company running ten condo associations, it covers the operational and financial workload.

## Making the Right Choice for Your New York Association

Start with structure. If you are a co-op, BuildingLink (paired with a co-op-specific accounting tool) is the typical stack. If you are a self-managed condo focused on audit-trail compliance and reserve fund separation, Gavelhouse is the closest fit. If you are a staffed condo high-rise with amenities, Condo Control or BuildingLink covers the operational breadth.

For 23 NYCRR 500 compliance, verify your software has role-based access, MFA, audit logging, and a documented incident response process. Most enterprise platforms cover this; verify before signing.

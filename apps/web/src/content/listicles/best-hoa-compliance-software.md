## Why HOA Compliance Software Is Rated by Documentation, Not Features

Every HOA software platform on the market claims compliance support. The marketing language is similar across platforms: "audit-ready," "fully compliant," "state regulation support." The actual question is what the platform documents automatically — and whether that documentation survives the situations where compliance evidence actually matters.

The two situations: (1) a state audit or CPA review of the association''s books, and (2) a board liability dispute, typically initiated by a homeowner alleging mismanagement.

In both cases, the operative evidence is the audit log. Who did what, when, with what before-and-after values. A platform that captures this for every transaction produces the evidence that resolves the dispute or audit. A platform that captures it intermittently — or only for specific action types — produces gaps that surface as commingling claims, fiduciary duty violations, or insurance coverage disputes.

This is the filter that separates real compliance software from marketing-language compliance software. The question to ask any platform: "Show me the audit log entry for a single $500 disbursement made last month." The answer reveals whether the platform actually captures what compliance requires.

## What to Look For in HOA Compliance Software

**Per-transaction audit log.** User, timestamp, action, before-state, after-state, IP. For every fund movement and record change, not just specific high-risk actions.

**Fund-level operating/reserve separation.** At the database level, not the report layer. State commingling statutes apply to the underlying records, not the presentation.

**Dual control on disbursements.** Two-user authorization required for transactions above a threshold. Both an industry best practice and a fidelity bond underwriting requirement.

**Document retention.** Meeting minutes, notices, financial reports, correspondence — retained and producible on owner request.

**Role-based access.** Granular permissions matching board roles. Treasurer can do what president cannot, and vice versa.

**Fidelity bond evidence reporting.** Reports that document the controls in place, suitable for fidelity bond underwriting submission.

## The Five Best Options

### 1. Gavelhouse — Best for Self-Managed Boards Focused on Audit Trails

We built Gavelhouse''s audit log as a non-optional part of the platform, not a feature flag. Every transaction is captured with user, timestamp, action, before-state, after-state, and IP. Fund-level separation is enforced at the database layer. Dual-control workflows require two distinct users to authorize disbursements above board-configured thresholds.

For a self-managed board where the dominant compliance pressure is reserve fund management, fiduciary duty documentation, and state audit readiness, Gavelhouse covers the audit-trail surface area at flat pricing. The trade-off: Gavelhouse launched in 2026 and does not yet have the report customization depth of enterprise platforms.

### 2. Vantaca — Best for Enterprise Management Companies

Vantaca provides enterprise-grade audit logging and dual-control workflows for management companies. The compliance reporting layer is mature; the audit log captures the depth state audits and CPA reviews expect.

Pricing requires sales contact and implementation runs months. For a 30-150 community management company that needs the depth, the friction is acceptable. For a smaller operator, Gavelhouse''s flat pricing covers the same compliance surface area at materially lower cost.

### 3. CINC Systems — Best for Traditional HOA Management Companies

CINC Systems offers detailed audit logging, dual-control workflows, and banking-integrated controls. Purpose-built for HOA management companies; the compliance workflow matches the traditional management-company operating model.

Pricing requires sales contact. Implementation timelines run long. For a traditional HOA management company that wants a fully HOA-specific compliance platform, CINC fits.

## The Other Two

### 4. PayHOA

PayHOA captures activity logs on owner-facing actions and supports document retention. The fund-level separation gap and the lack of dual-control disbursement workflow mean PayHOA''s compliance depth is operational rather than financial-controls granular. For a board where owner-facing compliance dominates and financial controls live elsewhere, PayHOA covers the relevant workload.

### 5. MoneyMinder

MoneyMinder''s fund accounting model produces a clean financial-transaction audit trail. The lack of dual-control workflow, document retention, and homeowner-facing features means it covers the treasurer-side compliance surface area at low cost while leaving the rest of the compliance workload to other tools or paper-based processes.

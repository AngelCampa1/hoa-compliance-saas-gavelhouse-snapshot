regulations:
  - heading: Independent Reserve Review Every Three Years
    content: >-
      HRS §514B-148 requires condominium associations to conduct an independent
      review of reserves at least every three years. Act 62 (2022, effective
      January 1, 2023) extended the required projection horizon from 20 years to
      30 years, aligning Hawaii with Florida's post-Surfside standard. The study
      must assess all major components, their remaining useful life, and the
      cost to repair or replace them using minimum 30-year cash-flow
      projections.
    variant: warning
  - heading: Only State with Explicit Minimum Funding Standard
    content: >-
      Hawaii is the only U.S. state with an explicit minimum reserve funding
      threshold. Associations must maintain at least 50% of estimated
      replacement reserves under the component method, or 100% under the
      cash-flow method. This is not a recommendation or best practice, it is a
      statutory floor. Associations below the threshold are out of compliance
      with HRS §514B-148.
    variant: warning
  - heading: Personal Liability for Board Members
    content: >-
      HRS §514B-148 makes explicit that board members can face personal
      liability for mismanagement of reserve funds. Hawaii courts have applied
      this provision in cases where board members knew about a funding shortfall
      and continued to approve operating expenses that depleted reserves.
    variant: warning
  - heading: "Act 296 (2025): Condominium Loan Program"
    content: >-
      Act 296 (2025) created the Hawaii Condominium Loan Program, providing
      state-backed financing for essential repairs. This gives associations
      access to below-market-rate loans to fund major repairs when reserves are
      insufficient. The program targets associations facing urgent structural or
      safety-related repairs that the reserve fund cannot cover.
    variant: info
  - heading: "Applies to Condos Only, Not Non-Condo HOAs"
    content: >-
      HRS Chapter 514B applies only to condominium associations. Non-condo HOAs
      in Hawaii, such as planned community associations, are not subject to the
      reserve study mandate, minimum funding standard, or three-year review
      cycle. Non-condo boards should still review their governing documents for
      private reserve requirements.
    variant: info
  - heading: Fannie Mae Reserve Allocation Requirement
    content: >-
      Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03 sets two deadlines: (1) The Limited
      Review process for condo projects is retired effective August 3, 2026. (2)
      The minimum reserve allocation increases from 10% to 15% for Full Review
      loan applications dated on or after January 4, 2027. Associations below
      the 15% threshold will be classified as non-warrantable, preventing
      conventional mortgage lending on units in the community.
    variant: warning
  - heading: Documented Compliance Reduces Personal Risk
    content: >-
      A board that obtains a reserve study from a qualified reserve specialist,
      adopts a funding plan based on the study, and reviews both at annual board
      meetings has a documented record of compliance. Hawaii courts look at
      whether the board acted in good faith and with reasonable care. A current
      study and a funded reserve are evidence of both.
    variant: success
faqs:
  - q: How often must a Hawaii condo association review its reserves?
    a: >-
      HRS §514B-148 requires an independent review at least every three years,
      not five as many older summaries state. Act 62 (2022, effective January 1,
      2023) also extended the minimum projection horizon from 20 to 30 years.
      Associations relying on pre-2023 studies should confirm they meet the
      updated projection requirement.
  - q: What is Hawaii's minimum reserve funding standard?
    a: >-
      Hawaii is the only state with an explicit minimum: 50% of estimated
      replacement reserves under the component method, or 100% under the
      cash-flow method. Associations below the threshold are out of statutory
      compliance. This standard applies to condominiums only, not to non-condo
      HOAs.
  - q: Can a Hawaii condo association waive its reserve funding?
    a: >-
      HRS §514B-148 does not contain a waiver provision comparable to Florida's
      two-thirds vote option. Hawaii does not allow reserve waivers by member
      vote. The funding obligation is mandatory.
  - q: Does Hawaii's reserve mandate apply to non-condo HOAs?
    a: >-
      No. HRS Chapter 514B applies only to condominium associations. Planned
      community HOAs are not subject to the reserve study mandate, three-year
      review cycle, or minimum funding standard. Non-condo boards should review
      their governing documents for any private reserve requirements.
  - q: What is the Condominium Loan Program under Act 296?
    a: >-
      Act 296 (2025) created a state-backed Condominium Loan Program that
      provides below-market-rate financing for essential repairs. Associations
      facing urgent structural or safety repairs that exceed current reserve
      balances can access this program to fund the work without relying entirely
      on special assessments.
  - q: How does Fannie Mae's reserve requirement affect Hawaii associations?

      applications dated on or after January 4, 2027. Non-warrantable

Hawaii's reserve fund requirements are strict by any measure. The personal liability language in HRS §514B-148 is not buried in a footnote; it is the consequence the legislature attached to inadequate reserve management. For volunteer board members in Honolulu or Kailua, that means the decision to defer reserve contributions is not just a financial trade-off. It is a decision that could attach personal liability if the resulting shortfall causes harm.

The five-year study cycle is manageable for most associations if they plan for it. The cost of a reserve study is predictable and can itself be included in the operating budget. Boards that treat the study as an unexpected expense every five years are not budgeting correctly. Planning for the study is part of the compliance obligation, not separate from it.

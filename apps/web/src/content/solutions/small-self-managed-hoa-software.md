## Small does not mean simple forever

Many small communities assume they can keep running on email and spreadsheets indefinitely. Usually that holds until dues follow-up gets messy, the treasurer changes, or the board has to explain reserve posture to owners. The software decision should happen before those moments compound.

## Built for the Starter tier: 50 homes or fewer

Most HOA software is priced for professional property managers. That means small HOAs either pay for features they will never use or get charged per unit. A 25-home HOA pays just as much as a 250-home one.

Gavelhouse Starter is for HOAs of 50 homes or fewer. One volunteer doing the books. No per-unit fees. Flat pricing that fits a small budget.

The core jobs for a small board are the same as any other board: keep operating and reserve funds separate, collect dues, track who owes what, and produce a clean financial report for the annual meeting. Gavelhouse handles those jobs from day one. You do not need to hire a CPA to set it up or a trainer to learn it.

If your board is taking over its own finances after working with a management company, the starting point is different. See [self-managing after leaving a management company](/solutions/self-managing-hoa-after-management-company/) for guidance on that handoff.

## When one person does the books

Small HOAs often have one person handling the finances. That person is usually a volunteer with a full-time job. When they leave the board, everything they know leaves with them.

Gavelhouse keeps the records in the HOA's account. The new treasurer does not need the old treasurer to explain the QuickBooks file. Payment history, reserve balance, and owner accounts are all there, ready to hand off.

This matters more in a small HOA than almost anywhere else. There is no management company to absorb the transition. There is no staff. The board is it.

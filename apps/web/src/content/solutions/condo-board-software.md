    a: >-
      Condo associations are financially responsible only for common elements,
      not the interiors of individual units. Unit boundary accounting tracks
      which expenditures belong to the association versus which are the owner's
      responsibility under the declaration. Misclassifying a repair as a common

      during resale or insurance claims. Software that does not enforce this

## Condominiums are not HOAs with a different name

The legal distinction between a condominium association and a homeowners association is not a technicality. Most states maintain separate statutes for each. There is a Condominium Act and an HOA or Planned Community Act. The compliance obligations differ in ways that matter operationally.

The most significant difference is reserve funding. HOA statutes in many states allow boards to waive or reduce reserve contributions with a member vote. Most state Condominium Acts do not. Florida went further after the 2021 Surfside collapse. Senate Bill 4-D (2022) requires structural integrity reserve studies for buildings three stories or taller. It prohibits condo associations from waiving or reducing reserves for structural components. California's Davis-Stirling Act requires full reserve funding for all major components with a remaining useful life under 30 years.

Boards that use software built for the more flexible HOA reserve rules carry a compliance gap. They may not find it until a lender questionnaire or a state inquiry arrives.

## The commingling problem is structural, not behavioral

Operating and reserve fund commingling is one of the most common violations in self-managed condo associations. It usually happens not because a board member is acting in bad faith. It happens because the accounting tool does not enforce the separation.

QuickBooks does not prevent an operator from posting a reserve expense to the operating account or vice versa. Spreadsheets have no controls at all. The commingling risk is a software architecture problem, not a training problem.

Gavelhouse was built to solve this at the database layer. Operating and reserve funds are separate accounts with separate ledgers. A transaction posted to the operating account cannot pull from reserve funds without an explicit, documented transfer. That constraint is enforced by how the data model works. It is not just a workflow suggestion.

## What condo board software needs to handle

Condo boards evaluating software should think in terms of their actual compliance obligations, not the feature list:

**Reserve fund review.** Does the software keep operating and reserve fund balances separated so the board can compare them against the reserve study in its existing review process?

**Fund-aware accounting.** Is the chart of accounts built around operating versus reserve separation, or does the board have to enforce that discipline manually through naming conventions?

**Structural integrity reserve review (where applicable).** For Florida condominiums subject to SB 4-D, does the board maintain structural integrity reserve study inputs in a process that can be reviewed alongside the financial records?

**Governance documentation.** Can the board produce auditable meeting minutes, financial statements, and board resolutions that satisfy the state Condominium Act?

**Lender and resale readiness.** Condo resales require the association to produce financial statements, reserve balances, and disclosure packages quickly. Software that requires manual reconstruction for every resale adds real cost and delay.

## The volunteer board continuity problem

Self-managed condo boards face a continuity problem that property management firms do not. When a volunteer treasurer or president rotates off the board, the knowledge about reserve history and financial structure often leaves with them.

The outgoing treasurer's spreadsheet is not a handoff. It is a gap. The incoming board member has to reconstruct context that should already be in the system.

Software built for condo boards should treat continuity as a first-order problem. Every reserve contribution, every fund transfer, and every board decision should be documented and accessible to the next person who takes the role.

## Condo fund records and mortgage lending

When a unit owner in your building tries to get a mortgage, the lender reviews the condo project. Fannie Mae and FHA both look at the association's reserve funding and financial health. A board that cannot produce clean, fund-separated records can slow or block unit sales.

The board does not need to track Fannie Mae thresholds on its own. That is the job of your reserve study and lender questionnaire process. What the board does need is clean records to hand over when asked. That means separate reserve balances, documented contribution history, and a financial report ready to go without rebuilding under deadline pressure.

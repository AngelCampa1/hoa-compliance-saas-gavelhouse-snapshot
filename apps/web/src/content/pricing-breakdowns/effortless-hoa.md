## The per-home pricing curve

Effortless HOA uses a single per-home rate with no tiered pricing or volume discounts. This makes the cost calculation simple and predictable at small sizes, but creates significant cost differences compared to flat-rate platforms as communities grow.

| Community size | Effortless HOA monthly | Gavelhouse monthly |
| -------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------ |
| 30 homes       | $90                    | $20                |
| 50 homes       | $150                   | $20                |
| 75 homes       | $225                   | $49                |
| 100 homes      | $300                   | $49                |
| 150 homes      | $450                   | $49                |
| 200 homes      | $600                   | $99                |

The cost gap opens significantly between 75 and 200 homes. A 150-home community pays $401/mo more with Effortless HOA than with Gavelhouse, nearly $4,800/year.

## What the $3/home covers

Effortless HOA is an all-inclusive single tier. You pay $3/home and get access to all features: dues collection, homeowner portal, violation tracking, the AI CC&R assistant, document storage, and communications tools.

The AI CC&R assistant is a notable differentiator. Homeowners can ask questions about community rules and get automated responses, which reduces the email volume board members field. This is a real operational benefit for boards where homeowner questions about parking, landscaping, and modification requests consume significant time.

## What the $3/home does not cover

Reserve fund tracking is not in Effortless HOA's feature set. Rental management is explicitly excluded. For communities where either of these matters, $3/home buys a platform that covers roughly half the administrative load.

## The growth budget risk

Per-home pricing is unpredictable over a multi-year budget horizon. If your community builds out remaining lots, takes in new units, or converts garages to ADUs, your software cost increases automatically. A board that approved $150/mo in software costs can find itself at $300/mo two years later without any pricing change from the vendor.

Flat-rate pricing by tier locks the cost at a known level for the life of the tier. Most HOA boards benefit from that predictability when presenting budgets to homeowners.

## The Foundation Problem: Enforcement Without a Valid Policy

Before discussing evidence and fines, understand the most common reason HOA short-term rental enforcement fails: the restriction isn't valid.

Boards frequently discover they've been trying to enforce an STR rule that exists only in board minutes, a letter to owners, or an informal community policy. When an owner pushes back and hires an attorney, the first thing that attorney checks is whether the restriction is recorded in the CC&Rs or a formal amendment to the Rules and Regulations.

If the restriction isn't recorded, the board has no legal authority to enforce it. Courts have dismissed HOA enforcement actions in California, Florida, and Texas because the "policy" being enforced existed only as a board resolution or management letter. The owner continues renting; the board pays legal fees to defend a lost position.

The fix is straightforward but requires work: adopt a proper STR restriction through the correct amendment process, record it with the county, and enforce from that clean foundation.

## What a Valid STR Policy Must Include

A short-term rental restriction embedded in your CC&Rs or recorded Rules and Regulations should define:

**Minimum lease term.** The most common definition is "no rental for a period of less than 30 consecutive days." Some communities use 60 days or 6 months. The chosen threshold determines how the policy interacts with state law preemption rules (see below). Be precise — "short stay" or "transient rental" without a day count invites definitional disputes.

**What counts as a rental.** Does a guest who does not pay rent trigger the restriction? Does an owner who lets a family member stay for two weeks violate the policy? Most STR restrictions focus on commercial rental activity — receiving compensation through a booking platform or direct arrangement — rather than non-commercial personal use.

**Prohibited activities.** The restriction should expressly prohibit both the act of listing a unit for short-term rental and the act of accepting bookings for stays shorter than the minimum term. Prohibiting only the completed stay creates an argument that the listing itself doesn't violate anything.

**Remedies.** Cite the specific fine amounts or the board's authority to set fines consistent with state law, the right to seek injunctive relief, and the ability to place a lien for unpaid fines.

## Building an Evidence Package

Once you have a valid restriction and identify a likely violator, gather evidence before sending any notice.

### Platform Screenshots

Search for the unit on Airbnb, VRBO, Booking.com, Hipcamp, and any other platforms you're aware of. Enter the community address or the nearest cross streets as the search location. When you find a matching listing:

- Screenshot the listing header showing the property description, photos, and location
- Screenshot the pricing page showing nightly or weekly rates
- Screenshot the calendar showing available or blocked booking dates
- Note the host name or username
- Record the full URL of the listing

Date-stamp everything. Use your computer's screenshot tool, which embeds metadata, or a browser extension designed for legal documentation. A screenshot with no verifiable timestamp is still useful but is weaker than one with a clear date-and-time record.

### Corroborating Evidence

Platform screenshots establish that the unit is being offered for short-term rental. Additional evidence that strengthens the case:

- **Neighbor witness statements** — written statements from adjacent unit owners who observed unfamiliar guests with luggage arriving and departing on short cycles
- **Parking records** — visitor parking logs showing multiple different vehicles registered to the same unit over short periods
- **Package delivery logs** — if your community has package management, a pattern of deliveries to a unit from multiple different names suggests transient occupancy
- **Property manager records** — if the unit has a professional STR property manager, their marketing materials may confirm the unit is operated as an STR

Triangulated evidence across multiple sources is significantly harder to contest than a single screenshot.

## The Cease-and-Desist Process

Send the formal notice to the owner by certified mail with return receipt requested. Email alone is insufficient as primary notice in most states — use mail as the primary method and email as a supplement.

The notice should:

1. State the specific restriction violated with the CC&R or Rules section number
2. Attach the evidence (screenshots, witness statements)
3. Demand the listing be removed and all future bookings be cancelled within 10–14 business days
4. State the fine schedule that will begin accruing if the violation continues
5. Notify the owner of their right to request a board hearing within the timeframe specified in your CC&Rs (typically 14–21 days)

Keep a complete copy of the notice package, including all attachments. When the certified mail return receipt comes back, staple it to the copy of the letter in your enforcement file.

## Fines: Structure and Statutory Limits

Fines for STR violations should be structured as continuing violations — meaning the fine accrues each day the listing remains active after the deadline in your cease-and-desist notice.

Statutory caps vary by state:

- **Florida** (Chapter 720.305): Fine cap of $100 per violation per day, up to $1,000 aggregate per violation, unless the CC&Rs specify higher amounts within the limits of the statute.
- **California** (Civil Code 5725): Fines must be set by the board in a schedule adopted at a duly noticed meeting; the statute does not set a specific cap but requires fines to be "reasonable."
- **Texas** (Property Code Chapter 209): HOAs can impose reasonable fines for violations; the statute does not set a specific daily cap but requires the amount to be disclosed in the association's fine schedule.
- **Colorado** (HB 22-1137): Limits fines to $500 for a first violation per occurrence for associations over a certain size.

Check your own state's HOA statute for the applicable cap. Structure the fine schedule in your governing documents or adopted fine policy to reflect the maximum the statute allows — if your fine schedule was set years ago, it may be below what the current statute permits.

## Preemption: When Cities and States Limit HOA Authority

### State-Level Preemption

Arizona enacted SB 1168 in 2023 prohibiting HOAs from banning STRs entirely. Arizona HOAs can require owners to register short-term rentals with the association, require guests to comply with community rules, and limit the number of overnight guests — but cannot adopt a blanket prohibition.

Florida has not enacted statewide HOA STR preemption, but several Florida cities have STR licensing ordinances that operate alongside HOA rules. Owning a city STR license does not protect an owner from HOA enforcement under a validly adopted restriction.

Texas preempts certain municipal STR regulations but has not preempted HOA authority over STRs. HOAs in Texas can ban or restrict STRs through CC&Rs.

### Municipal Preemption

Some cities explicitly state that their STR ordinance supersedes any conflicting private restriction. This is uncommon and legally contested, but boards in cities with aggressive STR regulation should confirm with an HOA attorney whether local ordinances affect enforceability of CC&R-based restrictions.

## Platform Notification as a Secondary Tool

Both Airbnb and VRBO have processes for reporting listings that violate local laws or governing documents:

- **Airbnb** has a "Report a listing" function and a dedicated channel for government and association requests at their Trust and Safety team.
- **VRBO/Vrbo** accepts legal notices through their owner relations process.

Neither platform guarantees removal based on an HOA complaint, and the process can take weeks. Platform notification works best after you've sent the cease-and-desist to the owner and the listing is still active. Include a copy of the applicable CC&R provision and your enforcement notice when contacting the platform. Frame the request around the owner's violation of applicable governing documents rather than as a personal complaint.

## Managing Enforcement Consistently

Selective enforcement — pursuing some STR violations while ignoring others — creates legal exposure for the board. Once you establish an enforcement policy, apply it uniformly. Document every case in writing. If you receive a complaint about one unit but not another, investigate both.

Inconsistent enforcement doesn't just expose the board to selective enforcement claims; it signals to the community that violations have no real consequences, which increases the violation rate over time.

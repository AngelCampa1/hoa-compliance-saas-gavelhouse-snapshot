  Most HOA software is built for professional property managers. If your board

faqs:
  - q: How long does it take to switch HOA management software?
    a: >-
      Most boards can migrate from spreadsheets to a new platform in a weekend.
      Migrating from one software to another takes longer, usually two to four
      weeks, because you need to export historical data, clean it up, and import
      it into the new system. Budget time for training board members who will
      use the software.
  - q: Should the full board vote on HOA software?
    a: >-
      That depends on your bylaws and budget approval process. For software
      costing more than $100/month, most boards bring the decision to a board
      vote. The treasurer and president typically lead the evaluation, then
      present the recommendation with pricing and a comparison to the current
      process.
  - q: Can you switch HOA software mid-year?
    a: >-
      Yes, but the cleanest transition happens at the start of a fiscal year
      when you are starting a fresh accounting period. Mid-year switches require
      migrating partial-year data, which adds reconciliation work. If you find
      the right tool mid-year, start a trial, run it parallel to your current
      process for 30 days, then switch at year-end.
definitions:
  - term: Fund Accounting
    definition: >-
      An accounting method that tracks money in separate pools called funds. An
      HOA's operating fund covers dues income and routine expenses; the reserve
      fund covers capital replacement savings. Fund accounting keeps these pools
      separate at the ledger level with independent balances and reports, the
      structure required for state reserve disclosures.
  - term: Per-Unit Pricing
    definition: >-
      A software pricing model where the monthly cost is calculated by
      multiplying the number of units in the community by a per-unit rate.
      Per-unit pricing makes cost unpredictable as communities grow: $2/unit is
      $100/mo at 50 units but $500/mo at 250 units.
  - term: Reserve Compliance Dashboard
    definition: >-
      A software feature that tracks your current reserve fund balance against
      the target balance from your reserve study, expressed as a percent-funded
      figure. Platforms with this feature allow boards to monitor compliance
      status at a glance rather than maintaining separate spreadsheets.
answers:
  - question: How do you evaluate HOA software for reserve fund compliance?
    answer: >-
      Ask vendors directly whether they have fund accounting with separate
      operating and reserve ledgers, and whether they track your reserve balance
      against reserve study targets. If they refer you to a QuickBooks
      integration page, the answer to both questions is no. For boards in
      California, Florida, Virginia, Washington, and other states with reserve
      mandates, these two features are not optional.
  - question: How long does it take to switch HOA management software?
    answer: >-
      Migrating from spreadsheets to a new platform typically takes a weekend.
      Migrating from one software to another takes two to four weeks due to
      historical data export, cleanup, and import. Budget time for training
      board members who will use the new system.
  - question: Should the full board vote on HOA software?
    answer: >-
      For software costing more than $100/month, most boards bring the decision
      to a board vote. The treasurer and president typically lead the evaluation
      and present a recommendation with pricing and a comparison to the current
      process. The vote creates a documented record of the decision.
  - question: How do you calculate total cost when evaluating HOA software?
    answer: >-
      Calculate your 12-month total at your current community size and at 150%
      of that size. The gap between those numbers shows the risk of per-unit
      pricing. Include setup fees, payment processing fees (typically 2-3% on
      dues), and any integration costs for tools the platform requires (like
      QuickBooks for HOALife).

steps:
  - title: Audit what your board actually does now
    content: >-
      Before comparing software, list every task your board handles: dues
      collection, violation notices, financial reports, homeowner communication,
      maintenance requests, meeting minutes. Note which ones create the most
      friction. If you spend two hours per month chasing unpaid dues, that is
      your primary problem. If you field ten calls a week about account
      balances, an owner portal matters more than accounting depth. Software
      evaluations that start with features instead of pain points usually end in
      over-buying.
  - title: Check if the tool separates operating and reserve funds
    content: >-
      This is the most important technical question in HOA accounting. Operating
      funds pay for regular maintenance, insurance, and administrative costs.
      Reserve funds accumulate for capital replacements: roofs, paving, pool
      equipment. Most HOA software treats these as a single general ledger. If
      your state requires reserve fund disclosures (California, Florida,
      Virginia, Washington, and others do), you need a tool that separates the
      two at the accounting level, not just as budget line items. Ask vendors
      directly: do you have fund accounting with separate operating and reserve
      ledgers? If they send you to a QuickBooks integration page, the answer is
      no.
  - title: Confirm it handles your state's reserve requirements
    content: >-
      Twelve states mandate reserve studies: California, Colorado, Delaware,
      Florida, Hawaii, Maryland, Nevada, Oregon, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia, and
      Washington. Florida's requirements tightened significantly after the
      Surfside collapse in 2021. Condo associations now have hard deadlines for
      reserve study updates and funding milestones. If you are in one of these
      states, your software needs to track your reserve balance against study
      targets, not just let you record transactions. A tool that does not cover
      your state's specific requirements will leave your board doing manual
      tracking in parallel.
  - title: Calculate total cost including setup and scaling fees
    content: >-
      Software pricing for HOA tools hides cost in a few common places. Per-unit
      fees compound as your community grows: $2/unit looks small at 50 units but
      is $200/month at 100 units and $500/month at 250 units. Setup fees for
      data migration or onboarding can add $200-$1,000 upfront. Integration fees
      for payment processing typically add 2-3% on top of dues. Flat-rate tools
      eliminate the scaling surprise but may cost more at small community sizes.
      Calculate your 12-month total cost at your current community size and at
      150% of that size. The gap between those numbers tells you how risky the
      pricing model is for your budget.
  - title: Run a 30-day trial before the board votes
    content: >-
      Every major HOA tool offers a trial period. Use it. Have the treasurer run
      a full month of transactions in the new system: record dues payments, post
      a violation, run a financial report, test the owner portal from a
      homeowner account. Most software problems reveal themselves in the first
      week of real use, not in a demo. Bring the trial results to the board
      vote: what worked, what did not, what questions came up. A vote on a tool
      you have actually used is more defensible than a vote on a sales demo.

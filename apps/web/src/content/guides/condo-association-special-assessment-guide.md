# Condo Association Special Assessment: Vote Thresholds, Notice, and Liens

We built Gavelhouse because the gap between what self-managed condo boards are required to do and what most boards actually do has grown wider every year — especially since Surfside. The 2021 collapse of Champlain Towers South triggered a regulatory rewrite in Florida that has since influenced legislation in other coastal states. Boards that have not updated their reserve practices and assessment procedures since 2022 are operating under rules that no longer apply.

This guide covers special assessments specifically — what triggers them, how the vote works, and how to record liens correctly when owners do not pay.

## Condo associations vs HOA associations

Condominiums operate under state condo acts. HOAs typically operate under separate planned community or general nonprofit statutes. The differences matter:

- **Florida:** Condos governed by Statute 718. HOAs governed by Statute 720. Different reserve, notice, and lien rules.
- **California:** Both fall under Davis-Stirling Common Interest Development Act (Civil Code 4000+), but condo-specific provisions exist for structural elements.
- **Illinois:** Condos governed by 765 ILCS 605 (Condominium Property Act). HOAs governed by 765 ILCS 160 (Common Interest Community Association Act).
- **Other states:** Similar bifurcation.

When in doubt, identify your governing statute by name and read the assessment chapter directly.

## What changed after Surfside

The Champlain Towers South collapse in June 2021 killed 98 people. The investigation revealed years of deferred structural maintenance that condo boards had funded inadequately. Florida Senate Bill 4-D (2022) and follow-on legislation responded with major reforms:

### Mandatory structural integrity reserves

For condominium buildings three stories and taller in Florida, structural integrity reserve items can no longer be waived by member vote. Categories include:

- Roof systems
- Load-bearing walls
- Floor structure
- Foundation
- Fireproofing and fire protection systems
- Plumbing
- Electrical systems
- Waterproofing
- Windows
- Other structural elements identified by the inspector

### Milestone inspections

Florida Statute 553.899 requires structural inspections at 30 years of age (25 years near coastline) and every 10 years thereafter, conducted by licensed engineers or architects.

### Structural Integrity Reserve Studies (SIRS)

Required every 10 years for condominium buildings three stories and taller, separate from the general reserve study.

### Disclosure expansion

New owners and existing members must receive the milestone inspection summary and SIRS findings.

## What this means for assessments

For older Florida condominiums that previously waived structural reserves — a common practice before 2022 — funding the new requirements often means:

- Dues increases of 30-100%
- Per-unit catch-up special assessments of $20,000-$100,000 or more
- Multi-year assessment plans

These outcomes are not optional. Boards that try to maintain pre-2022 funding levels are exposing directors to personal liability for non-compliance with the statute.

## The assessment vote process

### Step 1: Read the declaration and statute

The declaration sets the threshold for board-only action. Most Florida condo declarations allow board imposition up to a defined limit (often a percent of budget) and require member vote above. The statute sets the procedural floor — declarations cannot waive statutory protections.

### Step 2: Calculate the assessment

Work from the reserve study or SIRS findings. Identify the gap, the timing of the funding need, and the per-unit amount. Build payment plan options for owners who cannot pay lump sum — typically 12, 24, or 36 month plans with formal written agreements.

### Step 3: Schedule the vote with proper notice

Notice requirements (Florida Statute 718.112 for condos):

- 14 days written notice for member meetings on most assessments
- Notice must include the assessment amount and purpose
- Posted in the building and mailed/delivered to each unit owner
- Specific budget meeting notice rules (typically 14 days for adoption, with budget package delivered to owners)

California requires 30 days notice for special assessments above 5% of budget under Civil Code 5605.

### Step 4: Conduct the vote

Confirm quorum at the start. Tabulate votes per the declaration's voting power rules. Use an inspector of elections where required. Document the result in minutes.

### Step 5: Bill and collect

Issue formal billing per the resolution. Track payments separately from regular dues. For non-payment, follow the lien procedure exactly.

## The lien procedure

Most condo acts grant an automatic statutory lien for unpaid assessments. Recording the lien requires:

### Pre-lien notice

Florida requires 30 days written notice before recording a claim of lien (Statute 718.121). California requires 30 days written notice and offer of payment plan or meet-and-confer (Civil Code 5660).

The notice must include statutorily required content — typically the amount owed, the time period, and the consequences of non-payment.

### Recording

After the notice period, the lien is recorded with the county recorder. The lien attaches to the unit and survives transfer.

### Foreclosure

If the lien remains unpaid, judicial or non-judicial foreclosure follows under state-specific procedure. Foreclosure for unpaid assessments is one of the most powerful collection tools, but also one of the most procedurally exposed — defective notice or improper recording invalidates the foreclosure and may shift fees to the association.

## Common procedural mistakes

### Skipping the SIRS or milestone inspection

Florida boards that have not commissioned the required inspections face direct statutory non-compliance regardless of how they assess.

### Insufficient notice

Sending the notice 10 days before the meeting when the statute requires 14. Easy to do, fatal to the assessment.

### Defective pre-lien notice

Recording the lien without the statutorily required content in the pre-lien notice. Voids the lien.

### Disclosure failures post-assessment

Approving a $50,000 per-unit assessment and failing to update resale disclosures. Direct path to personal director liability when units sell during the assessment period.

## Disclosure obligations

Every special assessment changes the resale disclosure landscape:

- Pending and approved special assessments must be disclosed
- Florida resale disclosures (Statute 718.503) require specific assessment information
- California Civil Code 4525 requires similar disclosures
- Misrepresentation creates personal director liability

Update the resale package the day the vote passes. Brief the closing agent. Notify the insurance carrier if D&O coverage requires it.

## The compliance lens

Condo special assessments are technical, procedural, and unforgiving. The statute prescribes the timeline, the content, and the consequences. Boards that follow the procedure carefully — even when the assessment itself is large and unpopular — survive challenges and protect directors. Boards that improvise face invalidated assessments, voided liens, and personal lawsuits.

If your condominium is in Florida and you have not updated your reserve practices since 2022, that is the first project. Everything else flows from the funding model the statute now requires.

## The legal floor under landscaping has shifted

Twenty years ago, an HOA could enforce a green-lawn rule almost anywhere in the country. Today, the legal floor under landscaping rules is fundamentally different. State legislatures responding to drought, water-supply pressure, and pollinator-habitat advocacy have enacted preemption statutes that void HOA rules conflicting with water-wise, native, drought-tolerant, or vegetable-garden landscaping. Most boards still operate as if the old framework applies and continue enforcing rules the state legislature has invalidated.

The exposure is twofold: the rule itself is unenforceable, so the homeowner does not have to pay the fine and may recover legal fees under the state HOA act''s prevailing-party provision. And the directors who voted to enforce a preempted rule may face personal liability for ultra vires acts — actions outside the corporate authority of the association. D&O coverage on ultra vires claims is uneven; some carriers exclude them entirely.

## State-by-state preemption snapshot

### California

Civil Code 4735 voids any HOA rule that prohibits or restricts low-water-using plants, artificial turf, or pervious paving. The statute also voids rules that require turf or that specify watering requirements above what local water authorities allow. The carveout: HOAs may still require homeowners to comply with general aesthetic standards as long as those standards do not effectively prohibit water-wise landscaping.

### Florida

Florida Statute 720.3075(4) protects "Florida-friendly landscaping" as defined in 373.185. The principles include right plant in the right place, water efficiency, fertilizing appropriately, mulching, attracting wildlife, managing yard pests responsibly, recycling yard waste, reducing stormwater runoff, and protecting waterfront. HOA covenants cannot prevent application of these principles. Florida also separately protects vegetable gardens on residential property under HB 145 (2019).

### Colorado

CRS 38-33.3-106.5 protects xeriscape, low water-using turf grass, drought-tolerant vegetative landscapes, and rain barrels. Boards may regulate height, weeds, and dead-plant removal, but cannot require turf or restrict water-wise alternatives.

### Nevada

NRS 116.330 protects water-wise landscaping. The Southern Nevada Water Authority''s rebate program for turf removal has driven widespread conversion; HOA enforcement that conflicts with the state preemption is invalid.

### Texas

Property Code 202.007 protects drought-resistant landscaping after a state water emergency declaration and protects rain barrels and composting devices. The protection is narrower than California or Colorado but is expanding.

## Architectural review under preemption

Architectural review provisions in CC&Rs typically allow the committee or board to approve or deny modifications based on published criteria. Under preemption, the criteria must not effectively prohibit protected landscaping. A criterion that says "all front-yard plant material must match the community palette" applied to deny drought-tolerant plants will fail.

The defensible approach is to publish criteria framed in principles — visual harmony with surrounding properties, plant health, no abandoned-looking sites, no debris, screening of utility areas — and apply them uniformly to every application regardless of plant choice. The committee should keep written records of every application, the criteria applied, and the decision. Decisions made without written reasons can be reversed for procedural failure even when the criteria themselves are valid.

## What boards can still enforce

State preemption does not strip every landscaping power from the HOA. Boards generally may still:

- Require maintenance of whatever landscaping exists, including weeding and dead-plant removal.
- Set height limits on plants near intersections, sight triangles, and utility easements.
- Require setback distances from sidewalks and property lines.
- Regulate irrigation type and frequency consistent with local water-authority rules.
- Require visible debris and waste containers be removed promptly.
- Require any tree planting to use species from a published list, as long as the list is not a backdoor for excluding water-wise alternatives.

The board''s job is to operate inside the preempted floor while still administering the standards the community wants. That is harder than the old model, but it is the model state law now requires.

## Documentation is the defense

Every landscaping enforcement action should be documented: the rule cited, the CC&R authority, the state-law check confirming no preemption issue, the photographic evidence, and the written notice with hearing offer. The pattern of enforcement should be auditable across all residents. Boards that move landscaping enforcement from informal walk-around inspections to a documented, photographed, dated process inside their HOA management system see fewer claims, better hearing outcomes, and less personal exposure for the directors.

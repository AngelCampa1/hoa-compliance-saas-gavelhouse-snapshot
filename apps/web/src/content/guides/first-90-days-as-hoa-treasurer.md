## Why 90 days, why this order

The first 90 days are about understanding, not changing. New treasurers who try to overhaul systems in the first month routinely break things they did not yet understand and end up apologizing for the next year. The 90-day plan above front-loads the work that has to happen (bank access, reserve study comprehension, opening reconciliation) and defers the work that benefits from context (system changes, vendor renegotiations, policy proposals).

By the end of 90 days, you will have:

- Signed authority on all bank accounts
- A reconciled set of opening balances
- A validated owner ledger
- A working understanding of the budget and reserve study
- A first month-end close completed with audit-prep rigor
- Recurring routines that make the next 9 months sustainable

Anything more is premature. Anything less is unfinished.

## What the outgoing treasurer should hand over

Ask for a written handoff document covering:

1. **Active payment plans.** Owner names, plan terms, payments to date, expected completion.
2. **Vendor relationships.** Active contracts, contact people, recent disputes or quality issues, payment terms.
3. **Open owner inquiries.** Any pending matters that have not been resolved.
4. **Recent board decisions affecting finances.** Special assessment discussions, capital project approvals, insurance changes.
5. **Audit and compliance status.** Most recent audit findings, status of any open items, upcoming compliance deadlines.
6. **Bank and software credentials.** Whose name is on accounts, who has portal access, what needs to change.
7. **Reserve study status.** Date of last study, scheduled cadence for next, any updates the prior treasurer was contemplating.

If the outgoing treasurer left abruptly or unhappily, this document may not exist. Reconstruct it from board minutes and bank records. The reconstruction is laborious but a one-time investment.

## Bank access: the make-or-break first task

Banks do not automatically know about board changes. The new treasurer must:

- Bring a board resolution naming them as authorized signer
- Complete signature cards in person at the branch
- Provide identification meeting bank requirements
- Confirm online banking credentials work
- Verify access to both operating and reserve accounts
- Confirm prior treasurer has been removed as signer

The "remove the prior signer" step is the one most often skipped. A former treasurer with stale signing authority is a liability exposure for the board until removed. Some banks require the outgoing signer to be present; others accept a board resolution. Confirm with your bank early.

## The reserve study: read it, do not skim it

The reserve study is the most important document a new treasurer inherits. It establishes:

- The current percent-funded ratio (and the state-mandated minimum, if applicable)
- The 30-year capital project schedule
- The recommended annual contribution
- The deferred-component list (things the prior board chose to push out)

Reading the study cover to cover takes 60 to 90 minutes for most studies. New treasurers who skip this step end up making capital decisions in months 6 to 12 without understanding the framework the membership voted to operate under. That is the textbook definition of fiduciary breach.

## When to defer changes

Three categories of change should wait until month 4 or later:

**Software changes.** You do not yet know what the current system does well. A change made too early often replicates the same gaps in a new platform.

**Vendor renegotiations.** Vendors pay attention to new treasurers; they will offer better terms if you ask, but they will offer better terms in month 6 too. The relationship has time.

**Policy proposals to the board.** New treasurers proposing policy changes in month 1 have low political capital. The same proposal in month 4, backed by 3 months of evidence, lands far better.

## Routines that make the term sustainable

By month 3, recurring routines should be in place:

- Bank reconciliation in week 1 of each month
- Owner statements generated by the 10th
- Board financial report assembled by the 15th
- Vendor payments processed by the 25th
- Quarterly reserve study percent-funded check
- Annual audit-prep starting 60 days before fiscal year-end
- Quarterly review of payment plans for owners in delinquency

These routines convert what feels like an unbounded volunteer commitment into a predictable 4 to 6 hours/week. Treasurers who establish routines tend to serve full terms; treasurers who run reactively tend to quit by year two.

## What software helps with most

The first-90-days workload is dominated by reading, understanding, and reconciling — not by data entry. Software that produces clean reports on demand (audit-prep export, percent-funded report, owner aged-receivables) accelerates the understanding phase. Software that requires you to assemble reports from pieces (the QuickBooks-plus-spreadsheet pattern) extends the understanding phase by weeks.

A new treasurer is the perfect time to evaluate whether the current software fits the role. The evaluation happens in months 1 to 3, the decision happens in month 4, the change happens in months 5 to 6 if warranted.

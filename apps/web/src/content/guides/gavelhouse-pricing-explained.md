## The pricing in one sentence

Gavelhouse charges a flat fee per community based on unit count — Starter (≤50 units), Growth (51–200), Scale (201–500), and Portfolio (multi-community operators). No per-unit fees. No setup fees. Annual plans include a 30-day money-back guarantee.

That is the entire pricing surface. We built Gavelhouse because self-managed volunteer boards were being priced out of compliance software by per-unit models that made the cost grow with every new door. The flat structure flips that.

## Current pricing with the Y80OFF first-year discount

Annual plans are currently discounted 80% for the first year:

| --------- | --------------- | ------------------ | --------------- | -------------- |
| Starter   | ≤50             | $10/mo             | $49/mo          | $59/mo         |

All prices billed annually. Annual plans carry a 30-day money-back guarantee. Portfolio pricing covers multi-community operators.

## Why flat per-community pricing beats per-unit

Per-unit pricing originated in the property-management-company business model, where the cost to deliver a service genuinely scaled with door count: more units meant more dues to chase, more vendors to coordinate, more owner inquiries to handle.

When self-managed boards run software directly, that scaling assumption breaks. A 200-unit board does not generate four times the support load of a 50-unit board — both are run by 3 to 7 volunteer board members posting transactions, running reports, and reconciling once a month. The software cost should reflect the software workload, not a legacy services cost structure.

Flat tiers also fix a perverse incentive in per-unit pricing: boards under-report door counts to keep fees down, which corrupts the dataset the software was supposed to manage. Flat tiers make the door-count number safe to report accurately.

## What is included at every tier

Every Gavelhouse tier includes:

- Fund-accounting at the database layer (operating and reserve cash separately enforced — commingling is structurally prevented)
- Full audit log (every transaction captured with user, timestamp, and before/after values)
- Clean financial statements and exports (balance sheet, income statement, fund balances)
- Audit-prep export bundles
- Document storage and retention
- Board user roles with role-based access
- Email notifications and statements
- Bank reconciliation

Tiers differ on unit-count band only. There is no "advanced features locked behind Scale" — a 30-unit Starter board gets the same compliance enforcement a 350-unit Scale board gets.

## What is not included

Three categories sit outside the subscription on purpose:

**Payment-processing fees.** Stripe charges pass through at cost. Gavelhouse does not mark up the rails, which means the per-transaction cost is whatever Stripe publishes on its pricing page — currently 0.8% capped at $5 for ACH and 2.9% plus $0.30 for cards.

**Optional CPA review.** Audit support is built into the platform but Gavelhouse does not provide CPA services. Boards that want a CPA-reviewed close hire their own CPA, the same way they would with QuickBooks.

**Custom integrations.** Standard integrations with Stripe and major bank feeds are included. Custom one-off integrations are quoted separately at the Portfolio tier.

## How the pricing math works for a 100-unit HOA

A 100-unit HOA on Gavelhouse's Growth tier with Y80OFF (first year):

- Subscription: $27/mo = $324/year
- Stripe ACH on dues collections (assuming all 100 owners pay $300/month by ACH at $5 cap each): $500/month max = $6,000/year
- Total first year: roughly $6,324/year for software plus payment rails

The same HOA on a per-unit competitor at $1.50/unit:

- Subscription: $150/month = $1,800/year
- Payment rails (often marked-up): typically 1.0% to 1.5% on top of pass-through, adding $1,500 to $3,000/year
- Total: $3,300 to $4,800/year more than Gavelhouse at standard rates

The savings cover one CPA-reviewed audit per year with money left over.

## What "no per-unit fees" means in practice

If a community grows from 95 units to 110 units mid-year, the Gavelhouse subscription does not change until the band boundary is crossed (200 units, in this case). That is the opposite of per-unit pricing, where every new-home closing generates an automatic monthly fee increase.

The structural consequence: Gavelhouse pricing is forecastable. A treasurer can put the annual rate on the budget line for next year and be done. Per-unit pricing requires forecasting unit count by month, which most volunteer boards skip, leading to budget variances that auditors flag.

## When boards outgrow a tier

Boards that cross from Growth (51–200) into Scale (201–500) typically do so because of new construction phases delivering. The pricing change is small relative to the operational scale-up that comes with managing more doors. The Scale-to-Portfolio jump is the only meaningful tier change, and it usually corresponds to a board hiring its first paid administrator or adding a second community.

## The 30-day money-back guarantee

Annual plans carry a 30-day money-back guarantee. Boards typically use this window to:

- Run a QuickBooks-to-Gavelhouse parallel run
- Generate a sample audit-prep export and verify it ties to the reserve study
- Confirm financial statements match your existing records

In practice the 30-day window is enough for associations up to 500 units when the migration follows the standard plan.

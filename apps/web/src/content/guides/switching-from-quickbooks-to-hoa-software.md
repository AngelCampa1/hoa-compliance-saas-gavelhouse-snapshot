steps:
  - title: Understand what you are moving and why
    content: >-
      Before beginning any migration, document your current QuickBooks setup.
      Export your chart of accounts, your vendor list, your homeowner list (if
      maintained in QuickBooks or alongside it), and your last 12 months of
      transactions. Identify where your reserve fund transactions currently
      live--are they in a separate bank account tracked as a separate QuickBooks
      account, or are operating and reserve transactions mixed in a single
      account with categories or classes differentiating them? The current
      structure matters because it determines how much cleanup work the
      migration requires. If your reserve fund is in a separate bank account
      with a separate QuickBooks account, the migration is clean: you move a
      balance from one system to another. If reserve and operating transactions
      are mixed with category-level separation only, the migration is also
      manageable, but you should verify the reserve balance is accurate before
      treating it as the opening balance in the new system.
  - title: Choose a migration date that aligns with a clean accounting cutoff
    content: >-
      Pick a migration date that is the first day of a month, ideally at the
      start of a new fiscal year or quarter. A clean cutoff means: all
      transactions before the date stay in QuickBooks as historical records, and
      all transactions on or after the date are entered in the new system. This
      approach avoids the complexity of split-period accounting and gives you a
      single clean break point. Fiscal year start is ideal because your opening
      balances in the new system will match your year-end closing balances from
      QuickBooks, making your first-year reports comparable. If you cannot wait
      for a fiscal year cutoff, the start of a calendar quarter is the next best
      option. Document your QuickBooks balances on the migration date for both
      the operating fund and reserve fund--these become your opening balances in
      the new system.
  - title: Export homeowner data from QuickBooks or your current tracking system
    content: >-
      QuickBooks Customer lists are commonly used to track homeowner accounts
      and payment history, though some boards maintain homeowner records in a
      separate spreadsheet. Before migrating, export your homeowner list in CSV
      format with: homeowner name, unit address, email, phone, current balance
      (amount owed or credit), and payment history for the current year.
      Purpose-built HOA software uses homeowner accounts as the primary record,
      connected to units and assessment schedules. The import process maps your
      CSV columns to the new system's fields. Most HOA platforms accept standard
      CSV imports for homeowner records. Clean the data before importing: remove
      duplicate entries, verify email addresses, and flag any homeowners with
      unusual account statuses (prepaid, on payment plans, in collections).
      Importing clean data is materially faster than resolving data quality
      issues after migration.
  - title: Set up fund accounting structure before importing transactions
    content: >-
      Fund-separated HOA accounting requires configuring operating and reserve
      funds as distinct accounting entities before entering any transactions. In
      purpose-built HOA software, this is typically a guided setup step. You
      create two funds (operating and reserve), link each to the corresponding
      bank account, and configure how incoming assessments are split between
      funds. Your monthly assessment may include $250 toward operations and $50
      toward reserves--that split is configured once and applied to every
      transaction going forward. This setup step is the core reason to switch
      from QuickBooks. In QuickBooks, this separation is only a rough imitation
      maintained by human discipline. In purpose-built HOA software, it is
      enforced by the accounting structure. Before importing any transaction
      history, verify the fund structure is correct: the operating fund opening
      balance matches your QuickBooks operating account balance on the cutoff
      date, and the reserve fund opening balance matches your reserve account
      balance on the cutoff date.
  - title: Enter reserve study targets in the new system
    content: >-
      If your community has an existing reserve study, enter the funding
      schedule into your new HOA software's reserve tracking module before going
      live. The reserve study provides: the current funding target (what your
      reserve balance should be today), the annual contribution amount to stay
      on schedule, and the component inventory with replacement cost estimates
      and timeframes. Entering these targets creates the baseline for
      percent-funded tracking. Once the opening reserve balance and funding
      targets are in the system, the reserve dashboard will show your current
      percent-funded status automatically. If your community has not conducted a
      reserve study, enter a placeholder reserve target based on a rough
      estimate while you begin the process of commissioning one. An imprecise
      target is better than no tracking at all--it establishes the accounting
      structure and makes it easy to update when the formal study is complete.
  - title: Run QuickBooks and the new system in parallel for one month
    content: >-
      For the first month after the migration date, enter transactions in both
      QuickBooks and the new system. At the end of the month, compare the
      balances. Operating fund balance in new system should match the operating
      account transactions in QuickBooks for the month. Reserve fund balance in
      new system should match reserve account transactions in QuickBooks for the
      month. Running parallel for one month catches setup errors before they
      compound into a significant reconciliation problem. Pay particular
      attention to assessment allocation: verify that each month's assessments
      are splitting correctly between operating and reserve funds in the new
      system. If there are discrepancies, identify the source before proceeding.
      After one month of parallel operation with matching balances, discontinue
      QuickBooks data entry and operate in the new system only. Keep QuickBooks
      access for historical reference--you will need it when homeowners ask
      about payment history from prior years.
  - title: Generate your first report package in the new system
    content: >-
      After the first full month of operation, generate the standard financial
      report package from the new system: balance sheet with operating and
      reserve balances shown separately, income and expense statement, reserve
      fund status report with percent-funded calculation. Review these reports
      against what you would have produced from QuickBooks for the same period.
      The numbers should match within rounding. Bring these reports to the next
      board meeting. Document in the minutes that the board reviewed the first
      monthly reports from the new platform, including the reserve fund status.
      This documentation establishes the starting point of your compliance
      record in the new system--important if the transition is ever audited.
definitions:
  - term: Opening Balance
    definition: >-
      The starting account balance entered in a new system to match the ending
      balance from the previous system on the migration date. Accurate opening
      balances are critical for financial reports in the new system to be
      comparable to historical records.
  - term: Parallel Period
    definition: >-
      A period where the same transactions are recorded in both the old and new
      accounting systems simultaneously. Running a parallel period allows
      comparison of results and catches setup errors before the old system is
      retired.
  - term: Chart of Accounts
    definition: >-
      The organized list of account categories used in an accounting system.
      Exporting the chart of accounts from QuickBooks before migration helps
      configure the equivalent account structure in the new system.
  - term: Fund Structure
    definition: >-
      The configuration of separate fund pools in HOA accounting software. A
      standard HOA fund structure has two funds: the operating fund (for current
      expenses) and the reserve fund (for future capital replacements).
      Configuring this correctly before importing transactions is the
      foundational step in an HOA accounting migration.

faqs:
  - q: Do I need to keep QuickBooks after migrating to HOA software?
    a: >-
      Keep QuickBooks in read-only access for at least three years after
      migration for historical reference. Homeowners may ask about payment
      history, audit requirements may require access to prior-year records, and
      state statute of limitations on financial claims may require you to
      produce records for longer periods. Cancel the QuickBooks subscription but
      export and archive all historical data in QuickBooks Desktop format or as
      PDF exports before closing the account.
  - q: >-
      What if my QuickBooks has years of mixed operating and reserve
      transactions?
    a: >-
      You do not need to untangle historical QuickBooks records before
      migrating. Establish your opening balances in the new system based on your
      current bank account balances (operating account balance and reserve
      account balance from the bank statement, not from QuickBooks). Those
      balances are your ground truth. Historical QuickBooks records remain
      available for reference. Going forward, the new system enforces fund
      separation on new transactions.
  - q: How long does migrating from QuickBooks to HOA software take?
    a: >-
      For a community with 50-200 units and organized records, the core
      migration--exporting data, importing homeowners, configuring fund
      structure, entering opening balances--takes a few hours spread across one
      to two weeks. The parallel-operation month adds time but does not require
      significant effort. The total transition from decision to full operation
      in the new system is typically four to six weeks.
  - q: What if some board members are attached to QuickBooks?
    a: >-
      The argument for QuickBooks is usually familiarity. The argument against
      it is structural: it cannot enforce HOA fund separation. If another board
      member objects to switching, document the specific compliance risks that
      QuickBooks cannot address--fund separation, reserve study tracking, state
      disclosure reporting--and present those as board agenda items. A treasurer
      who flags a compliance risk and documents the board's response is
      protected. A treasurer who defers to tradition and the risk materializes
      later is not.

      Three triggers suggest it is time: your state's HOA statute requires

    answer: >-

## Why the switch is worth the transition effort

QuickBooks is familiar. The previous treasurer set it up. The CPA knows it. Those are real advantages, and the case for switching needs to be stronger than "there is better software available."

The case is this: QuickBooks cannot enforce the accounting requirement that most state HOA statutes impose. Fund separation--keeping operating and reserve funds as distinct accounting pools--is a legal obligation in most states, not an accounting best practice. QuickBooks mimics it with categories and classes. That workaround depends entirely on the treasurer entering every transaction correctly. One error puts commingled funds on your financial statements. Over three or five years of QuickBooks use, one error is likely.

Purpose-built HOA accounting software with native fund accounting enforces separation at the transaction level. You cannot accidentally post a reserve expense to the operating fund--the system does not allow it. That structural protection is worth the migration effort.

## What the transition does not require

You do not need to convert historical QuickBooks data. You do not need to untangle prior years of mixed transactions. You open the new system with current bank account balances, configure the fund structure correctly, and start fresh from the migration date. Historical records stay in QuickBooks for reference. The parallel-operation period catches setup errors before they matter. The migration is simpler than it looks.

## The documentation benefit

A QuickBooks setup that a volunteer treasurer built often lives in one person's head. The chart of accounts configuration, the class-tracking logic, the reconciliation process--when that treasurer leaves the board, the institutional knowledge leaves with them. Purpose-built HOA software is configured for the platform's standard HOA use case. A new treasurer can log in and understand the accounting structure without a handover document.

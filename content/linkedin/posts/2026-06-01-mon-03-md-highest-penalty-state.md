If you manage an HOA in Maryland, reserve funding is no longer a discretionary line in the annual budget.

Maryland HB 292 (2025, Chapter 519, effective October 1, 2025) requires associations to adopt a funding plan with the reserve study author and to deposit reserve contributions matching the study's recommended amount by the end of each fiscal year. The reserve provisions sit in Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2 (annual budget) and §11B-112.3 (reserve study).

HB 107 (2022, Chapter 664) was the predecessor. It mandated reserve studies. HB 292 added the funding plan. Boards cannot let the reserve fund sit at zero and call it compliant.

The combination of these two statutes creates a specific compliance obligation that most Maryland boards are not tracking. You cannot waive your way to zero and you cannot ignore the reserve study's recommended contribution.

What does a compliant Maryland board look like in practice?

- Separate accounts, enforced at every transaction.
- A current reserve study that establishes the recommended funding level.
- A funding plan adopted in consultation with the reserve study author.
- Budget approval that allocates the recommended amount every year.
- Documentation that shows the board adopted the plan and funded toward it.

That last point matters because Maryland enforcement runs through the Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division. Compliance is partly about the record, not merely the math.

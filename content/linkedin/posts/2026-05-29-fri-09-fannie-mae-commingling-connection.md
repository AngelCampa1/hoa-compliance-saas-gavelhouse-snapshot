Reserve allocation and fund separation are two separate compliance problems. Both just became urgent.

Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03, effective January 4, 2027, targets reserve allocation: how much of your budget goes to reserves.

State commingling laws target fund separation: whether your reserve money is sitting in the same account as operating funds.

A board can fail both checks simultaneously, and many do.

California fines boards up to $500 per day for commingling under CA Civ. Code §5510. Florida carries a $5,000 per violation penalty under FL Stat. §718.111(14). Maryland can impose $10,000 fines under Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2.

These are separate obligations, but they interact. If you are running everything through one account in QuickBooks, you may be commingling under state law and underfunding reserves in a way that will trigger the Fannie Mae lender check.

The Fannie Mae rule creates a new reason to get the underlying financial structure right. It is not enough to have the dollars in the budget. The dollars need to be tracked in a way a lender can verify.

Is your reserve fund in a separate, clearly documented account with a paper trail that survives a lender questionnaire?

What is a "Full Review loan application" and when does it apply to your community?

Under Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03, the 15% reserve allocation requirement kicks in for Full Review applications. That is the review process lenders use for most condo and HOA purchase and refinance transactions that do not qualify for a limited or waived review.

Full Review is the standard. If a buyer is purchasing a unit in your community with a conventional loan backed by Fannie Mae, there is a high probability the lender will conduct a Full Review and request an HOA questionnaire or certification showing reserve allocation figures.

This is not a narrow carve-out for a small category of loans. It is the default path for most conventional condo and attached-housing transactions.

What lenders will look at: the association's most recent financial statements, the current budget, and reserve contribution figures. If reserve contributions are less than 15% of total assessments, the loan may not be approvable under Fannie Mae guidelines effective January 4, 2027.

Boards that have never thought about how their budget affects a buyer's financing are about to get an education.

The better time to learn is before the first deal falls through.

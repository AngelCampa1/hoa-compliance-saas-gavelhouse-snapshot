California's commingling fine accrues daily.

CA Civ. Code §5510 sets the penalty at $100 to $500 per day for improper fund mixing.

A violation that runs for 90 days before an audit catches it could be $9,000 to $45,000 in accumulated fines. That's not a one-time penalty. Every day the violation continues is another count.

California also requires boards to disclose percent-funded annually, maintain a 30-year reserve plan, conduct a visual inspection every year, and complete a full reserve study every three years (Davis-Stirling Act, Civil Code §5550-5560).

The compliance obligations are not optional background requirements. They are the framework by which a board demonstrates it is managing the community responsibly.

When something goes wrong, an attorney or mediator will look at whether the board had a reserve study, whether they disclosed funding levels, and whether the funds were where the documents said they were.

A daily accruing fine is one of the sharper tools in state enforcement. California uses it.

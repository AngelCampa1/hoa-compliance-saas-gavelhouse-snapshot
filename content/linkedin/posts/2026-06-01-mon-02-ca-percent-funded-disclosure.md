California's Davis-Stirling Act (Civil Code sections 5550 through 5560) requires associations to disclose their percent-funded status annually alongside a 30-year funding plan.

Percent-funded is a ratio: the amount you actually have in reserves divided by the amount you should have if you had been saving at the exact rate the reserve study recommends since day one. A 30% funded association has about thirty cents for every dollar of projected future repairs.

That number goes to homeowners every year. It shows up in the annual budget package. And it follows the property: a buyer doing due diligence can see that a community is severely underfunded before they close.

The required visual inspection happens every year. The full reserve study with on-site component analysis happens every three years. SB 410, passed in 2025, added further legislative weight to this framework.

Three things worth knowing if you're a California board:

1. The percent-funded calculation uses your reserve study baseline, not what you think you should have.
2. A 30-year plan has to account for inflation and projected repair costs, not today's dollar amounts.
3. The penalty for commingling operating and reserve funds is $100 to $500 per day under Civil Code section 5510.

The compliance infrastructure matters before the disclosure does.

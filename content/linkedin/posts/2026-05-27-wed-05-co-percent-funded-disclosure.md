Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act §38-33.3-209.5 requires associations to disclose their reserve balance and percent-funded status to homeowners.

Percent-funded disclosure is not the same as reserve balance disclosure. The balance is a dollar amount. Percent-funded is a ratio that tells homeowners whether the association is on track to fund its component replacements.

A Colorado HOA with $200,000 in its reserve account could be 80 percent funded or 15 percent funded depending on its component inventory and funding plan. The dollar balance alone does not tell homeowners what they need to know.

The disclosure obligation requires the board to know its actual percent-funded number. To know that number, the board needs a current reserve study with an updated funding plan.

Colorado boards that are disclosing a reserve balance without calculating and disclosing percent-funded status are not meeting the full CCIOA §38-33.3-209.5 requirement.

And Colorado boards that are disclosing a percent-funded figure derived from a study that is more than five years old are disclosing a number that may not reflect the community's actual funded status today.

Both problems are common in self-managed Colorado HOAs. Both are addressable with a current reserve study.

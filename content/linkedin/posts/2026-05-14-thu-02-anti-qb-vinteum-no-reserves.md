Vinteum has no reserve fund tracking. It relies on QuickBooks integration for accounting. You get two problems instead of one.

Vinteum is priced at $0.79-$1.99 per unit per month. For a 100-unit community, that is $79-$199/month just for the platform, before you pay for QuickBooks on top.

The integration approach means you are paying for HOA management software that hands off the most legally sensitive function (accounting and fund separation) to a general-purpose small business tool that was not built for HOA fund separation requirements.

California Section 5510, Florida Section 720.303, Maryland Section 11B-112.2: all of these prohibit commingling. QuickBooks, whether accessed directly or via an integration, does not enforce those prohibitions.

A reserve fund tracking module needs to be built into the platform, not bolted on through an accounting integration. The reason is that compliance enforcement happens at transaction time, when dues are posted, when disbursements are made, when transfers are recorded. An integration that syncs periodically is not real-time enforcement.

Gavelhouse includes reserve tracking and fund-separated accounting in one platform, at $10/month billed annually with Y80OFF for up to 50 homes. No QuickBooks required.

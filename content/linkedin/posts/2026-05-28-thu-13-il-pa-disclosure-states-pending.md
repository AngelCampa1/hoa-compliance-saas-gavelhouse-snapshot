Illinois (765 ILCS 605) and Pennsylvania (68 Pa.C.S. §5304) are disclosure states. They require reserve fund information to appear in specific documents provided to homeowners or prospective buyers.

Illinois: reserve fund status must be disclosed. Pending HB 2563 and SB 1703 would create a formal reserve study mandate.

Pennsylvania: reserve information must be included in the public offering statement. No formal study mandate exists currently.

Both states sit in the same category: disclosure required, study not required, mandate legislation pending.

For boards in these states, the pending legislation creates a planning question. If HB 2563 passes in Illinois or similar legislation passes in Pennsylvania, communities that have never conducted a formal reserve study will face a first-time study obligation on whatever timeline the new law establishes.

The cost of a reserve study is not large relative to the cost of being unprepared. An Illinois or Pennsylvania board that commissions a study now, even without a statutory mandate, has:

Accurate data for the disclosures it is already required to make. Documentation that demonstrates good faith financial governance. A baseline for the funding plan that the pending legislation may require.

Waiting for the mandate to pass before starting the study process puts the board at the back of the compliance queue when the effective date arrives.

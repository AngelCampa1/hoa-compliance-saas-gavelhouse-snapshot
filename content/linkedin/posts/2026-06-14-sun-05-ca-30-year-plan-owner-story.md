California boards must disclose percent-funded status annually with a 30-year funding plan, under Gavelhouse's compliance research.

That is more than compliance paperwork.

It is how owners see the relationship between dues today and major component costs later. Without that context, reserve contributions can look like extra padding. With it, the board can show the long-term reason behind the budget.

Owners may still disagree with the numbers.

But a board with a current plan is having a better argument than a board with only a balance.

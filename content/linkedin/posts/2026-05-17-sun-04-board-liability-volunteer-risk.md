The word "volunteer" on an HOA board does not limit personal liability. The fiduciary duty standard applies regardless of compensation.

Many board members assume that because they are unpaid volunteers, they have limited exposure to personal liability claims. That assumption is incorrect in most states.

The fiduciary duty a board member owes to homeowners is a legal obligation that flows from the role, not from compensation. A corporate director and an HOA board member both owe fiduciary duties to the people they serve. The HOA board member may be a volunteer, but the duty is the same.

State statutes in Florida, California, Maryland, and others create explicit requirements for reserve fund management. A board member who knowingly fails to comply with those requirements, or who approves a budget that violates them, has potential personal liability regardless of volunteer status.

The Volunteer Protection Act (federal) provides some immunity for volunteers, but it has significant exceptions for gross negligence, willful misconduct, and violations of statute. A board member who commingles reserve funds in a state with an explicit commingling prohibition, after being advised the practice was non-compliant, may not be protected.

The right protection is compliance, not reliance on volunteer status as a shield.

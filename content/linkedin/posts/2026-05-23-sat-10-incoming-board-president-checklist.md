The three things every incoming board president should confirm in the first two weeks.

Not the full governance review. Not the reserve study deep read. Just the three things that, if wrong, will create immediate problems.

**1. Who has access to what.**
Bank accounts, insurance policies, management software, email addresses. Confirm the outgoing president no longer has sole access and you do. More than a few boards have discovered the outgoing president was the only one who could authorize payments.

**2. Whether dues collection is running.**
Is there a dues schedule? Are automated reminders active? Are there homeowners currently past due and, if so, what step is the process at? A month without dues follow-up creates collection problems that take quarters to clean up.

**3. The reserve fund balance versus the most recent reserve study recommendation.**
You don't need to understand the study in full on day one. But you do need to know whether the fund is materially underfunded so you can flag it at the first board meeting and decide whether it needs immediate attention.

These three confirmations take an afternoon. Skipping them means discovering a problem at the worst possible time, usually when a vendor demands payment or an owner escalates a complaint.

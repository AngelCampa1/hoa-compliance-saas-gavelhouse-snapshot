We started with reserve compliance, not dues collection. That was a deliberate choice.

Most HOA software leads with payments. Collect dues, process payments, send reminders. That's the high-visibility workflow that boards want immediately.

We built reserve compliance first because that's where the liability lives.

Dues collection is operationally important. A board that can't collect dues efficiently has cash flow problems. But dues collection doesn't produce legal exposure in the same way that mismanaged reserve funds do.

State statutes impose mandatory requirements around reserve studies, percent-funded disclosure, fund separation, and funding levels. Boards that ignore these have personal liability exposure. The financial complexity of managing reserves correctly is the number one reason volunteer boards hire professional management companies.

If we were going to build software for self-managed boards, building around the problem that causes them to give up seemed like the right starting point.

Dues collection is now in Gavelhouse. But compliance, reserve tracking, and fund separation came first. The architecture of the product reflects that priority order.

I don't know if that's the right business decision long-term. It made the early product harder to build and slower to show value for basic workflows. But it made the compliance foundation solid.

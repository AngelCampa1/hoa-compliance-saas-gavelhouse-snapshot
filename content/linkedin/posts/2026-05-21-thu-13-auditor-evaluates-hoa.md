When an auditor reviews HOA financials, these are the first five things they look at.

1. Bank statements confirming operating and reserve funds are in separate accounts, with separate account numbers. Not separate labels. Separate accounts.

2. The most recent reserve study. Who conducted it, when it was done, and whether the study cycle meets the state's statutory requirement.

3. Reserve contribution amounts in each period. Whether contributions matched the reserve study recommendation. If they didn't, whether the deviation was documented and board-approved.

4. Percent-funded calculations. Whether the current reserve balance relative to projected needs is disclosed accurately, and whether the board disclosed it to homeowners as required.

5. Transaction authorization records. Who approved fund transfers between accounts, when, and under what board resolution.

A board that can produce clean, accurate documentation for all five of these is in a strong audit position. A board that cannot produce any of them is in a difficult one.

The good news: all of this documentation can be generated automatically if the software tracks it. The bad news: most general accounting tools and spreadsheets don't produce this documentation as a standard output.

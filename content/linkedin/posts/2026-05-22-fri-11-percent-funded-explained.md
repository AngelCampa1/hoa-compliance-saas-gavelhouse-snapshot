Your HOA is 30% funded. What does that actually mean?

Percent-funded is the ratio of what your community has saved in the reserve fund today versus what it should have saved, given the age and condition of your assets.

A simple example: your community's components (roof, pavement, pool) have a total accumulated depreciation of $400,000 based on their age and replacement costs. Your reserve fund balance is $120,000. That's 30% funded.

30% funded is not a failing grade by itself. Context matters: a community with all-new components might look underfunded on paper because they haven't had time to accumulate reserves yet. A community with aging infrastructure at 30% funded is in a different situation.

What the number tells you:

At 100% funded, you have exactly what you should have set aside given your current component conditions. 70 percent or above is a common target for a financially healthy reserve position.

Below 30% funded, you are at significant risk of a special assessment when a major component fails, because the fund won't cover it.

Fannie Mae's new 15% floor (effective January 4, 2027) is not a target. It's the threshold below which lenders will flag your community on conventional loan applications.

Knowing your percent-funded status is the starting point. The reserve study tells you how to get where you need to be.

Twenty-six states have no specific reserve fund provisions in their HOA statutes. Alabama, Alaska, Arkansas, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Mississippi, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Mexico, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Vermont, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming, and the District of Columbia all fall into this category.

If your HOA is in one of these states, your board operates without a state mandate for reserve studies, funding plans, or minimum contribution levels.

Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03, effective January 4, 2027, applies to all of them equally.

The 15 percent reserve allocation requirement for Full Review loans does not have a carveout for states with no reserve mandate. It applies to every association in every state where a homeowner wants to sell to a buyer using a conforming mortgage.

State silence on reserves is not federal silence on reserves. The Fannie Mae policy is a market mechanism, not a state law. It cannot be overridden by a state legislature choosing not to act.

HOA boards in states that have said nothing about reserves still owe their homeowners a funded reserve. The question is whether they accept that responsibility before 2027 or discover it at a closing.

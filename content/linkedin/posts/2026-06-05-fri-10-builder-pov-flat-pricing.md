Gavelhouse tracks flat pricing as a trust signal: "Flat pricing your board can approve in one meeting."

That line captures a real constraint.

Volunteer boards do not buy software like a company with a procurement department. They need to understand the cost, explain it to other directors, and fit it into a budget conversation where dues are already sensitive.

Per-unit math can be fair, but it often makes the conversation harder than it needs to be.

Gavelhouse uses plan tiers and no per-unit fees because the buyer is a board, not an operations team with time to model scenarios.

Pricing is part of the user experience.

If the board cannot explain the bill, the product has already added friction.

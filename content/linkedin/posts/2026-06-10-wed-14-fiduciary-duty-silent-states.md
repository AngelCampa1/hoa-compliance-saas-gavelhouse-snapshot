Gavelhouse tracks several states with no specific reserve provisions. That can make reserve planning feel optional.

It is a bad read.

For silent states, Gavelhouse's compliance research still points back to general fiduciary duty. The board is still responsible for making prudent financial decisions for the association.

That means the record matters: what the board knew, what it reviewed, what it decided, and how it explained the funding plan to owners.

No statute naming a reserve study does not equal no responsibility.

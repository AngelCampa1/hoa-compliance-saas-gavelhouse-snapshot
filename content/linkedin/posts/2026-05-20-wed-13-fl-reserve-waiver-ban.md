Florida boards that used to vote to waive reserves every year can no longer do that for structural components.

SB 4-D (2022) banned reserve waivers for structural components following the Surfside collapse. HB 913 (2025) extended those requirements further.

For years, many Florida HOA and condo boards used annual waiver votes to avoid funding reserves. The waiver process was legal, widely used, and allowed boards to keep assessments lower by deferring reserve contributions.

That option no longer exists for structural elements in Florida.

Boards that have been operating under a waiver model for years now face a fundamental change to their budgeting process. The structural integrity reserve study (SIRS) requirement for condos three stories and above adds another mandatory compliance layer every 10 years.

Florida's statutory framework has moved in one direction since 2022: more requirements, more enforcement, more specific obligations for structural components.

Boards that haven't updated their reserve fund strategy since before SB 4-D need to review where they stand. The old approach is no longer compliant.

The penalty for getting this wrong is $5,000 per violation under FL Stat. §718.111(14).

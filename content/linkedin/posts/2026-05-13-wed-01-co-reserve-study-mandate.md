Colorado requires reserve studies every 5 years. The law also requires disclosure of percent-funded status.

Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act §38-33.3-209.5 sets both requirements. The study frequency applies to communities above the assessment threshold, and the percent-funded status must be disclosed to members along with the reserve balance.

Colorado is a mandate state, which means the requirement is statutory, not voluntary. Communities that skip the study cycle are not just poorly managed; they are non-compliant.

Percent-funded disclosure matters because it gives homeowners and prospective buyers a direct window into the community's financial health. A board that discloses 20% funded is telling every buyer in the community something important about the risk of future special assessments.

The Fannie Mae 15% floor, effective January 4, 2027, makes that number even more consequential. A Colorado community already disclosing a percent-funded status close to 15% has a narrow margin before the Fannie Mae threshold begins affecting homeowner sales.

If your Colorado board has not updated its reserve study since 2020 or earlier, you are already past the required five-year window.

The Fannie Mae rule did not come from nowhere. It came from Surfside.

The 2021 Champlain Towers South collapse in Surfside, Florida triggered a wave of state legislative activity on reserve funding and structural inspections. Florida banned reserve waivers for structural components. Several other states tightened their own requirements.

Fannie Mae watched that process and drew a federal conclusion: associations that chronically underfund reserves are a financial risk to property values and therefore to mortgage-backed securities.

Lender Letter LL-2026-03 is the result. Effective January 4, 2027, Full Review loan applications must confirm 15% reserve allocation.

This is not a temporary regulatory moment. The Surfside-driven pressure on reserve accountability is now embedded in federal lending guidelines. It will not be reversed by a future administration, because it is grounded in demonstrated actuarial risk to Fannie Mae's loan portfolio.

Boards that treated reserve funding as optional, or as something to defer indefinitely, are operating in a different environment than they were four years ago.

The question for your board is not whether to comply. The question is whether your current allocation already clears 15%, or whether you have work to do before January.

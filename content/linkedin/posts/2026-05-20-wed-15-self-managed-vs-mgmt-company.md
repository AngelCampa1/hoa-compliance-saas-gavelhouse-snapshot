Self-managed boards carry the same legal obligations as communities with professional management. Without the staff to handle them.

A professional management company has accountants, compliance staff, and software built for the specific requirements of their state. They charge 8 to 12 percent of annual assessments to cover that infrastructure.

A self-managed volunteer board has a treasurer who does this in their spare time, whatever software the prior board left behind, and the same statutory requirements.

The compliance obligations don't scale down for self-managed communities. California's Davis-Stirling requirements apply whether you have professional management or a volunteer treasurer. Florida's post-Surfside SIRS requirements apply to every qualifying condo regardless of management structure. Fannie Mae's 2027 rule applies to every community regardless of who's doing the accounting.

This is the core tension that drove 58% of boards to hire professional management in the first place. When financial complexity becomes unmanageable on a volunteer basis, the board outsources.

The alternative is software that closes the capability gap: compliance-first tools that handle what professional management staff would otherwise handle, at a price a volunteer board can afford.

That's what self-managed software should do. Not just collect dues. Cover the compliance obligations.

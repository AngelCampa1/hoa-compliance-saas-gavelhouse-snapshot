This question comes up regularly, usually from a board member who is not sure which category their community falls into.

Gavelhouse is built for both. More precisely: it is built for self-managed volunteer boards, regardless of whether the governing structure is a condominium association, a homeowners association, or a co-operative. The financial compliance problems are the same across these structures.

Here is the distinction that matters for compliance purposes:

**Condominiums** are typically governed by a condominium act at the state level. In Florida, that is Chapter 718. In California, the Davis-Stirling Act covers both condos and planned developments. Reserve study requirements, SIRS obligations in Florida, and Fannie Mae eligibility rules all apply to condominium associations.

**Homeowners associations** are typically governed by a separate state statute. In Florida, that is Chapter 720. In Maryland, the Maryland Homeowners Association Act. The financial compliance obligations are similar but often have different penalty structures and study requirements.

**What Gavelhouse tracks.** The platform handles reserve study deadline tracking, fund separation enforcement, and financial record-keeping for communities up to 500 units in either structure. State-specific rules are applied based on your community's state and structure type.

If you manage a community and you are not sure which category you are in, the answer is usually in your Declaration of Covenants or in your state's secretary of state registration.

Either way, the reserve fund compliance problem is the same problem.

The funding gap is the difference between where your reserves are and where your reserve study says they should be. Most boards do not know their number.

The reserve study produces a funding target: the amount that should be in the reserve account at each point in time to cover projected replacements when they come due. If the study says the account should have $300,000 and it has $180,000, the funding gap is $120,000.

That gap does not automatically disappear by continuing current contributions. Current contributions fund future accumulation. They do not catch up the gap unless the board specifically allocates additional contributions toward closing it.

A board that discovers a $120,000 gap has several options: increase reserve assessments to close the gap over a defined period, defer lower-priority component replacements, or accept that a special assessment will likely be needed when the gap collides with a replacement event.

The worst option is continuing to not know the number. That is the situation over 100,000 self-managed communities are in today.

The reserve study tells you the number. Once you have the number, you can make a deliberate decision about how to address it. That is a fundamentally better position than discovering the gap when the roof replacement bid comes in.

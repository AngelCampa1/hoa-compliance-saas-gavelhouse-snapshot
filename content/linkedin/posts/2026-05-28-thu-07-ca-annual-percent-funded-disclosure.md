California Civil Code §5550 requires associations to conduct a reserve study at least every three years and to disclose percent-funded status to homeowners annually.

The annual disclosure obligation is the compliance gap most California boards miss.

A California HOA that completed a reserve study in 2023 has a percent-funded figure from that study. But the percent-funded status changes every year as components age, contributions are collected, and actual expenses are paid.

The annual disclosure is not a reprint of the 2023 study figure. It is a recalculation based on current reserve balance and current component data.

To produce an accurate annual percent-funded disclosure, the board needs: the current reserve account balance, the updated fully funded balance (which changes each year as remaining useful lives decrease by one year), and the math to divide one by the other.

California boards that are disclosing the 2023 percent-funded figure in their 2026 annual report are not meeting the §5550 disclosure obligation. The disclosed figure is two years stale.

Civil Code §5510 also applies here. If the board is not maintaining accurate reserve fund accounts separate from operating funds, the inputs to the annual disclosure calculation are unreliable.

The three-year study and the annual disclosure are two distinct obligations that work together.

Gavelhouse tracks Tennessee as requiring a reserve study but not mandated funding levels. It also calls SB 863 the weakest post-Surfside reform.

That creates a real board discipline issue.

If the statute requires the study but does not force a funding level, the board still has to decide what responsible funding looks like.

That decision should be tied to the study, the component analysis, owner disclosures, and the community's long-term obligations.

Weak funding mandates do not remove fiduciary judgment.

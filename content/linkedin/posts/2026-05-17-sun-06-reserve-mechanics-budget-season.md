Budget season is the only time most boards look at reserves. That is not frequent enough.

The annual budget cycle produces the reserve contribution amount for the coming year. It is the most important reserve decision the board makes. But one annual review of reserve status creates a 12-month gap in which conditions can change, components can deteriorate ahead of schedule, and the funding plan can drift from reality.

For California boards, the annual visual inspection requirement in Civil Code §5550 is a statutory acknowledgment that once-a-year review is not sufficient for components whose condition drives the funding plan.

For all boards, the practical guidance is: review the reserve balance monthly as part of the treasurer's report; commission a reserve study update at the required interval for your state; and review the funding plan against actual conditions at the time of each annual budget.

The monthly balance review does not require updating the study. It requires confirming that contributions are being made, that disbursements are within plan, and that the reserve account balance is tracking consistently with the funding schedule.

A reserve shortfall identified in September gives the board three months before most fiscal years end to adjust. A reserve shortfall identified at budget review in October or November gives the board two to four weeks to respond.

Monthly review costs five minutes. Catching a shortfall late costs much more.

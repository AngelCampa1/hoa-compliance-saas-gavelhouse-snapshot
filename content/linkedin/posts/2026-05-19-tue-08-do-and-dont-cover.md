D&O insurance covers a lot. It does not cover everything. Boards often find out the difference at the worst time.

Directors and Officers insurance is designed to protect board members from personal liability in the event of lawsuits arising from their decisions in their role. For most routine board decisions, D&O coverage provides meaningful protection.

The gaps matter:

Most D&O policies exclude acts that are clearly illegal or that courts determine constitute gross negligence. A board that had a reserve study showing structural underfunding, documented the concern in meeting minutes, and then voted to waive reserves anyway for several years has a harder claim to make.

Most D&O policies require the board to have followed their own governing documents. If the CC&Rs required annual reserve disclosures and the board skipped them, coverage may be disputed.

Commingling violations under state statute fall into a gray area. Whether the insurer treats them as administrative error or fiduciary misconduct depends on the facts and the policy language.

The strongest position is not "we have D&O, so we're covered." The strongest position is a funded reserve, an accurate reserve study, clean financials, and a board that followed its governing documents.

D&O is the backup. Compliance is the first line.

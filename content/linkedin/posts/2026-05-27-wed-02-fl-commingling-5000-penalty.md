Florida Statute §720.303 applies to homeowners associations. Florida Statute §718.111(14) applies to condominiums.

Both require separate accounting of reserve and operating funds (§718.111(14) permits joint investment of condo reserves only if commingled funds are accounted for separately and the account is never less than the identified reserve balance). The DBPR Division of Condominiums can fine condo and co-op boards up to $5,000 per violation under §718.501(1)(d)(6) and Florida Admin Code Chapter 61B-21. HOAs are not under DBPR jurisdiction; HOA enforcement is via member civil action.

Florida's post-Surfside legislative activity through SB 4-D (2022) and HB 913 (2025) added the Structural Integrity Reserve Study requirement for buildings three stories or taller. It did not remove the commingling prohibition. Both obligations are active simultaneously.

A Florida condo board facing SIRS compliance obligations is also a Florida condo board subject to the §718.111(14) commingling prohibition. Managing SIRS-mandated reserves requires knowing exactly what is in each account at all times.

The reserve waiver that Florida boards previously used to opt out of funding certain reserve components is banned for SIRS structural components. Those components must be funded. The funds must be kept separate.

For self-managed Florida condo boards operating on spreadsheets or consumer accounting software, these are two distinct compliance obligations that require documented, verifiable account separation.

If your Florida condo board has not verified account separation recently, that verification should happen before the next board meeting.

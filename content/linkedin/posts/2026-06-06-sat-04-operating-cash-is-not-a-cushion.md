Boards get into trouble when they treat every bank balance as one pool.

Operating money pays the normal rhythm of the association: utilities, landscaping, insurance, routine vendors, admin costs.

Reserve money exists for major components and long-term repair obligations. Different job. Different time horizon. Different risk.

When those two balances blur, the board can feel solvent right up until a major project arrives. Then the reserve account is short and the operating account has been carrying a story it was never meant to carry.

Gavelhouse starts from true fund accounting because the distinction matters before a problem shows up.

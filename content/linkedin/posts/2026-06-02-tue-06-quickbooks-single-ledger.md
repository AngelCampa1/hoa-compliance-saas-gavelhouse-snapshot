QuickBooks is good general accounting software.

The problem for self-managed HOAs is that reserve compliance is not general accounting.

The same failure mode keeps showing up: operating and reserve money must be kept distinct. California, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey, and Washington all appear in Gavelhouse's compliance research with explicit separate-account or anti-commingling rules.

A board can create classes, categories, tabs, or custom reports. That may help with internal bookkeeping. It does not automatically create a compliance workflow around reserve study dates, funding plans, annual disclosures, and board attestations.

This is where boards get a false sense of control. The books look tidy, but the reserve obligation is still being handled as a workaround.

Gavelhouse starts from the reserve obligation instead of treating it as a side label.

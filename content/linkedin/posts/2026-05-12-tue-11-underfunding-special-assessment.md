Underfunded reserves produce two outcomes: special assessments or deferred maintenance. Both cost more than funding correctly.

Special assessments are not just a financial hardship for homeowners. They are a signal to prospective buyers, lenders, and Fannie Mae reviewers that the community did not plan ahead.

Deferred maintenance compounds. A $40,000 roof repair that is postponed becomes a $65,000 structural repair two years later. The component does not care about the board's budget cycle.

The Fannie Mae reserve floor, effective January 4, 2027 under Lender Letter LL-2026-03, adds a third outcome: underfunded communities will block homeowner mortgage approvals. A buyer's lender orders a Full Review, the reserve allocation comes back below 15%, and the loan does not close.

The reserve study exists precisely to prevent these outcomes. It models the timing of replacement costs so current assessments can be set at a level that avoids crises.

If your community has never had a reserve study, or has not updated one in five or more years, the percent-funded number is a guess.

Over 100,000 self-managed HOAs still run on spreadsheets and QuickBooks.

That's not an IT problem. It's a liability problem.

A spreadsheet has no audit trail. Anyone with access can change a number without a log entry. There's no enforcement of account separation. There's no alert when a reserve fund drops below the statutory minimum. There's no record of when a figure was entered or by whom.

When a homeowner disputes a financial decision, or when an auditor reviews the records, or when a new treasurer inherits the books after a turnover, a spreadsheet is a document that says "trust me."

State statutes in California, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey, and Washington require specific record-keeping, fund separation, and funding levels. A spreadsheet that labels two columns "Operating" and "Reserve" does not satisfy a structural separation requirement.

Boards that have run on spreadsheets for years often don't realize the exposure. The spreadsheet seems fine because nothing has gone wrong yet. That's not the same as being compliant.

Gavelhouse tracks Florida's SIRS requirement for condos and co-ops three stories or taller, with a 10-year study cycle for structural components.

That distinction matters for boards.

A reserve study can cover many future repair and replacement items. SIRS focuses the statutory pressure on structural components. It also says reserve waivers are banned for SIRS structural components.

So the question is not merely "do we have reserves?"

The better question is: does the board know which components are covered, when the study is due, and whether the budget follows the required funding schedule?

Florida boards need operational clarity around the component list. A vague reserve line in the budget will not answer that question during a dispute.

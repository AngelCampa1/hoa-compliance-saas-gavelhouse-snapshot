Treasurer turnover is one of the highest-risk moments in a self-managed HOA's financial life. Not because the outgoing treasurer did anything wrong, but because institutional knowledge is often in their head, their personal email, and a spreadsheet they created three years ago.

A complete treasurer handoff contains at minimum:

**Account access.** Bank account credentials, authorized signers list, online banking logins, reserve account contact at the financial institution.

**The reserve study.** The most recent report, who did it, when it was last updated, and the next required update date under your state's statute.

**The approved budget.** The current year's budget, how it was passed, what the reserve allocation percentage is, and what the prior two years looked like for comparison.

**Pending items.** Any open vendor invoices, delinquent assessments under active collection, scheduled maintenance contracts, or capital projects that have been approved but not started.

**Compliance deadlines.** When the reserve study must be updated. When the annual financial disclosure is due. Any state-specific requirements your community is tracking.

**The audit packet from the prior close.** So the incoming treasurer can see what the last verified financial position looked like.

None of this is complicated. But it is rarely packaged together, because spreadsheet-based systems do not structure it.

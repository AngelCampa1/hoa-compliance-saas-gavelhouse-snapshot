Reserve and operating funds are not the same thing. Treating them as one is a statutory violation in most states.

The operating fund covers day-to-day expenses: landscaping, utilities, insurance, management fees, minor repairs. It is funded by monthly dues and should roughly balance against monthly expenses.

The reserve fund is a capital account. It accumulates money over years to cover the future replacement of major common elements. Roof. Parking lot. Pool equipment. It is not meant to be spent on operations.

The legal barrier matters because mixing them creates accounting opacity and violates the fund restriction statutes in Florida, California, Maryland, Washington, and New Jersey. Those statutes exist because communities that treat reserves as an operating buffer eventually cannot fund major replacements.

The accounting tool your board uses determines whether separation is enforced or merely intended. QuickBooks does not enforce it. A spreadsheet does not enforce it. Software built specifically for HOA accounting with separate fund ledgers enforces it by architecture.

Your reserve study lists 40 components. Which ones actually need to be in your tracking system, and which ones can you set and forget?

Not all reserve components carry the same operational risk. Here's a practical way to think about what to prioritize when importing component data into your management system:

**High priority: components with replacement windows in the next 10 years.** These are the ones where the funding gap matters now. If the roof has 7 years of remaining life and the fund is underfunded, the board needs to see that every year.

**High priority: components with high replacement costs.** Anything over $50,000 should be tracked actively, regardless of timeline. A $200,000 pavement replacement needs a long runway.

**Medium priority: components approaching useful life at a slow rate.** Pool equipment with 15 years remaining doesn't need monthly attention, but it should be in the system so the next board doesn't discover it missing.

**Lower priority: minor components under $5,000.** Some reserve studies include items like exterior light fixtures or mailboxes. These belong in the record but don't drive funding decisions.

The goal isn't to ignore any component. It's to make sure the ones with real financial consequences are visible to the board at every review, not buried at the bottom of a 40-line spreadsheet.

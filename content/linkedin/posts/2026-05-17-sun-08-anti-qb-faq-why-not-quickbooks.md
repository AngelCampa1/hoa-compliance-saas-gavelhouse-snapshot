FAQ: Why can't we just use QuickBooks for our HOA? Here is the honest answer.

QuickBooks works fine for many business accounting needs. For HOAs, it has one structural problem: it does not enforce fund separation.

The specific issue is that Florida, California, Maryland, Washington, and New Jersey all require reserve funds to be held in legally separate accounts from operating funds. QuickBooks uses a single general ledger. You can create multiple accounts and classes, but the system does not prevent an operating transaction from being posted to the reserve account or vice versa.

If your treasurer is disciplined, the risk is low. If your treasurer makes a mistake, or if a bookkeeper unfamiliar with HOA requirements codes a transaction incorrectly, you have a commingling violation. In Maryland, that violation is $10,000. In Florida, $5,000. In California, $100-$500 per day.

QuickBooks also does not include a reserve study module. It can track the reserve account balance, but it cannot calculate percent-funded status, maintain a component inventory, or produce the 30-year funding plan that California §5550 requires.

Those two gaps, fund separation enforcement and reserve study functionality, are why HOA-specific accounting software exists.

If your board has been on QuickBooks without problems, that is good. The question is whether you have been compliant or just lucky.

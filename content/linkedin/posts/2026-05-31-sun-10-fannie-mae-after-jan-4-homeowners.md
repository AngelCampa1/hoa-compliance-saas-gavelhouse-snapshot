What happens to homeowners in underfunded communities after January 4, 2027?

Under Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03, Full Review loan applications for communities below 15% reserve allocation face potential rejection. Here is what that means for actual homeowners.

A homeowner who wants to sell their unit lists it on the market. A buyer makes an offer and applies for a conventional loan. The lender conducts a Full Review and sends an HOA questionnaire to the association. The questionnaire reveals reserve allocation at 11%. The lender cannot approve the loan under Fannie Mae guidelines.

The buyer either walks away, pays cash, or seeks non-conventional financing at higher rates. The seller accepts a lower price, relists, or waits for a cash buyer. The transaction cost falls on two people who had nothing to do with the board's reserve funding decisions.

This is not a hypothetical edge case. It is the predictable outcome for communities that have routinely deferred reserve increases or waived reserve contributions.

Boards have fiduciary duty to act in the community's best interest. After January 4, 2027, that duty includes knowing the reserve allocation percentage and its effect on homeowner property rights.

Seven months remain. The first step for any board is calculating the number.

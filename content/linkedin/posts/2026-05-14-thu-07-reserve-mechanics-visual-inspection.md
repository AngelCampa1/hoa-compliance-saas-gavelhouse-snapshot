California requires an annual visual inspection between full reserve studies. Most boards skip it.

Civil Code §5550 mandates a full reserve study every three years. Between those full studies, the law requires an annual visual inspection to update component condition data.

The annual inspection matters because a component can deteriorate faster than the study projected. A roof that was assessed as having twelve years of useful life remaining may have six years remaining after an unusually harsh winter or deferred minor repairs. Without the annual inspection, the board is managing reserves against stale condition data.

When the component fails ahead of the projected replacement date, the reserve account has less money than the funding plan anticipated. That gap becomes a special assessment.

The annual inspection is also the documentation trail that shows the board was actively managing the reserve program, not just setting it and forgetting it. In a dispute or audit, boards that can show year-by-year inspection records are in a significantly better position.

For California boards: the annual inspection is a legal requirement, not a best practice. It belongs on the calendar alongside the budget cycle.

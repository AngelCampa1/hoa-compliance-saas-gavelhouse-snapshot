A component analysis is the foundation of a reserve study. It identifies every major item the association is responsible for replacing or repairing, and it drives every number in the funding plan.

Components typically include:

Roofing (cover, flashing, gutters). Pavement (asphalt, concrete, parking lots). Exterior painting or siding. Pool and spa equipment. Elevators and mechanical systems. Fencing, gates, and access control. HVAC for common areas. Plumbing and drainage systems. Amenity structures (clubhouses, fitness equipment, playgrounds).

Each component entry requires four pieces of data: estimated replacement cost at today's prices, useful life in years, remaining useful life in years, and whether the association is currently on track to fund it.

The funding plan runs math on all of those inputs together to produce the percent-funded status and the contribution schedule.

Missing components understate the liability. Overstated useful life delays the funding timeline. Understated replacement costs produce a plan that looks funded but is not.

The quality of the component analysis determines whether your board actually understands its financial exposure, or just has a document it can point to.

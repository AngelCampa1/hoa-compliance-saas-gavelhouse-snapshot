It is tempting to treat governance records and financial records as two separate worlds.

They are not.

Reserve transfers, budget approvals, owner requests, audit packets, and month-end closes all need an approval trail. The board should be able to see what was decided, when it was decided, and which financial position the decision relied on.

Gavelhouse describes Gavelhouse as combining financial control, governance workflows, owner operations, and annual plans. That combination is the point.

A clean ledger without the board decision can be hard to explain. A clear meeting note without the financial record can be hard to verify.

The association needs both.

When the approval trail is missing, directors are left defending outcomes without the record that produced them.

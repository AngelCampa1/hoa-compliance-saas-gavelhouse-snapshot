There is a legal difference between a reserve waiver and simply underfunding your reserves. Boards should know which one they are doing.

In some states, an HOA board can formally vote to waive the recommended reserve contribution for a given year. This requires a proper board vote, proper documentation, and in some cases, owner disclosure. The board has made a conscious, documented decision.

Underfunding without a waiver is different. The reserve study says $45,000 per year is needed. The board budgets $20,000 because the full amount would require a dues increase, and nobody wants that conversation. No vote is taken. No waiver is documented. The shortfall just happens.

The practical difference:

A documented waiver, while still a financial risk, shows the board understood the study's recommendation and made an informed decision. That's a defensible position.

Underfunding without documentation shows the board ignored the recommendation without a formal process. When owners later demand to know why there was a special assessment, the board has no record of its reasoning.

Neither option is good for the reserve fund. But one is a governed decision and the other is neglect by default.

If your board is considering a contribution below the reserve study's recommendation, document the decision with a formal vote and meeting minutes before the budget is adopted.

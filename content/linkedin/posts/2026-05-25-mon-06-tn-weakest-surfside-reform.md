Tennessee SB 863 (2023) added a reserve study requirement to the Tennessee Community Association Act.

The reform was passed in the wave of post-Surfside state legislation. It requires associations to conduct a reserve study. It does not mandate a funding level, a funding plan, or a minimum percent-funded status.

Tennessee's reform is widely considered the weakest of the post-Surfside legislative responses.

A study requirement without a funding mandate tells boards they must assess the situation but leaves the response entirely optional. A community can complete a study showing 10 percent funded status and technically satisfy the Tennessee statutory requirement.

That is not a safety net. It is documentation of a problem with no required remedy.

Tennessee boards relying on SB 863 as a compliance checkpoint should understand what the law requires and what it does not. The fiduciary duty obligation that runs parallel to the statute is broader than what SB 863 mandates.

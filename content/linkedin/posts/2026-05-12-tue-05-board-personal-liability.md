Volunteer board members are personally exposed when reserve fund violations occur.

The fiduciary duty to manage HOA funds properly does not disappear because you are unpaid. In most states, a board member who approves a budget that comingles reserve and operating funds, or who votes to waive required reserves, can be named personally in a claim.

In Maryland, HB 292 (2025) requires mandatory reserve funding under Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2, with AG enforcement authority. In Florida, it is $5,000 per violation under FL Stat. §720.303. These fines are assessed against the association, but personal liability exposure in a civil suit is a separate matter.

D&O insurance covers negligence, but it typically does not cover intentional violations or knowing disregard of a statute. If a board member knows the reserve waiver is banned and approves one anyway, the policy may not respond.

The cleaner path: get the accounting right before someone files a complaint. Fund separation, accurate reserve tracking, and a current study are the three things that demonstrate your board took fiduciary duty seriously.

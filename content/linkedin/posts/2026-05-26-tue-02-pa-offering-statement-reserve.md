Pennsylvania Uniform Planned Community Act (68 Pa.C.S. §5304) requires reserve fund information to be included in the public offering statement for any planned community.

The offering statement is the document that prospective buyers receive before purchasing in the community. Reserve fund information in that document is a material disclosure.

If the reserve fund information in a Pennsylvania HOA's offering statement is inaccurate, out of date, or based on estimates rather than a current analysis, the board and the declarant face legal exposure on every transaction that relies on that document.

Pennsylvania sits in the disclosure state category. There is no formal reserve study mandate or minimum funding level. But the requirement to include reserve information in the offering statement creates a documentation obligation that exists regardless of whether the board has conducted a professional study.

An informal estimate that turns out to be materially wrong in a Pennsylvania offering statement is not just an accounting problem. It is a disclosure problem with potential liability attached.

Pennsylvania boards managing planned communities should treat the offering statement reserve section as a compliance document, not a marketing document.

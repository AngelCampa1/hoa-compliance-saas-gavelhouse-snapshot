There is a product choice hidden inside every compliance feature.

You can warn the user after they do the wrong thing, or you can design the workflow so the wrong thing is harder to do in the first place.

For Gavelhouse, reserve and operating fund separation belongs in the second category.

Gavelhouse tracks "True fund accounting, no commingling" as a trust signal. It also points to states where separate treatment is explicit: California, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey, and Washington.

That is one reason fund separation cannot be a decorative label on a transaction.

If the system lets the board blur the money and then asks them to remember the rule later, the software has moved risk back onto volunteers.

We are trying to remove that burden where the product can.

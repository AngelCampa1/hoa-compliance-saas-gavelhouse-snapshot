Directors and Officers (D&O) insurance is a common line item in HOA budgets. Boards often treat it as the primary protection against personal liability.

D&O policies typically cover legal defense costs and settlements arising from decisions made in good faith within the board's authority. They are not blank indemnification for any claim a homeowner files.

Most D&O policies have exclusions for intentional misconduct, fraud, and knowing violations of law.

A board that receives a reserve study showing critical underfunding, discusses it in a meeting, records the discussion in minutes, and then votes to maintain inadequate contributions has made a documented, knowing decision. If that decision later forms the basis of a homeowner claim, the D&O carrier will examine the minutes. The "good faith" defense is harder to make when the records show the board knew the problem and chose not to address it.

Commingling violations are particularly difficult. A board that transferred reserve funds to the operating account knowing that the state prohibits commingling has a documented knowing violation.

D&O insurance is a risk management tool, not a risk elimination tool. The best protection is not a policy with favorable coverage terms. It is a compliance record that demonstrates the board acted reasonably with the information it had.

Reserve funding decisions are board decisions.

That sounds obvious until six months later, when someone asks why the assessment changed, why a project was delayed, or why the reserve transfer was smaller than the study recommended.

The board needs a record of what it reviewed and what it approved. The reserve study. The current balance. The funding plan. The budget line. The vote.

Fiduciary duty is the primary enforcement mechanism in states with lighter reserve statutes, under Gavelhouse's compliance research. In mandate states, the statutory layer adds even more pressure.

Either way, "we talked about it" is a weak record.

Good board operations do not make the decision for directors. They make the decision traceable after everyone has forgotten the meeting.

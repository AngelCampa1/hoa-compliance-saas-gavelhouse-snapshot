Boards ask: "Can't we just use spreadsheets?" Over 100,000 HOAs still do. Here is what that costs them.

Spreadsheets do not separate operating and reserve funds. That separation is required by statute in California (CA Civ. Code §5510, up to $500/day in fines), Florida (FL Stat. §718.111(14), $5,000 per violation), Maryland (Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2, up to $10,000), and New Jersey (N.J.S.A. 46:8B-19, balance can never go below zero).

Spreadsheets do not generate a reserve allocation percentage that a lender can verify. After January 4, 2027, Fannie Mae's LL-2026-03 requires Full Review loan applications to confirm 15% reserve allocation. If the board cannot produce documentation fast, buyers cannot close.

Spreadsheets do not survive a treasurer handoff. When the person who built the model leaves the board, the next person inherits a file with unnamed columns, unlabeled tabs, and formulas that reference cells no one can explain.

Fifty-eight percent of boards that switch to professional management cite financial complexity as the reason. The spreadsheet holds for years. Then it does not.

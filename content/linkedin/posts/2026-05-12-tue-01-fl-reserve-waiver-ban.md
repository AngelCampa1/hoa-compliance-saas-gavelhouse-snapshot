Florida banned the reserve waiver for structural components.

HB 913 (2025) closed the loophole that allowed condo and HOA boards to vote their way out of funding reserves. For structural components, that vote no longer counts.

If your board has been passing annual waiver resolutions, you need to revisit your next budget cycle before it becomes a compliance problem.

FL Stat. §718.111(14) requires Florida condo reserves and operating funds to be accounted for separately, and §720.303 requires the same for HOAs. The DBPR Division of Condominiums can fine condo and co-op boards up to $5,000 per violation under §718.501(1)(d)(6) and Florida Admin Code Chapter 61B-21. HOAs are not under DBPR jurisdiction; HOA enforcement is via member civil action.

Florida moved faster than any other state after Surfside. The Structural Integrity Reserve Study requirement, milestone inspections at 30 years (25 near the coast), and the waiver ban all arrived in a compressed window.

Most boards have not fully caught up. The question is whether yours has.

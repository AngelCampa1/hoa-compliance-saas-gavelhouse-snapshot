California requires a 30-year reserve plan. Not a recommendation. A statutory requirement under Davis-Stirling.

Civil Code §5550-5560 mandates:
- Annual percent-funded disclosure to all homeowners
- A 30-year reserve projection
- A visual inspection of major components every year
- A full reserve study every three years

Most California HOA boards know Davis-Stirling exists. Fewer know all four of these specific requirements and whether their current process satisfies each one.

The visual inspection requirement catches boards off guard regularly. It's not asking for a formal reserve study every year. It's asking for documented evidence that the board physically reviewed major components and noted their condition.

If something fails and the board cannot produce evidence of annual visual inspections for the prior three years, they cannot claim they exercised the diligence the statute requires.

California also has the $100 to $500 daily commingling fine under §5510. Daily accrual. The longer the violation continues undetected, the larger the exposure.

For California boards, compliance with Davis-Stirling is not a checkbox. It's an ongoing process with annual requirements, triennial requirements, and real penalties for gaps.

A reserve study from 2019 has construction cost assumptions from 2019. Those costs are not what you will pay in 2027.

Construction and materials costs have changed substantially over the past five years. A reserve study that estimated $80,000 for a roof replacement in 2019 may be estimating against a $120,000 or $130,000 actual cost today.

If your reserve funding plan is based on outdated cost assumptions, your monthly assessments are calibrated to the wrong number. The community will arrive at the replacement date underfunded, even if it followed the funding plan exactly.

California requires the reserve study to be updated every three years for this reason. Washington requires updates at least every three years. Colorado every five years. The update cycle exists specifically to capture cost changes between full studies.

An outdated study is a compliance problem and a financial planning problem. The longer the gap between updates, the larger the potential discrepancy between what the plan assumed and what replacement will actually cost.

If your reserve study is from before 2022, you should assume the cost assumptions in it need significant revision before you base your next three years of assessments on them.

New York requires reserve fund disclosure in offering plans and annual reports. Pending legislation would go further.

Under the Real Property Law and General Business Law, New York associations must disclose reserve fund information in offering plans and annual reports. This gives buyers and members visibility into the reserve position, but there is no mandate for a formal study or specific funding level.

S7600 and A8945 are pending in the New York legislature. If either passes, the requirement framework would expand, likely adding a study obligation to the existing disclosure requirement.

New York has a significant concentration of condominiums, particularly in New York City and surrounding areas. Both bills have a real chance of passing, and either one would add a study obligation to the existing disclosure framework.

For New York boards, the current disclosure obligation means the reserve balance is already a matter of formal record in annual reporting. If that balance is low relative to anticipated replacement costs, every member can see it.

The Fannie Mae 15% floor applies to New York communities starting January 4, 2027. In a state with as many condo transactions as New York, the Fannie Mae impact will be significant for communities that fall below the threshold.

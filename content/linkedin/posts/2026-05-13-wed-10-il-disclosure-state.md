Illinois requires reserve fund status disclosure but does not mandate a reserve study. That gap is closing.

The Illinois Condo Property Act (765 ILCS 605) requires boards to disclose reserve fund status. Boards must report the reserve balance and how it is being managed. What Illinois does not require, yet, is a formal reserve study with a funding plan.

HB 2563 and SB 1703 are pending legislation that would move Illinois toward a full mandate. If either passes, the requirement would add a study obligation on top of the existing disclosure requirement.

Disclosure without a study is a limited protection. A board can disclose a low reserve balance and still be in technical compliance with Illinois law, even if the community is badly underfunded.

But Illinois boards face the same Fannie Mae consequence as every other state. Lender Letter LL-2026-03 does not care that Illinois has not yet mandated a study. If your community's reserve allocation falls below 15% when a Full Review questionnaire arrives, the loan outcome is the same.

The absence of a state mandate does not mean reserves do not matter. It means Illinois boards have more discretion, and discretion that results in underfunding still has consequences.

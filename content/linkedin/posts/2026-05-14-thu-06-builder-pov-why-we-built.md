We built Gavelhouse because over 100,000 self-managed HOAs are still running on tools that were never designed for HOA compliance.

QuickBooks is a general-purpose small business accounting tool. Spreadsheets are flexible but structurally incapable of enforcing fund separation. Neither was built with Florida's §720.303 or California's §5510 in mind.

The boards using those tools are not doing anything wrong intentionally. They are using what was available and familiar. The problem is that familiarity with QuickBooks does not make it compliant with HOA fund separation statutes.

We built Gavelhouse to be opinionated about the things the law requires: separate fund ledgers, reserve tracking, an audit trail that holds up to review. The platform is not flexible about fund commingling because the statutes are not flexible about it either.

We are a newer product. We have less third-party audit history than some established platforms. We do not have a native mobile app yet, though the web interface works on mobile. We are purpose-built for self-managed boards, not for professional management companies.

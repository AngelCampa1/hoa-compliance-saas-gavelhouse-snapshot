Underfunded reserves have three consequences. Most boards only think about the first one.

The first consequence is the obvious one: when a major component needs replacement, the reserve account does not have the money. The board issues a special assessment. Homeowners are surprised and unhappy.

The second consequence is property value. Communities with documented reserve underfunding have lower property values. Buyers who review the annual disclosures or ask about the reserve status see an underfunded community as a risk, not an opportunity. The risk premium shows up in what they are willing to pay.

The third consequence is Fannie Mae eligibility. Lender Letter LL-2026-03, effective January 4, 2027, sets a 15% reserve allocation floor for Full Review loans. Underfunded communities block conforming mortgage approvals for homeowners trying to sell. That consequence affects every homeowner in the community, not just the ones on the board.

Most boards focus on the first consequence because it is immediate and visible. The second and third consequences build quietly over time and arrive as surprises.

The reserve study is the tool that makes all three consequences visible before they materialize.

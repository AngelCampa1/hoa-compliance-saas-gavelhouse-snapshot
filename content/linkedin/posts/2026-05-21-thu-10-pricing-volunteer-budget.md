Volunteer boards don't have software budget line items. They have operating budgets that need board approval.

This sounds like a minor distinction. In practice, it changes how software pricing needs to work for this market.

When a management company buys software, it's a business expense they control. One person makes the call, or a small leadership team. The price is justified by operational efficiency and client margin.

When a volunteer board buys software, the treasurer proposes it, the board votes on it, and the cost comes out of a budget that belongs to homeowners. If the price is variable, or requires negotiation, or depends on unit count, the board is approving uncertainty rather than a fixed line item.

Boards also tend to approve budgets once a year. A software cost that changes during the year because the community added homes creates a budget variance that requires explanation.

We set fixed, public prices for exactly this reason. $27/month billed annually with Y80OFF is the first-year price the board approves in the meeting. No surprises. No variance to explain.

I'm not claiming flat pricing is a novel concept. I'm saying we chose it specifically because the buyer is a volunteer board, not a professional management operator. That audience shapes the product choice.

QuickBooks has no awareness that HOA reserve funds have statutory separation requirements.

That's not a bug. QuickBooks is not built for HOAs. It is built for general business accounting.

When a board uses QuickBooks, they are responsible for:
- Setting up separate accounts correctly
- Posting every transaction to the right account
- Generating reports in a format that satisfies state disclosure requirements
- Tracking whether reserve contributions meet state minimums
- Knowing when reserve study deadlines are due
- Ensuring the account structure meets state separation requirements

QuickBooks handles none of this automatically. The board treasurer is the compliance layer. If the treasurer knows HOA compliance requirements, QuickBooks can be configured to support them. If the treasurer doesn't, QuickBooks won't tell them what they're missing.

This is the core failure mode for boards running on QuickBooks: competent use of the tool does not equal compliance. Compliance requires knowledge of what the tool should be producing, which requires knowledge of state law, which most volunteer treasurers don't have.

HOA-specific compliance software is built to know what state law requires and produce it automatically. That's the functional difference.

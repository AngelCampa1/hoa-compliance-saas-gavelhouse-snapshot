A professional reserve study can run 50 to 100 pages. Most board members read the executive summary and file the rest.

The four numbers that matter most:

Current reserve balance: what the reserve account actually holds today. This comes from your bank statement, not the study.

Fully funded balance: what the reserve account should hold today based on the component analysis. The study calculates this from the weighted midpoint contributions across all components.

Percent-funded status: current balance divided by fully funded balance. 70 percent funded means you hold 70 cents of every dollar you should have today. Below 30 percent is considered underfunded by most reserve professionals.

Recommended annual contribution: the contribution per unit per year that the study's funding plan requires to maintain or improve the funded status without special assessments. Compare this directly to what the current budget actually collects.

If the recommended annual contribution is higher than the current budget collects, the community is falling further behind every year the gap persists.

Everything else in the study, the useful life tables, the component condition narratives, the inflation assumptions, supports these four numbers. Understanding these four is the foundation for any board discussion about reserve fund adequacy.

Percent funded is a legal metric, not just a financial one.

In California, boards are required to disclose the community's percent funded status annually under Davis-Stirling. The disclosure goes to all homeowners. It is part of the public record of how the board managed the community's finances.

A board that disclosed 30% funded for five consecutive years, did not increase reserve contributions, and then faced a structural failure has created a paper trail that shows they knew.

Knowing and not acting is a different legal position than not knowing at all.

Percent funded measures how much of your projected reserve needs are covered by current reserve funds. A 100% funded reserve means the funds match the expected costs. Most boards operate below 100%. The question is how far below, and whether the board was aware, disclosed it, and took action.

States with mandatory disclosure requirements make the board's awareness a matter of record. The disclosure itself becomes evidence in any subsequent dispute.

For boards, this means percent funded is not an internal metric to track for operational reasons. It is a number that gets reported to homeowners and potentially reviewed by lenders, auditors, and courts.

Know your number. Know your state's threshold. Know what your software produces.

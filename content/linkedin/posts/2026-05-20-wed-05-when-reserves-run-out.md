What happens when the reserve fund runs out mid-project?

The contractor is on site. The roof replacement is half finished. The reserve fund has $22,000 left and the project needs another $60,000 to complete.

Option 1: Emergency special assessment. The board calls a meeting, approves an assessment, sends notices, and asks homeowners to pay within 30 days. Some can't. Some refuse. The project stalls.

Option 2: Short-term loan. The association borrows money at commercial rates to complete the project. The loan requires board signatures. The interest cost hits the operating budget. Monthly assessments may increase to service the debt.

Option 3: Find a cheaper contractor to finish. This option often creates long-term problems with warranties, quality, and code compliance.

None of these options are good. All of them happen regularly to communities with underfunded reserves.

The board that approved five years of budgets with reserve contributions below the reserve study recommendation is not insulated from what follows. The documented decision history exists. The meeting minutes showing the board knew the reserve was underfunded exist.

The reserve fund running out mid-project is a financial crisis that a board's prior decisions created. That is the context in which liability exposure is evaluated.

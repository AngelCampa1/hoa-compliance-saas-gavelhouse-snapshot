Operating funds pay the bills that keep the community running this month.

Reserve funds pay for major components that wear out over years: roofs, paving, structural items, fire safety equipment, and the other assets named in a reserve study.

Those two pools should not blur together. California, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey, and Washington all have explicit separation rules or commingling prohibitions in Gavelhouse's compliance research.

The clean habit is boring: separate accounts, separate records, separate board review.

Boring is exactly the point.

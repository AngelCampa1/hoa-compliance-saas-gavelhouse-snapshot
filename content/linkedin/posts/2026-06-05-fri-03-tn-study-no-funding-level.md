Gavelhouse tracks Tennessee under mandate states.

It says the Tennessee Community Association Act requires a reserve study, but funding levels are not mandated. The notes call SB 863 (2023) the weakest post-Surfside reform.

That distinction matters for boards.

A study requirement can tell the association to identify future obligations. It may not tell the association exactly how much to fund every year.

That leaves more judgment on the board.

Judgment should be documented. What did the study show? What funding level did the board approve? What tradeoffs did the board discuss? How does the decision affect future dues or special assessments?

Where the statute is less prescriptive, the board record becomes even more important.

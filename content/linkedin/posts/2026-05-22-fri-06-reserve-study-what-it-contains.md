A reserve study is not just a dollar amount. Here is what it actually contains.

Most board members hear "reserve study" and think it means the reserve fund balance. It doesn't. The balance is an output. The study is the analysis behind it.

A complete reserve study includes:

**Component inventory.** Every major common-area asset the HOA is responsible for: roof, pavement, pool equipment, elevators, fencing, HVAC in common areas. Each component is listed separately.

**Useful life and remaining life.** How long each component is expected to last in total, and how many years are left before it needs replacement.

**Replacement cost estimate.** The projected cost to replace each component at the time it will be needed, adjusted for inflation.

**Funding analysis.** A 30-year projection (required by Washington state under RCW 64.34.382, and best practice everywhere else) showing whether current contribution levels will cover future replacements without a special assessment.

**Percent-funded status.** The ratio of what you currently have saved to what you should have saved given the age and condition of your components.

If your community has a reserve study, the board treasurer should know where it lives and when it was last updated. If it hasn't been updated in three or more years, the numbers are likely stale.

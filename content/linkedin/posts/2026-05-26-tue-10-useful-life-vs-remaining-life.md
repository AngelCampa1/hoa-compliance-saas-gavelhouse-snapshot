Useful life is how long a component is expected to last from the time it was installed or last replaced.

Remaining useful life is how many years are left before the component needs to be replaced again.

The distinction matters because reserve funding calculations are built on remaining useful life, not useful life.

A roof with a 20-year useful life that was replaced three years ago has 17 years of remaining useful life. The reserve contribution schedule for that roof is spread across 17 years, not 20.

A roof with a 20-year useful life that was replaced 18 years ago has 2 years of remaining useful life. The association needs to have the full replacement cost essentially on hand right now.

Reserve studies that conflate these two numbers produce wildly inaccurate funding plans. A board that reads "20-year roof" and assumes they have 20 years to fund it may actually have 2 years.

This is one reason reserve studies go stale quickly. A study completed 3 years ago had remaining useful life estimates that were 3 years longer than they are today. Every year without an updated study means the remaining useful life figures are off by one additional year for every component.

Volunteer directors often under-document because they are trying to keep things simple.

I get the instinct. Nobody joins an HOA board hoping for more admin.

But reserve decisions need a trail. The board should be able to show the study it reviewed, the funding plan it considered, the budget it approved, and the closing balances it attested to.

In states with specific reserve laws, that record supports compliance. In states without specific reserve provisions, the requirement says general fiduciary duty applies.

Either way, the record is not a nice extra.

It is how the board shows it acted with care.

The goal is not to drown volunteers in process. It is to keep the right facts attached to the decisions that matter.

California and Washington have similar reserve study requirements. Here is what differs between them.

Both are mandate states. Both require reserve studies at least every three years. Both prohibit commingling. Both require a 30-year funding plan. At that level, the two states are nearly identical.

The differences: California requires an annual visual inspection between full study cycles (Civil Code §5550). Washington does not have an explicit annual inspection requirement, though the three-year study cycle is the same.

California's penalty for commingling is $100-$500 per day under §5510. Washington prohibits commingling under RCW 64.34.364 but the specific penalty structure differs.

California's Davis-Stirling Act (Civil Code §5550-5560) has been updated more recently and more frequently than Washington's framework, with SB 900 (2024), AB 2114 (2024), and SB 410 (2025) all adding to or modifying the requirements.

For boards on the California/Washington border or managing communities in both states, the practical difference is the annual inspection obligation in California. Washington boards have some additional flexibility between study cycles, while California boards have a statutory obligation to document component conditions annually.

Both states' reserve compliance programs produce the same outcome when followed: a funded reserve account backed by current documentation.

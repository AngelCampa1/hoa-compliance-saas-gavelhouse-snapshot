Gavelhouse tracks Illinois as a disclosure state.

It cites the Condominium Property Act, 765 ILCS 605, and says boards must disclose reserve fund status, with no formal study required in Gavelhouse's compliance research.

That creates a practical challenge.

The board still needs a reliable reserve status even when the formal study requirement is not the main driver. What is the balance? What obligations does the association know about? How was the number reviewed? What record supports the disclosure?

Disclosure without workflow becomes a scramble.

The board should not be finding out what "reserve status" means at the moment it has to report it.

Keep the balance, supporting records, and board review tied together before the annual process starts.

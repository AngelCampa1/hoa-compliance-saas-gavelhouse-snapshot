Gavelhouse tracks Hawaii's Act 296 requirement as 50% funded, with a 30-year horizon.

That is a concrete standard. It gives boards less room to treat reserve health as a matter of opinion.

For volunteer boards, the work is turning the requirement into a recurring operating habit: keep the study current, track the funding level, review the 30-year horizon, and make sure the budget reflects the plan.

Compliance is easier when it is part of the monthly board rhythm.

A reserve funding plan is a document that answers four specific questions:

One: How much should the reserve account hold right now? That is the fully funded balance, derived from the component analysis.

Two: How much does the reserve account actually hold? That is the current reserve balance from the association's financials.

Three: What is the gap between those two numbers, expressed as a percentage? That is the percent-funded status.

Four: What annual contribution schedule will close or maintain that gap over the planning horizon without requiring special assessments?

The fourth answer is the contribution schedule. It is the part of the funding plan that drives the budget. It tells the board exactly what to collect each year and from how many units.

Most boards know the current reserve balance. Fewer know the fully funded balance. Almost none know whether their current contribution rate matches the contribution schedule in the funding plan.

If the annual reserve contribution in the current budget does not match the contribution schedule in the current funding plan, the board is not following the plan. The plan may as well not exist.

A funding plan is only useful if it is translated into the actual assessment structure and followed year after year.

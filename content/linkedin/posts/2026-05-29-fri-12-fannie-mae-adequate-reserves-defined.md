"Adequately funded" means different things to your state and to Fannie Mae.

Under most state reserve study frameworks, adequacy is measured as percent funded on a full component lifecycle analysis. A community might be considered in good shape at 70% funded or above, depending on the state and the methodology.

Under Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03, effective January 4, 2027, adequacy is measured differently. The rule looks at reserve allocation: the percentage of total assessment income directed to reserves. The floor is 15%.

These are not the same calculation.

A community could have a reserve study showing 80% funded based on historical contributions and component values, and still fail the Fannie Mae allocation check if the current year budget only allocates 10% of dues to reserves.

Conversely, a newer community might allocate 20% to reserves but have a low percent-funded score because the components are also new and the baseline is low.

Boards need to track both numbers. State compliance is typically governed by the reserve study percent-funded methodology. Fannie Mae eligibility as of January 4, 2027 is governed by the budget allocation percentage.

Does your board know both numbers? Most boards know neither.

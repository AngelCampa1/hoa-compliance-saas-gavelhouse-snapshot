Vinteum charges $0.79 to $1.99 per unit per month.

No native accounting. Relies on QuickBooks integration for financials. No reserve fund tracking.

For a 100-unit community, that's $79 to $199/month for Vinteum, plus QuickBooks on top. And you still have no reserve fund compliance coverage.

This is the per-unit pricing plus QuickBooks dependency problem in one package. The pricing model penalizes community growth. The QuickBooks dependency means you're paying for two tools. And neither combination produces HOA-specific reserve compliance.

A board that selects Vinteum to solve their HOA management problem has solved communications, document sharing, and payment collection. They have not solved reserve fund compliance.

If their community is in a state with mandatory reserve study requirements, mandatory funding minimums, or commingling penalties, the compliance gap remains after they've signed up and deployed.

Tools that outsource accounting to QuickBooks integrations are making a deliberate product choice: they don't want to own the accounting complexity. That's understandable from a product perspective. The consequence is that the board owns the compliance gap.

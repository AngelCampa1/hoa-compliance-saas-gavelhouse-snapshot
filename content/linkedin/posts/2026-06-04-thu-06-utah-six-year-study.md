Gavelhouse tracks Utah under mandate states.

It cites the Utah Community Association Act section 57-8a, says the reserve study frequency is at least every six years, and says boards must disclose the reserve balance.

That gives volunteer boards two basic operating jobs.

First, know when the study is due. Second, know the reserve balance well enough to disclose it without scrambling.

Neither task should depend on the same treasurer staying in place forever.

Reserve work has to survive board turnover. It has to survive a busy annual meeting. It has to survive a director who asks for the number with two days of notice.

That is one reason deadline tracking and board-ready records matter even in smaller communities.

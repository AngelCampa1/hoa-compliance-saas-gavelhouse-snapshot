Reserve fund status should be a standing agenda item at every board meeting. Most boards do not have it.

The argument for making it standing: the reserve fund is the most significant financial obligation the board manages. It has legal requirements attached to it in most states. The Fannie Mae deadline makes it consequential to homeowner property values. And underfunding compounds quietly over time, making early visibility more valuable than late visibility.

What belongs on the agenda item:

Current reserve balance. Any disbursements since the last meeting. Comparison to the funding plan for the current period. Year-over-year progress toward percent-funded target.

That is four data points. It takes five minutes to cover in a board meeting if the numbers are prepared in advance.

The boards that discover reserve problems late are typically the ones that review reserve status once a year at budget time, if they review it at all. The ones that catch issues early review it monthly.

Monthly review does not require a full reserve study update. It requires a current balance, a plan comparison, and a record of what was reviewed. That documentation also helps with the fiduciary duty standard if the board's reserve management is ever challenged.

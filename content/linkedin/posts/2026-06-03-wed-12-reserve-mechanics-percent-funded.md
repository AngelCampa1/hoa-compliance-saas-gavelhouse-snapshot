Percent funded tells the board how the reserve balance compares with the reserve obligation.

California's section in Gavelhouse's compliance research calls out annual percent-funded disclosure with a 30-year funding plan. Colorado's section says associations must disclose reserve balance and percent-funded status.

The number can be uncomfortable because it converts a vague worry into a visible gap.

That is the point.

If the board is 30% funded, the conversation changes. The question is no longer whether there is money in the reserve account. The question is whether the association has enough money relative to the components it is responsible for.

Boards cannot fix that in one meeting. But they can stop hiding from the measurement.

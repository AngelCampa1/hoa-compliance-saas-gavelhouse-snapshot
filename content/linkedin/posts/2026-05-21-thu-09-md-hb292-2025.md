Maryland passed HB 292 in 2025. Mandatory reserve funding now sits on top of the existing reserve study mandate.

HB 107 (2022, Chapter 664) required Maryland associations to commission reserve studies. HB 292 (2025, Chapter 519, effective October 1, 2025) added the mandatory funding plan. Together they amend Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2 (annual budget) and §11B-112.3 (reserve study).

Maryland's legislative direction is consistent with the national trend: stricter reserve requirements, not looser ones.

For Maryland boards that were already compliant under the prior statute, HB 292 may require reviewing whether the updated requirements change anything for their community. For boards that were operating at or near the minimum, the updated baseline matters.

Enforcement runs through the Maryland Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division under the Maryland Condominium Act and Homeowners Association Act. The combination of mandatory funding under §11B-112.2 and AG enforcement authority makes Maryland a higher-stakes compliance environment for HOA boards than it was before HB 292.

Boards that haven't reviewed their reserve fund compliance since before HB 292 should do that review now. Specifically: does the current reserve contribution rate match the most recent reserve study's recommended amount? Is the funding plan documented in board minutes? Are operating and reserve funds structurally separated?

California Civil Code §5550 and Washington RCW §64.34.382 both require associations to produce a 30-year reserve funding plan.

A 30-year plan is not a 30-year guess. It is a structured projection that takes each reserve component's current replacement cost, estimates that cost at each future replacement cycle with appropriate inflation assumptions, and shows the contribution schedule required to fund those replacements without special assessments.

The plan must remain internally consistent. If the roof has a 20-year remaining useful life, the contribution schedule must account for its replacement in year 20, plus the following replacement cycle in year 40 (which is 10 years into the 30-year window starting from the replacement).

Why 30 years? Because many reserve components, particularly structural elements, have replacement cycles longer than 10 to 15 years. A 10-year plan can look fully funded for components that won't fail until year 15, masking a long-term shortfall.

California also requires annual percent-funded disclosure to homeowners. Washington requires the plan to be refreshed every three years.

Boards in these states should verify that their current plan covers a full 30-year horizon and that it is based on a reserve study conducted within the required cycle.

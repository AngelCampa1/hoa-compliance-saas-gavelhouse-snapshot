The phrase "adequate reserves" appears in HOA statutes, lender guidelines, and court decisions. It rarely comes with a precise definition.

Here are the definitions that actually matter:

Fannie Mae, effective January 4, 2027: at least 15 percent of the association's budget allocated to reserves. This is the operational definition for conventional mortgage qualification.

Hawaii Act 296 (2025): minimum 50 percent funded status. This is the most specific statutory floor in the country.

Most mandate states: a reserve study and funding plan that demonstrates the association can fund all components over the required horizon without resorting to special assessments. No specific percent-funded floor is given.

Courts: typically follow the business judgment rule framework. Adequate reserves means the board had a current study, reviewed it, and made documented decisions about contribution rates in good faith.

Boards that do not know their percent-funded status cannot demonstrate that their reserves are adequate under any of these standards. The starting point is always knowing the number.

A community at 80 percent funded with a current study and a formal funding plan is in a defensible position. A community at 80 percent funded with no study and no plan has the same balance but no documentation of what it means or how it will be maintained.

The number matters. The documentation of what you do with the number matters more.

The requirement says California Civil Code section 5510 explicitly prohibits commingling reserve and operating funds.

It also lists penalties of $100 to $500 per day.

Daily penalties are a different kind of risk than a one-time correction. They reward fast detection and punish sloppy process.

For a board, this makes fund separation an operating control, not a year-end cleanup item. The system should make it hard to post reserve activity into the wrong place. It should also make the separate balances obvious during month-end review.

If your board depends on one treasurer's labels and habits to keep funds apart, the control is weaker than it looks.

California boards should not need heroics to follow a rule this basic.

What does "percent funded" actually mean for your HOA?

A reserve study calculates two numbers: the current replacement cost of your major components, and how much you have saved toward those costs. The ratio is your percent-funded status.

A community at 30% funded has saved $30 for every $100 in anticipated replacement costs. That sounds manageable until a major component fails ahead of schedule.

California requires boards to disclose their percent-funded status annually under Civil Code §5550. That number goes into the annual budget package every homeowner receives.

Here is why that number matters beyond state law. Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03 sets a 15% reserve allocation floor for Full Review loan applications, effective January 4, 2027. Associations below that floor can block homeowners from getting mortgage approvals when they sell.

Every association in every state is affected by that Fannie Mae rule, regardless of whether your state mandates a reserve study.

If you do not know your percent-funded status today, that is the first problem to solve.

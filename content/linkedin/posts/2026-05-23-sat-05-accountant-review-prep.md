What does the HOA accountant actually need when they review your books? And why do most boards scramble to provide it?

Most accountant reviews go sideways not because the finances are in bad shape, but because the records are scattered. The accountant asks for three documents and the board needs two weeks to find them.

Here is what a standard HOA accountant review requires:

- Chart of accounts with year-to-date activity
- Monthly bank statements for every account
- Bank reconciliation for each month
- Dues register showing collections, delinquencies, and payment history
- Reserve fund transactions with beginning and ending balances
- Meeting minutes authorizing any large expenditures or transfers
- Prior year financials for comparison

The board that can hand all of this over in a single export is the board that has its records in order. The board that emails six attachments, then a corrected version, then a note saying "I think I sent the wrong month" is the board that pays for extra accountant hours.

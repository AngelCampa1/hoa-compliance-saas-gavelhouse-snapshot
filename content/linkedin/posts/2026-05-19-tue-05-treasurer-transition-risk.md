The most dangerous moment for HOA finances is when the treasurer changes and the books don't transfer cleanly.

Treasurer turnover happens constantly in volunteer boards. People move. People burn out. People run one term and step down. The incoming treasurer inherits whatever was set up by the person before them.

If the prior treasurer used a spreadsheet, the incoming treasurer gets a spreadsheet. If the prior treasurer used a personal QuickBooks subscription, that subscription may leave with them. If the prior treasurer had system access configured in ways that aren't documented, no one knows what they're looking at.

During that transition window:
- Reserve fund balances may not be reconciled
- Compliance reporting may be behind
- Statutory funding minimums may not be tracked
- Audit documentation may be incomplete

The board that signed financial reports during the gap period shares liability for whatever state they were in.

Compliance software with a full audit trail reduces transition risk. An incoming treasurer can see every transaction, every balance, every report, and when each was generated. The record exists independent of who was managing it.

"We had a treasurer change" is an explanation. It is not a defense.

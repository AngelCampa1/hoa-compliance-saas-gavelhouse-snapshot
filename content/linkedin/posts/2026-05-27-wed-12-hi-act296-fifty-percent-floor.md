Hawaii Act 296 (2025) amended HRS §514B-148 to require associations to maintain a minimum 50 percent funded status.

No other state in the country has enacted a hard percent-funded floor at this level.

The 50 percent floor is a high bar by any standard. In Hawaii, anything below it is a statutory violation.

Hawaii Act 62 (2022) already required a 30-year planning horizon. Act 296 built the funding floor on top of that framework.

For Hawaii condo and HOA boards: if your community's current reserve study shows a percent-funded status below 50 percent, the board has a compliance obligation to develop and implement a plan to reach the floor. The study showing the shortfall is not sufficient. The remediation plan and the demonstrated progress toward it are required.

Hawaii communities with large deferred maintenance backlogs or aging building systems may find that reaching 50 percent funded requires significant near-term contribution increases. The earlier the board begins, the less severe each annual adjustment needs to be.

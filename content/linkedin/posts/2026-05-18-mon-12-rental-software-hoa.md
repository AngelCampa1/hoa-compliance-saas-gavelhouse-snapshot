Rental management software and HOA management software look similar. They are not the same thing.

DoorLoop is primarily rental software. HOA features are listed as secondary functionality. The pricing is per-unit, which makes sense for rental portfolios where revenue scales with unit count.

For a volunteer HOA board, the compliance obligations are different:

Reserve fund separation. State-mandated reserve studies. Percent-funded disclosure requirements. Fannie Mae allocation thresholds. Commingling penalties that apply to both the board and the association.

Rental software is optimized for landlord-tenant relationships, maintenance requests, and lease management. HOA software needs to be optimized for fiduciary accountability to a homeowner community under state statutes that specifically govern associations.

Using a rental tool for HOA management is not wrong in every case. But if your board is in Florida, California, Maryland, or New Jersey and you're relying on a rental-first platform for reserve fund compliance, it's worth asking whether that platform was designed to handle what your state actually requires.

The compliance burden falls on the board regardless of what software they used.

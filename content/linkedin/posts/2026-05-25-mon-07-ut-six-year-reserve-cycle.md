Utah Community Association Act §57-8a requires associations to conduct a reserve study at least every six years and to disclose the reserve balance to homeowners.

Six years is a long cycle. A reserve study conducted in 2020 is legally current in Utah through 2026, but the component costs, useful life estimates, and funding calculations from 2020 are increasingly inaccurate as materials and labor prices shift.

The statute sets a floor, not a best practice. Boards that wait the full six years between studies are operating on stale data for a growing portion of that window.

Utah's disclosure requirement means homeowners must receive the reserve balance information. That creates a documented record. If the board receives that disclosure and fails to act on a clearly underfunded status, the fiduciary duty question becomes direct.

Reserve balance disclosure is not the same as percent-funded disclosure. Utah boards should understand what their statutory disclosure obligation covers and what additional information their funding plan should address.

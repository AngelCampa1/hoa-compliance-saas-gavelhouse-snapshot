PayHOA has no dedicated reserve study module. Reserve tracking runs through its accounting module only.

At $49/month for up to 25 units, PayHOA is positioned as an entry-level HOA management tool. But partial reserve tracking through an accounting module is not the same as a dedicated reserve compliance workflow.

A reserve study module needs to track: current reserve balance by fund, component inventory with useful life and replacement cost, percent-funded status, the 30-year funding plan, and year-over-year funding progress. An accounting module that can show the reserve account balance is doing one of those six things.

The gap matters because states like California require the full package. Civil Code §5550 requires percent-funded status, the funding plan, and a full component analysis. A platform that shows the balance but does not maintain the study structure cannot produce the disclosure California requires.

For communities in mandate states, the accounting module approach is not adequate for compliance. For communities in disclosure-only states, it may technically satisfy the current requirement but leaves them unprepared for Fannie Mae's 15% floor and for pending legislation that could add study mandates.

The tool your board uses for reserves should be built specifically for the reserve compliance problem, not adapted from general accounting.

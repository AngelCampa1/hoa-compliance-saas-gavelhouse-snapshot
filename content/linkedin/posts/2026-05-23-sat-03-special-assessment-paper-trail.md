A special assessment without a proper paper trail is an invitation for an owner lawsuit.

Special assessments are among the most contentious things a board can do. You're telling homeowners to pay an unexpected lump sum, often for something they believe should have been planned for. The only protection the board has is a clean record of the process.

What the paper trail needs to include:

**The rationale.** What failed or will fail? What are the repair or replacement costs? Why is the reserve fund insufficient?

**The board vote.** When was the assessment authorized? Who voted yes? Was there a quorum?

**Owner notification.** When were owners notified? How? What was the amount and the due date?

**Collection tracking.** Who paid, when, and in what amounts. Who has a payment plan in place. Who is delinquent and what steps the board has taken.

**Fund designation.** Is the assessment money going into the operating fund for an immediate repair, or into the reserve fund for a future replacement? The distinction matters for accounting and for owner questions.

Boards that go through a special assessment without this documentation are the ones who end up defending against owner complaints that the assessment was improper or the money was misused.

The paper trail is not bureaucracy. It's how the board shows it acted responsibly.

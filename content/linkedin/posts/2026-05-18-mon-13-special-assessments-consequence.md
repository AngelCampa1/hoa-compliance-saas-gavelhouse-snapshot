Special assessments are the bill that arrives when the reserve fund runs out.

The roof fails. The elevator needs replacement. The parking structure has a structural issue. The reserve fund has $40,000 and the repair costs $180,000.

The association has three options: borrow the money, defer the repair, or assess homeowners directly for the shortfall.

Special assessments can be $5,000 or $30,000 per home, payable in a lump sum or over months. They are not optional. Homeowners who can't pay face liens on their property.

This is the real consequence of underfunded reserves. Not a fine. Not a compliance violation. An unexpected bill that lands on every family in the community because the board never funded the reserve adequately.

Financial complexity is the number one reason volunteer boards hire professional management companies. When the special assessment arrives and homeowners start asking questions, that's when boards get replaced or resign.

The time to fix a reserve fund is before the project, not after.

Who bears the burden of the Fannie Mae reserve rule? The board does.

Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03, effective January 4, 2027, creates a 15% reserve allocation requirement for Full Review loan approvals. The lender asks the HOA to certify its financials. The HOA's board is responsible for those financials.

If a homeowner's sale falls through because the community fails the reserve check, the homeowner may look at who approved the budgets that kept reserves below 15%.

If a buyer cannot get conventional financing because the questionnaire shows inadequate allocation, and they can document it, the board that made those budget decisions is not insulated from the resulting questions.

Volunteer board members carry fiduciary duty to their communities. That duty has always included prudent reserve funding. LL-2026-03 makes the connection between underfunding and direct homeowner harm more traceable than it has ever been.

This is not legal advice. Gavelhouse is a compliance operating tool, not legal counsel. But it is worth understanding that every board vote to defer a reserve increase, after January 4, 2027, carries a documented consequence that did not exist before.

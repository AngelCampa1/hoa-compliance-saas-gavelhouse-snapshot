Florida has two different statutes governing reserve compliance: Chapter 718 for condos and Chapter 720 for HOAs. They have different requirements.

Chapter 718 (condos) includes the Structural Integrity Reserve Study requirement for buildings 3+ stories. Chapter 720 (HOAs) does not have the same SIRS requirement because it governs planned communities, not multi-story residential buildings.

Both chapters require separate accounting of reserve and operating funds (FL Stat. §718.111(14) for condos, §720.303 for HOAs). Enforcement differs: the DBPR Division of Condominiums can fine condo and co-op boards up to $5,000 per violation under §718.501(1)(d)(6) and Florida Admin Code Chapter 61B-21. Florida HOAs are not under DBPR jurisdiction; HOA enforcement is via member civil action.

The reserve waiver ban from HB 913 (2025) applies to structural components under both chapters, but the specific structural component definitions differ between condos and HOAs because the physical structures they govern differ.

Florida boards need to know which statute governs their community. A community that self-identifies as an "HOA" but actually has multi-unit residential buildings may be subject to Chapter 718 requirements. The community's governing documents and the nature of the ownership structure determine which chapter applies.

Getting this wrong matters because the Structural Integrity Reserve Study obligation under Chapter 718 has a hard deadline and a specific professional requirement. Missing it because the board thought Chapter 720 applied is not a defense.

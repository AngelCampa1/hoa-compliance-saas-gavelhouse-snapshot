Nevada NRS §116.31152 requires a reserve study every 5 years. No exemptions for small communities.

Nevada is a mandate state. The five-year update requirement applies to common-interest communities, and the results must be maintained and available for member inspection.

The Nevada statute sits alongside a broader framework that includes reserve disclosure requirements and restrictions on how reserve funds can be used. Reserve funds in Nevada are legally restricted to the purposes identified in the study.

That restriction matters because some boards treat reserves as a buffer for operating shortfalls. Using reserve funds for operating expenses is a violation, even if the board intends to replenish the funds later.

The "borrow and repay" approach that many small boards use informally runs into legal trouble in mandate states. Nevada, Florida, California, Maryland, and Washington all have statutory protections on reserve fund use that prohibit the informal borrowing approach.

If your Nevada community last did a reserve study more than five years ago, the update is already overdue. The statute does not provide a grace period for communities that have been deferring.

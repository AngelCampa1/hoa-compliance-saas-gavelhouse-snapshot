"We have $80,000 in reserves" sounds responsible until you ask the next question.

What components is that money supposed to cover?

A small community with modest common elements may be in decent shape. A larger association with aging roofs, pavement, elevators, or structural components may be badly underfunded with the same balance.

That is one reason this planning work keeps pointing to study cycles, component analysis, 30-year funding plans, and percent-funded status.

Reserve health is relative to the obligation.

Boards do not need to turn every meeting into an actuarial exercise. But they do need to stop treating the bank balance as the whole answer.

The reserve study gives the board the context. The budget has to carry that context forward.

58% of boards that hired professional management cited finances as the trigger.

Not governance conflicts. Not vendor management. Not homeowner relations. Finances.

Reserve fund management is the number one reason self-managed boards give up and hire a management company. The compliance requirements, the accounting complexity, the liability exposure, and the year-over-year burden become more than volunteers can manage alongside their regular lives.

When that happens, the community starts paying management company fees on top of their existing operating costs to solve a problem that was primarily about financial infrastructure.

The financial infrastructure problem can be solved with software. It doesn't require professional management if the right tools are in place.

Gavelhouse was built because that gap exists: the financial complexity that drives boards toward professional management is a compliance and tooling problem. Volunteer boards shouldn't need to hire management companies because their accounting software doesn't know what state they're in.

The trigger for 58% of board transitions is preventable.

Washington's condominium statute RCW §64.34.382 and the common interest community chapter RCW §64.90 require associations to conduct a reserve study at least every three years.

The study must include a 30-year funding plan.

Washington also prohibits commingling of reserve and operating funds under RCW 64.34.364. Separate reserve accounts are required.

A 30-year funding plan is a meaningful commitment. It requires the board to project component replacement costs over three decades, account for inflation and useful life remaining, and show a contribution schedule that keeps the association funded throughout that window.

Boards that commission a reserve study and receive a 30-year plan need to review that plan, adopt it formally, and then track actual contributions against the plan each year.

Receiving the plan is not the same as implementing it. Washington law requires both.

The three-year study cycle means the plan must be refreshed regularly. A 30-year plan from 2019 is five years stale and is not a current plan under the RCW.

Tennessee SB 863 (2023) created a reserve study requirement under the Tennessee Community Association Act. It did not create a funding mandate.

Tennessee boards can complete a reserve study showing the community is 8 percent funded, receive that finding, and technically satisfy SB 863.

The question is what fiduciary duty adds on top of the statute.

Fiduciary duty in Tennessee, as in every state, requires board members to act with reasonable care in managing the community's finances. A board that commissions a study, receives a finding of critical underfunding, and then does nothing is not acting with reasonable care, regardless of whether the statute required a specific response.

SB 863 does not provide a compliance shield for inadequate reserve funding. It creates a documentation trail. The study exists. The finding is in the study. The board's response, or lack of response, is in the minutes.

Tennessee homeowners who suffer financial harm from a board's failure to act on the study findings have a fiduciary duty claim that SB 863's minimal requirements do not foreclose.

Tennessee passed the weakest post-Surfside reform. That does not mean Tennessee boards have unlimited discretion to ignore their reserve study findings.

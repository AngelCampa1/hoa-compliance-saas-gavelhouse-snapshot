CINC Systems starts at $250/month minimum. Vantaca is $300 to $500+ per month. Both are quote-based.

Neither is built for self-managed volunteer boards.

These are enterprise platforms designed for professional management companies running dozens of communities with dedicated accounting staff. The feature sets, the onboarding requirements, the pricing structures, and the support models all assume professional management as the operating context.

A self-managed 80-unit HOA with a volunteer treasurer is not the buyer these platforms are designed for. The pricing alone puts them out of reach. The feature complexity assumes capabilities a volunteer board doesn't have.

This is not a criticism of CINC or Vantaca. Enterprise management software for professional management companies is a legitimate category. The problem is when a board is searching for HOA software and lands on platforms built for a different buyer entirely, evaluates them against their needs, and concludes the options are either too expensive or too complex.

The category gap is real: most enterprise HOA platforms are built for professional management. Most cheap tools lack compliance features. The middle ground for self-managed boards has historically been underserved.

Most reserve fund problems do not become visible on the day the mistake happens.

They show up later, when a roof project is approved, a lender asks questions, a homeowner challenges the budget, or a new treasurer tries to reconstruct what happened.

That is one reason the board record matters.

Meeting minutes, reserve study dates, funding decisions, operating and reserve balances, and month-end close records should tell the same story.

The goal is not to create paperwork for its own sake. The goal is to make board oversight provable.

Nevada Revised Statutes Chapter 116 creates one of the more closely regulated common-interest community frameworks in the western United States.

The Nevada Real Estate Division (NRED) has jurisdiction over common-interest communities and administers the reserve study requirement under NRS §116.31152.

The five-year reserve study cycle under §116.31152 is not a suggestion. NRED can receive complaints, investigate associations, and impose penalties on boards that are not meeting their statutory obligations.

Nevada's regulatory infrastructure means that a homeowner who believes the board has failed to conduct a reserve study, or has failed to fund reserves adequately, has an administrative avenue to pursue that complaint in addition to civil litigation.

For self-managed Nevada HOA boards, the NRED framework creates a level of accountability that does not exist in states where reserve complaints must go directly to court.

A board that is approaching or past the five-year study deadline should prioritize commissioning the update before a complaint is filed. The difference between being proactive and being reactive on a Nevada reserve study deadline is the difference between an administrative inconvenience and an investigation.

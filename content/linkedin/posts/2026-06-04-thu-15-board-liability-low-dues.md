Every board feels pressure to keep dues low.

That pressure is real. Homeowners have budgets too.

But reserve obligations do not disappear because a dues increase is unpopular. The compliance research points to states with study cycles, funding plans, percent-funded disclosures, baseline funding, separate-account rules, and penalties. It also points to Fannie Mae's 2027 reserve floor.

A board can be sympathetic and still be responsible.

The fiduciary question is not "did homeowners like the number?" The better question is whether the board reviewed the association's obligations and made a prudent funding decision with a clear record.

Sometimes the responsible decision is uncomfortable.

That is exactly why the record matters.

California and Washington both mandate 30-year reserve plans and both prohibit commingling. That is where the similarities mostly end.

California (Davis-Stirling Act):
Reserve study every 3 years, visual inspection annually. Percent-funded disclosure required annually. Commingling prohibited under Civil Code §5510, penalty $100-$500 per day. Three major reserve-related statutes passed in 2024-2025.

Washington (RCW §64.34.382):
Reserve study every 3 years. 30-year funding plan required. Separate reserve accounts mandatory under RCW 64.34.364. Applies to both condominiums (§64.34) and common interest communities (§64.90).

Key differences: California requires annual percent-funded disclosure. Washington does not have that specific annual disclosure obligation. California has higher commingling penalties per day. Washington's separate account requirement is explicit and bank-account-level.

For boards managing communities in either state, the common thread is this: both states require you to know your percent-funded status, maintain a long-range plan, and keep the reserve money physically separate from the operating money.

The compliance infrastructure those requirements demand is the same in both states, even if the specific mechanisms differ.

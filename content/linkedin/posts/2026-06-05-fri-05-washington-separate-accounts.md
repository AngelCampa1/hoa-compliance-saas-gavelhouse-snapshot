Gavelhouse tracks Washington as a mandate state with a reserve study at least every three years and a 30-year funding plan.

It also says RCW 64.34.364 requires separate reserve accounts.

That combination matters.

The board needs to know both the long-range reserve obligation and the account structure that keeps reserve money separate from operating money.

This is where a general "cash on hand" view can mislead directors. A healthy operating balance does not mean reserves are on track. A reserve account balance does not mean the 30-year plan is properly funded.

Washington boards need the separation and the plan visible at the same time.

That is a workflow problem, more than an accounting problem.

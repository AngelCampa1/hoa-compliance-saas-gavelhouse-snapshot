California boards often talk about "the reserve study" as if it is one recurring document.

The practical cadence is different for each requirement.

Under the Davis-Stirling framework, the full study happens every three years. The visual inspection happens annually. Associations also must disclose percent-funded status annually with a 30-year funding plan.

That means the board cannot treat the reserve file as something to pull out once every three years and ignore in between.

The annual piece is where operations matter. Did the component list change? Did the board approve a major repair? Did the funding plan still match the budget after dues were set?

Compliance is much easier when those questions are tracked during the year instead of reconstructed from meeting minutes later.

The most painful moment in HOA governance is inheriting the treasurer role from someone who kept everything in their personal email and a spreadsheet.

You get handed a folder, maybe a Google Sheet with inconsistent formatting, and an assurance that "it's all in here somewhere." Then you spend three months reconstructing records that should have taken an afternoon to hand over.

A clean treasurer handoff requires:

- A reconciled ledger through the last full month
- Attested balances for each prior month-end
- The homeowner roster with current dues status
- The reserve fund balance with a copy of the most recent reserve study
- Bank account access transferred (not just login credentials shared)
- Vendor contracts and payment schedules
- Pending items: delinquencies in process, open architectural requests, anything requiring follow-up

The outgoing treasurer should be able to walk away and the incoming treasurer should be able to close the following month without a phone call.

This is why centralized records matter. When everything lives in a shared system, the handoff is a permission change, not a document archaeology project.

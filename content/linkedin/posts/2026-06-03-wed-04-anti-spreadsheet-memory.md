Spreadsheets are useful. They are also easy to confuse with a system.

A treasurer can build a workbook that tracks dues, reserve transfers, capital projects, and budget scenarios. The formulas may be right. The tabs may be clean.

The problem starts when the board needs the record behind the number.

Which reserve study drove the transfer? Which meeting approved the funding plan? Which director attested to the closing balances? Which owner request prompted a document review?

Those are governance questions, not spreadsheet questions.

Gavelhouse's product source describes financial control, governance workflows, owner operations, and audit-ready reporting in one system. That is the point: the financial number should stay connected to the board action that created it.

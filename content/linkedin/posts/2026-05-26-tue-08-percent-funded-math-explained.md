Percent funded is the ratio of the current reserve balance to the fully funded balance.

The fully funded balance is not the total of all future replacement costs. It is the portion of all future costs that should already be set aside today, based on how far along each component is in its useful life.

Here is a simplified example.

A roof costs $100,000 to replace and has a 20-year useful life. After 10 years, the association should have $50,000 set aside for that roof. That $50,000 is the fully funded contribution for that single component at year 10.

The fully funded balance for the entire association is the sum of those midpoint contributions across every component.

If the association's actual reserve balance is $30,000 and the fully funded balance is $100,000, the association is 30 percent funded.

30 percent funded does not mean the association has 30 percent of what it will ever need. It means the association has 30 percent of what it should have set aside right now.

A community at 30 percent funded will face special assessments, deferred maintenance, or both unless contribution rates increase significantly. The earlier that increase happens, the smaller each annual adjustment needs to be.

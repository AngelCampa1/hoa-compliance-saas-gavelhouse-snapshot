QuickBooks cannot separate your operating fund from your reserve fund.

That is not a feature request. It is how the software is built. QuickBooks uses a single general ledger. Every workaround accountants use to simulate fund separation is a manual step on top of a manual step.

In California, commingling reserve and operating funds violates CA Civ. Code §5510. Fine: $100-$500 per day. In Florida, §720.303 prohibits it. Fine: $5,000 per violation. In Maryland, HB 292 (2025) requires mandatory reserve funding under Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2; enforcement runs through the Maryland AG.

These statutes do not care that you are using QuickBooks because your bookkeeper knows it. They care whether your funds are actually separated.

We built Gavelhouse because fund separation needed to be enforced at the database layer, not managed with accounting tricks. When a dues payment comes in, it lands in the correct fund automatically. There is no manual reconciliation step where the wrong account gets used.

If your board is still on QuickBooks, ask your accountant to show you the reserve account reconciliation. The answer is usually uncomfortable.

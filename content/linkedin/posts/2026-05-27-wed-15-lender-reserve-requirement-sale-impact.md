When a buyer applies for a conventional mortgage to purchase a home in an HOA community, the lender evaluates the association's financial health alongside the individual borrower's creditworthiness.

Fannie Mae's guidelines, effective January 4, 2027 under Lender Letter LL-2026-03, require associations to allocate at least 15 percent of the budget to reserves for loans processed under the Full Review path.

If the association's reserve allocation is below 15 percent, the loan cannot go through Full Review. The buyer is limited to more restrictive financing options, or the deal falls apart.

The board members who set the budget allocation did not consider this consequence when they voted to keep reserve contributions at six percent of the budget. But the homeowner who cannot close because the lender flagged the reserve shortfall will consider it.

HOA boards have fiduciary duties that run to all homeowners, including homeowners who want to sell. A board decision that materially impairs the ability of community members to transact on their homes is a fiduciary duty question, not just a financial management question.

The Fannie Mae threshold is objective and measurable. Boards should check the current reserve allocation percentage now and plan the budget adjustment needed to reach 15 percent before January 4, 2027.

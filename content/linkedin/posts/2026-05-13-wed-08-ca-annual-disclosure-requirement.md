California boards must disclose percent-funded status to every member, every year.

Civil Code §5550 requires the annual budget report to include the reserve study summary, the reserve funding plan, and the percent-funded status. This disclosure goes to every member of the association as part of the annual reporting cycle.

That means every California homeowner in a governed community receives a number: what percentage of anticipated replacement costs the association has saved. If the number is 15%, they can see it. If it is 80%, they can see that too.

SB 900 (2024), AB 2114 (2024), and SB 410 (2025) have continued to update and tighten the Davis-Stirling framework. California has been one of the most active state legislatures on HOA financial requirements over the past two years.

The study itself must be updated every three years, with a visual inspection annually. The annual inspection keeps the component condition data current between full study cycles.

For California boards, the disclosure obligation means the reserve program is not just a private board matter. It is an annual public record within the community. That changes the accountability dynamic significantly.

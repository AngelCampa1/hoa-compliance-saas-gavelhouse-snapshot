Before Florida SB 4-D (2022), condo associations could hold a vote to waive reserve funding. A majority of homeowners could effectively opt out of contributing to reserves for specific components.

SB 4-D eliminated the waiver for structural components covered under the Structural Integrity Reserve Study. For buildings three stories or taller, reserves for items like roofs, load-bearing walls, floors, foundations, and fireproofing must be funded. The waiver option is gone.

HB 913 (2025) kept this framework in place.

Florida condo boards that previously managed reserves by letting homeowners vote to waive contributions now face a mandatory funding obligation for the SIRS structural components. The budget must include those contributions. They cannot be voted away.

The practical impact is that Florida condo community budgets post-SB 4-D are higher than they were before. The reserve contribution line is no longer subject to the annual waiver vote for covered components.

Communities that have not yet adjusted their contribution levels to reflect the post-SB 4-D requirements are accumulating a compliance gap with every budget cycle that passes without the mandatory contributions.

The SIRS requirement also has a ten-year cycle for the study itself, with milestone inspections at 30 years (25 near the coast). Both obligations are active.

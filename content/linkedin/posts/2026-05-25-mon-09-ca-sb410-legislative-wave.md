California's Davis-Stirling Act (Civil Code §5550-5560) has always been among the most detailed reserve compliance frameworks in the country.

SB 900 (2024), AB 2114 (2024), and SB 410 (2025) added to that framework in a compressed legislative window.

California already required a reserve study every three years with a visual inspection annually, a 30-year funding plan disclosed to homeowners, and disclosure of percent-funded status each year. The recent legislative activity layered additional obligations on top of that baseline.

California also prohibits commingling of reserve and operating funds under Civil Code §5510. Penalties run from $100 to $500 per day.

Three significant reserve-related statutes in two years means California boards need to confirm their current compliance against the updated law, not against what was current in 2023.

The percent-funded disclosure is an annual obligation. If your California HOA has not updated that disclosure to reflect the most recent legislative changes, the gap is already in the record.

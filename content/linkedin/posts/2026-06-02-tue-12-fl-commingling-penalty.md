Florida gets a lot of attention for SIRS, milestone inspections, and the post-Surfside reserve reforms.

There is another rule boards should keep close: the requirement says Florida Statute section 720.303(6)(a) for HOAs and section 718.111(14) for condos prohibit commingling reserve and operating funds. It also lists a $5,000 violation penalty.

That means the board's accounting setup matters before the reserve study deadline arrives.

If reserve money and operating money are being managed as one pool with labels added later, the board is asking process discipline to do the work of fund controls.

That is fragile. It gets worse during turnover, emergency repairs, and rushed budget cycles.

Separate funds should stay separate in the workflow, not merely in the board's intent.

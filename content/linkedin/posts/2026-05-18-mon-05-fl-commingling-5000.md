Florida's DBPR can fine condo boards up to $5,000 per violation for mishandling reserve and operating funds.

FL Stat. §718.111(14) requires operating and reserve funds to be accounted for separately by condos (commingling is permitted only for joint investment, with separate accounting). FL Stat. §720.303 imposes the same separate-accounting duty on HOAs. The DBPR fine authority (§718.501(1)(d)(6) and Florida Admin Code Chapter 61B-21) covers condos and cooperatives; Florida HOAs are enforced through member civil action.

Post-Surfside, Florida also banned reserve waivers for structural components. Under SB 4-D (2022) and HB 913 (2025), boards that used to vote to waive reserves every year can no longer do that for structural items.

The legislative intent is clear: Florida wants funded reserves and it wants the money where you say it is.

For volunteer board members, this creates personal exposure. You signed the financial reports. If those reports showed a combined account balance that included both operating and reserve funds presented as if they were separate, the violation follows the signature.

"We use the same software we've always used" is not a legal defense.

Check your accounts. Check your governing documents. Check your software enforces the separation before your next audit.

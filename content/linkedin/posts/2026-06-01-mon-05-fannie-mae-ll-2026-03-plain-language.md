Fannie Mae published Lender Letter LL-2026-03. It changes mortgage eligibility for condos and HOAs. Here is what it actually says, without the policy language.

**The rule.** Starting January 4, 2027, when a lender submits a Full Review loan application for a unit in a condo or HOA, Fannie Mae will require the association to be allocating at least 15% of its total budgeted income to reserves.

**What "Full Review" means.** Full Review is the most common loan review type for condos. If a buyer in your community is getting a conventional mortgage, there is a good chance their lender is running a Full Review.

**What "total budgeted income" means.** This is the total of all assessments and fees your association collects in a year, as shown in the approved budget. The reserve line item must be at least 15% of that total.

**What happens if you are below 15%.** The lender cannot sell the loan to Fannie Mae. That typically means the buyer cannot get a conventional mortgage, or the loan terms get significantly worse.

**What this does not do.** It does not require a reserve study. It does not directly fine the association. It does not override state law. It only affects mortgage eligibility.

**What the treasurer should actually do.** Open your current approved budget. Find total assessments. Find the reserve line item. Divide. If the result is below 0.15, your community has a mortgage eligibility problem that your homeowners do not know about yet.

This is the gap that trips up self-managed boards.

General accounting tools can record payments, deposits, categories, and reports. They can be useful for bookkeeping.

But reserve compliance asks a different set of questions.

When was the last reserve study? What is the next required update date in this state? Does the study need a 30-year funding plan? Is percent-funded status part of the annual disclosure? Does the budget meet the funding rule?

Gavelhouse includes state-specific reserve study deadline tracking. That is not the same as recording the invoice for the reserve study vendor.

The board needs the deadline and the financial workflow connected.

Otherwise the accounting record can be accurate while the compliance calendar quietly slips.

Virginia Property Owners' Association Act §55.1-1826 has required a reserve study every five years for some time. HB 1209 (2024) clarified what that study must contain.

The study must include a component analysis and a funding plan. That is now explicit.

Component analysis requires itemizing every major common element, assessing its current condition, estimating its remaining useful life, and projecting its replacement cost.

Funding plan requires showing the contribution schedule that will allow the association to accumulate sufficient funds to cover each component replacement before the component fails.

A Virginia association that completes a reserve study that simply states a recommended reserve balance without doing the component analysis is not meeting the §55.1-1826 standard as clarified by HB 1209. The analysis must be itemized.

Why does this matter? Because a reserve study that works from a top-down recommended balance can look adequate even when individual components are severely underfunded. A detailed component analysis surfaces those individual shortfalls.

Virginia boards reviewing their last reserve study should confirm the study includes an itemized component list with condition assessments, remaining useful life, and replacement cost for each item.

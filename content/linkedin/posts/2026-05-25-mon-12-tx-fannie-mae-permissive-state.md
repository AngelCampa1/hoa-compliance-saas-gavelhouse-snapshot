Texas authorizes HOA reserve funds but does not require them. There is no Texas statute that mandates a reserve study, a funding plan, or a minimum percent-funded level.

Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03 takes effect January 4, 2027 and applies to every association regardless of state law.

The requirement: associations must allocate at least 15 percent of the budget to reserves for loans processed under the Full Review path. That threshold applies to Texas HOAs the same way it applies to HOAs in California or Florida.

When a homeowner in a Texas HOA tries to sell to a buyer using a conventional mortgage, the lender will pull the reserve allocation from the association's financials. If it is below 15 percent, the loan does not qualify for Full Review.

That is not a hypothetical risk. It is a policy with an effective date that is less than two years away.

Texas boards operating without a reserve fund or with reserves well below 15 percent of the budget have time to make corrections. But that window is closing.

Fiduciary duty does not disappear because state law is silent. And after January 4, 2027, the consequences will show up at closing.

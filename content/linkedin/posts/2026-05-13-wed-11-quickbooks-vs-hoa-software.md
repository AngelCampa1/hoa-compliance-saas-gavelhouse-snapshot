QuickBooks was built for small businesses. HOAs are not small businesses. The difference is fund separation.

A small business has one set of books. Revenue comes in, expenses go out, profit remains. The single-ledger model QuickBooks uses is exactly what a small business needs.

An HOA has two legally distinct funds that must never mix. Operating income covers operating expenses. Reserve contributions accumulate in a restricted capital account. The architecture required is not a single ledger with categories. It is two separate ledgers that cannot contaminate each other.

Florida Section 720.303, California Section 5510, Maryland Section 11B-112.2, New Jersey Section 46:8B-19, and Washington RCW 64.34.364 all require separate reserve accounts. The legal requirement is for fund separation, not just fund labeling.

QuickBooks can label. It cannot legally separate. The distinction matters because compliance is based on legal separation, not on whether the QuickBooks report shows different columns.

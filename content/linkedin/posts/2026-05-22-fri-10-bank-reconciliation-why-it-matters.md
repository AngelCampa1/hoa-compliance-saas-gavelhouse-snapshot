Skipping bank reconciliation for one month is a small risk. Skipping it for six months is how HOA fraud goes undetected.

Reconciliation is the process of comparing your bank statement to your internal ledger and confirming they match. Every line item. Every deposit. Every check cleared.

When boards skip it, several things happen:

Errors accumulate. A payment posted twice, a returned check never removed, a vendor paid from the wrong fund. Each one is small. Combined across months, they can represent thousands of dollars in misstatement.

Unauthorized transactions go unnoticed. Most HOA fraud cases involve small, repeated transactions that wouldn't trigger alarm in isolation. Monthly reconciliation catches the pattern.

The audit trail breaks down. When you finally do need to explain the books to an accountant or a disgruntled owner, a six-month gap in reconciliation records is a problem.

Reconciliation is not optional for boards that want to maintain credibility with their members. It's a basic fiduciary control.

One more thing: in states that require annual financial disclosures (California, Florida, and others), clean reconciliation records are the foundation of everything that goes into that disclosure.

D&O insurance does not protect board members from all reserve fund liability. Read the exclusions.

Directors and Officers policies typically cover negligent decisions. A board member who made a good-faith error in judgment is generally covered. A board member who knowingly violated a statute is generally not.

The distinction is significant in reserve fund compliance. If a Florida board votes to waive structural reserves after HB 913 (2025) banned the waiver, that is not a negligent error. It is a knowing violation of a specific prohibition. The D&O policy may not respond to claims arising from that decision.

Similarly, a board that commingles reserve and operating funds in a state where commingling is explicitly prohibited by statute, after receiving legal advice that the practice was non-compliant, is in a different position than a board that commingled funds out of ignorance.

D&O insurance is valuable coverage. The way to make it work as intended is to avoid creating situations where the intentional-violation exclusion applies.

The practical answer is clean financials, documented reserve compliance, and board decisions that follow the statute rather than work around it.

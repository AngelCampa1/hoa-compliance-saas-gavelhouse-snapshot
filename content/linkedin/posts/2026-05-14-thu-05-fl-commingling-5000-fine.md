$5,000 per violation. That is the maximum DBPR fine on Florida condo and co-op boards for improperly handling reserve and operating funds under §718.501(1)(d)(6) and Florida Admin Code Chapter 61B-21.

FL Stat. §718.111(14) requires condo reserves and operating funds to be accounted for separately. FL Stat. §720.303 requires the same for HOAs. The DBPR fine authority covers condos and cooperatives; HOAs are enforced through member civil action rather than DBPR fines.

"Per violation" means per incident. A single bank statement showing reserve contributions deposited into an operating account is one violation. A year of monthly deposits into the wrong account is potentially twelve violations. An audit covering three years can identify substantially more.

Florida moved comprehensively after Surfside. SB 4-D (2022) and HB 913 (2025) added the Structural Integrity Reserve Study requirement, the milestone inspection requirement, and the ban on reserve waivers for structural components. The separate-accounting requirement for reserve and operating funds was already in §718.111(14) before Surfside. The post-Surfside reforms made the enforcement environment sharper.

Florida boards that are still using a single bank account for both funds, or that are running accounting through QuickBooks without true fund separation, should treat that as an immediate compliance issue, not a future concern.

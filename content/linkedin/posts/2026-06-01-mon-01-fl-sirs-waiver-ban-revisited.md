Florida boards had a pressure release valve for years: put the reserve waiver on the annual ballot, get a majority vote, and defer funding for another year. Structurally deteriorating buildings and comfortably balanced operating accounts, all perfectly legal.

HB 913 (2025) closed that valve for structural components.

Under Chapter 718 and the Structural Integrity Reserve Study requirements, boards of condominiums and co-ops three stories or taller can no longer waive reserve contributions for components covered by a SIRS. Roof systems, load-bearing walls, foundations, fire safety equipment: these cannot be deferred by a membership vote anymore.

The practical impact hits treasurers immediately. A budget that passed last year with a "reserves waived" line item may be non-compliant this year. The study must be current (every 10 years under statute), and funding must follow the study's schedule.

If your condo is in Florida and has not updated its reserve study since the SIRS requirement took effect, the clock is running. The study tells you what is owed. The statute now says you cannot vote your way out of paying it.

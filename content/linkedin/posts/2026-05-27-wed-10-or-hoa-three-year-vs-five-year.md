Oregon Revised Statutes §100.175 requires associations to conduct a reserve study every three to five years.

The statute does not define which communities require the three-year cycle versus the five-year cycle. The determining factors are generally the community's complexity, the number and diversity of reserve components, and the rate at which conditions change.

A larger condominium building with elevators, mechanical systems, a pool, and underground parking will typically need a more frequent update cycle than a small townhome community with roofs and pavement. The professional reserve analyst who conducts the study typically recommends the appropriate update frequency in the study itself.

Oregon also requires the study to include a funding plan. A board that is already at the five-year mark since the last study, or approaching it, should not be choosing between "do the study" and "don't do the study." The question is whether to do a full Level 1 study or an update.

The funding plan requirement means the update, whatever level it is conducted at, must still produce a documented contribution schedule. An update that refreshes cost estimates but does not revise the funding plan is not a complete update under ORS §100.175.

What makes Gavelhouse different from QuickBooks? One thing that matters more than the rest.

QuickBooks cannot separate operating and reserve funds at the database layer. It is a general-purpose accounting tool built for businesses with a single pool of money. An HOA has two: operating funds and reserve funds. Those are not the same pool, and in several states, mixing them is a statutory violation.

California: up to $500 per day under CA Civ. Code §5510.
Florida: $5,000 per violation under FL Stat. §718.111(14).
Maryland: up to $10,000 under Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2.
New Jersey: the balance can never drop below zero under N.J.S.A. 46:8B-19.

QuickBooks does not enforce this separation. A board can create separate accounts as a workaround, but if one person mistakenly posts a transaction to the wrong account, nothing stops it. The enforcement is manual and operator-dependent.

Gavelhouse enforces the separation structurally. Reserve contributions post to the reserve fund. Operating expenses post to operating. A transaction that does not belong to the right fund does not silently flow through.

That is the core difference. Not a feature list. A structural compliance guarantee that QuickBooks was never designed to provide.

If your community is currently on QuickBooks, does your board know what a state commingling audit would show?

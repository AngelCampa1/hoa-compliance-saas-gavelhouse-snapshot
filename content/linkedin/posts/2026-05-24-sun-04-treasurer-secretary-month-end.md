Month-end close is the treasurer's task. But the secretary owns half the inputs.

Before the treasurer can close the books, they need several things that typically live with the secretary or in the meeting records:

**Approved meeting minutes.** If the board authorized an expenditure in last month's meeting, that authorization needs to be in the system before the expense is posted. Minutes that haven't been approved are a gap in the audit trail.

**Architectural request decisions.** Any new approvals or denials from the prior month that have associated fees or deposits need to be posted.

**New owner transfers.** If a unit changed hands, the dues account needs to reflect the new owner before the next billing cycle.

**Open violations with associated fees.** If the board assessed a violation fee last month, the secretary's records and the dues register need to match.

The boards that run month-end close cleanly are the ones where the treasurer and secretary have a shared checklist. The boards that run it late or with gaps are the ones where each officer treats their responsibilities as independent.

Month-end is not the treasurer alone closing a spreadsheet. It's the point where all the prior month's activity gets reconciled into one accurate picture of where the association stands.

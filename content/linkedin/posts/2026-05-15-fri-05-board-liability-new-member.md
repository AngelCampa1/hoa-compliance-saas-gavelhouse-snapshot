New board members inherit the fiduciary duty from day one. The reserve compliance problems that existed before you joined are now your responsibility too.

Joining a volunteer HOA board is an act of community service. It is also a legal role. Board members owe homeowners a fiduciary duty, which means they are required to act with care, loyalty, and reasonable prudence in managing community assets.

If the board you joined has commingled reserve and operating funds for three years, that pre-existing problem does not belong only to the previous treasurer. As a current board member, you have an obligation to identify material compliance issues and take corrective action.

"I was not on the board when that happened" is a less effective defense than "I identified the problem when I joined, documented it, and the board took corrective steps."

The practical checklist for a new board member joining a self-managed community: request a current reserve study, confirm separate reserve and operating accounts exist, review the last audit or financial review, and check whether the state-required disclosures have been made.

That review takes a few hours. The liability exposure from skipping it can be significant.

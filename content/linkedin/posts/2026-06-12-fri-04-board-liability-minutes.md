Minutes do not need to be dramatic. They need to be useful.

When reserve funding is on the agenda, the record should show what the board reviewed: current reserve balance, study status, next required update date, planned contributions, major upcoming components, and any state-specific rule that applies.

That record helps later.

If an owner challenges the budget or a future board asks why dues changed, the minutes should point to the reserve facts the board had at the time.

That is basic fiduciary hygiene.

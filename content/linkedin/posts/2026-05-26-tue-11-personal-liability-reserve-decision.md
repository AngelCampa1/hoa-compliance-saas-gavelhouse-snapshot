---

Personal liability attaches when a board member makes a decision that breaches the fiduciary duty of care and that decision causes quantifiable harm to homeowners.

For reserve fund decisions, the triggering scenarios are concrete.

A board that receives a reserve study showing the community is critically underfunded and then votes to maintain current (inadequate) contribution rates has made a documented decision to accept the underfunded status. That decision is in the minutes. The study is in the records.

When the roof fails four years later and the association cannot pay for it without a large special assessment, the homeowners who paid that assessment can point directly to the board's 2022 decision.

The business judgment rule provides some protection. A board acting in good faith, on the basis of adequate information, and in the best interests of the association is generally protected. But "adequate information" requires actually having the information. A board that did not have a current reserve study when it made the contribution rate decision did not have adequate information.

The protection is procedural. Document the study. Document the options. Document why you chose the path you chose. The absence of that documentation removes the protection.

Compliance-first in marketing copy is easy. Compliance-first in code is a different commitment.

Any software company can write "compliance-first" in their headline. The question is what it means in the actual product.

In Gavelhouse, compliance-first means:

The database schema rejects transactions that would violate fund separation. Not a warning. A rejection.

The reserve tracking module knows your state's study cycle and funding requirements. Not a reference link. Actual enforcement logic.

Reports are generated in formats that satisfy state disclosure requirements. Not exported data you format yourself. Compliant output.

Alerts fire when contribution rates fall below statutory minimums. Not when you think to check. Automatically.

Building this way cost us development time on features that boards see and click first. Dues collection workflows, communication tools, the homeowner portal: those are the features boards mention in the first conversation. Compliance infrastructure is the thing they don't think about until something goes wrong.

We built it anyway because the whole point of the product is to protect boards from outcomes they don't see coming.

"Compliance-first" is a claim anyone can make. The architectural decisions are where you find out if the claim is real.

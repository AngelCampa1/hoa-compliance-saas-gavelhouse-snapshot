What does "attested balance" mean and why does your HOA need one every month?

Attestation is the act of a responsible board member reviewing the financial reports and signing off that the numbers are accurate to the best of their knowledge. It's not an audit. It's not a legal certification. It's a documented checkpoint.

Why it matters:

When board members change, an attested balance gives the incoming treasurer a verified starting point. Without it, they're trusting a spreadsheet handed over by someone who may have made errors or, in the worst case, hidden them.

When an owner disputes a charge, an attested monthly balance is contemporaneous evidence that the books were in good order at the time.

When an accountant reviews the year, attested month-end records show that the board was paying attention throughout the year, not just at year-end.

Attestation takes five minutes at the end of month-end close. The boards that skip it consistently are the ones who end up scrambling when someone asks a hard question.

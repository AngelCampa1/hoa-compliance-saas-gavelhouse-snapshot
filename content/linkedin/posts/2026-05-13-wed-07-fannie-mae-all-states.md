Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 applies to every association in every state. State law is irrelevant to this particular deadline.

The 15% reserve allocation floor for Full Review loan applications takes effect January 4, 2027. A community in Texas, which has no state-level reserve study mandate, is subject to the same Fannie Mae requirement as a community in California, which mandates studies every three years.

The floor is a lending requirement, not a state compliance requirement. Lenders ordering a Full Review questionnaire will check the reserve allocation. If it falls below 15%, the loan does not meet Fannie Mae eligibility criteria.

The impact is on homeowners trying to sell. A buyer who needs a conforming Fannie Mae loan cannot close if the association does not meet the reserve threshold. The seller, not the board, bears the immediate consequence. But the board created the condition.

Communities that are well-funded (above 30-40% funded) will clear the 15% bar comfortably. Communities hovering near zero are the ones at risk.

The time to address this is before January 4, 2027, not after a sale falls through.

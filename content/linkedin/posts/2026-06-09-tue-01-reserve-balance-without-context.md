"We have money in reserves" is not enough.

How much is required by the study? What components are coming due? When was the study updated? What funding path did the board approve? Is the balance separate from operating cash?

A reserve balance by itself can look comforting while the plan underneath is weak.

That is one reason Gavelhouse treats reserve tracking as part of the operating system, more than a number on a report.

Boards need the balance, the deadline, the components, and the funding context together.

Otherwise the meeting hears a number and misses the risk.

Illinois Condominium Property Act (765 ILCS 605) requires associations to disclose reserve fund status to homeowners.

The current law requires disclosure. It does not require a formal reserve study or a minimum funding level.

Pending legislation HB 2563 and SB 1703 would change that. Both bills propose moving Illinois from a disclosure-only requirement to a mandate with formal study obligations.

If either bill passes, Illinois condo boards that have been making disclosures based on informal estimates rather than a professional reserve study will find themselves out of compliance with the new law from day one.

The practical risk for Illinois boards right now is that disclosure without a formal study creates an accuracy problem. A board that discloses reserve fund status based on incomplete records is still making a representation to homeowners. If that representation is materially wrong, the exposure follows.

Illinois boards should not wait for HB 2563 or SB 1703 to pass before getting a current reserve analysis. The disclosure obligation exists now. A formal study gives you defensible numbers to support that disclosure.

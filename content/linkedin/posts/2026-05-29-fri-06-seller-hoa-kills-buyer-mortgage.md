A seller lists their unit. The buyer gets financing. The HOA kills the deal.

Not because the buyer was unqualified. Not because the property appraised low. Because the HOA could not show 15% reserve allocation.

This is the scenario Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03 creates, effective January 4, 2027. A seller has zero control over the reserve allocation decisions the board made years ago. But those decisions will appear on the HOA questionnaire a lender requests during the Full Review process.

If the allocation is below 15%, the lender cannot approve the conventional loan. The buyer either walks or pays cash. The seller takes a discount or relists.

This is not theoretical. The questionnaire requirement is already standard. LL-2026-03 simply adds a hard floor to what the questionnaire must show.

Volunteer boards often view reserve funding as an internal financial question. The Fannie Mae rule makes it a community-wide property value question.

Every homeowner has a stake in whether the board is contributing enough to reserves. Most of them do not know it yet.

Does your community explain reserve funding to homeowners annually? If not, that conversation is worth having before January 4, 2027.

Colorado and Nevada both require reserve studies every 5 years. If your community has not updated since 2020, you are past the window.

Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act §38-33.3-209.5 and Nevada NRS §116.31152 set parallel five-year study requirements. Both states are mandate states.

Colorado's requirement applies above an assessment threshold and requires disclosure of reserve balance and percent-funded status to members. Nevada's requirement applies to common-interest communities and the study must be maintained and available for member inspection.

The five-year cycle is longer than California's three-year cycle and Washington's three-year cycle, but it is a maximum gap, not a minimum. Communities in Colorado and Nevada can update their studies more frequently, and many should, particularly in communities with older buildings where component conditions can change meaningfully in five years.

Both states share the same Fannie Mae exposure as every other state. Lender Letter LL-2026-03 does not calibrate its 15% floor to state study cycle frequency. It simply requires 15% reserve allocation, effective January 4, 2027.

A Colorado or Nevada community that last did a study in 2020 is at the outer edge of compliance. Updating the study before 2027 would confirm the current reserve allocation figure that Fannie Mae lenders will be asking about.

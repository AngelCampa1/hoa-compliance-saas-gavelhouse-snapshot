Delaware Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act §81-318 requires associations to conduct a reserve study at least every five years and to include a funding plan in that study.

Many boards conflate the reserve study and the funding plan. They are different things.

The reserve study is the analytical document. It identifies components, assesses their condition, estimates remaining useful life and replacement cost, and calculates the current funded status.

The funding plan is the operational document. It prescribes the contribution schedule the association must follow, year by year, to reach and maintain an adequate funded level across the full planning horizon.

A reserve study without a funding plan tells the board what the problem is. The funding plan tells the board what to do about it.

Delaware §81-318 requires both. A board that commissions a reserve study and does not adopt a formal funding plan has completed the analysis and skipped the governance step the statute actually requires.

The funding plan must be adopted by the board and reflected in the annual budget and assessment structure. It is not a document that sits in a file. It is the financial commitment the board makes on behalf of the community.

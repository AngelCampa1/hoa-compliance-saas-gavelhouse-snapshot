The path from volunteer board member to personal defendant is shorter than most people realize.

It doesn't require malicious intent. It doesn't require knowingly stealing from the association. It can happen through:

- Approving budgets year after year that don't fund reserves to the required level
- Inheriting a QuickBooks setup that commingles funds without knowing the accounts are structurally combined
- Skipping the required reserve study because the cost seemed unnecessary
- Signing financial disclosures that show a percent-funded level below the state's required floor

Each of those is a fiduciary decision. Collectively, they create a documented pattern showing the board was aware of, or should have been aware of, the compliance obligations and did not satisfy them.

When a homeowner files a complaint or retains an attorney, the question is not whether the board intended harm. The question is whether the board exercised the care and diligence required of a fiduciary.

"We used QuickBooks and didn't know" is an explanation of how the failure happened, not a defense against having allowed it to happen.

The volunteer status matters for sympathy. It does not override fiduciary duty.

Compliance software creates the documentation trail that shows the board did its job. That trail is the actual defense.

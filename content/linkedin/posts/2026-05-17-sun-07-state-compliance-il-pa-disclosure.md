Illinois and Pennsylvania are disclosure states. The absence of a study mandate does not mean reserves do not matter.

Illinois under the Condo Property Act (765 ILCS 605) requires reserve fund status disclosure. Pennsylvania under the Uniform Planned Community Act (68 Pa.C.S. §5304) requires reserve information in public offering statements.

Neither state mandates a formal reserve study. Boards in both states can technically comply with current law by disclosing the reserve balance without a study backing it up.

That compliance gap creates two problems. First, without a study, the disclosed balance has no context. A board disclosing $50,000 in reserves cannot say whether that is 20% funded or 80% funded without the component analysis a study provides.

Second, the Fannie Mae 15% floor does not care that Illinois and Pennsylvania have not yet mandated studies. Starting January 4, 2027, the Full Review questionnaire will ask for the reserve allocation percentage. Without a study, Illinois and Pennsylvania boards may not have a credible answer.

HB 2563 / SB 1703 (Illinois) and the broader legislative trends suggest both states are moving toward mandate status. Boards in disclosure states that invest in a reserve study now will be ahead of the curve when that shift happens.

A treasurer handoff that skips the reserve study is incomplete.

The incoming treasurer needs to know which study is current, when it was completed, which components it covers, what funding plan it recommends, and when the next update is due under the state rule.

Without that, the new treasurer can reconcile the bank account and still miss the reserve obligation.

The compliance research shows why this matters. Study cycles vary by state. Some states require 30-year funding plans. Some require percent-funded disclosure. Fannie Mae's 2027 rule adds another reserve allocation question.

The handoff should not depend on the outgoing treasurer giving a perfect verbal tour.

Put the study, deadline, balance, funding plan, and board approvals where the next person can actually use them.

State mandates set floors, not best practices.

Utah: every 6 years. Delaware: every 5 years. Nevada: every 5 years. Colorado: every 5 years. Virginia: every 5 years. Oregon: every 3-5 years. California: every 3 years. Washington: every 3 years.

The question is not what the statute allows. The question is what keeps the funding plan accurate.

Three factors erode the accuracy of a reserve study over time:

Construction cost inflation. Labor and materials prices shift. A study that used 2021 replacement cost estimates is understating today's replacement costs. Each year that passes increases this gap.

Completed expenditures. If the association replaced the parking lot last year, that component is no longer at the same remaining useful life it was when the study was conducted. The plan needs updating.

Actual contribution shortfalls or surpluses. If the community did not collect the contributions the plan required, the reserve balance is lower than projected. The plan is already off-track.

Annual review of the reserve contribution rate against the plan, even in years between full study updates, is a governance practice that keeps the board aligned with the financial reality.

In states with longer statutory cycles, voluntary mid-cycle updates save money compared to discovering a large gap at the next required study.

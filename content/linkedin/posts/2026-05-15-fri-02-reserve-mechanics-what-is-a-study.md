What is a reserve study? A plain-language explanation for new board members.

A reserve study is a financial planning document for your community's major physical assets. It answers three questions:

What does the community own that will eventually need to be replaced? (The component inventory.) What will those replacements cost? (The replacement cost analysis.) How much should we be saving each month to have that money ready when we need it? (The funding plan.)

The study is typically prepared by a licensed reserve specialist who physically inspects the community's components, estimates remaining useful life for each, and projects replacement costs over a 30-year horizon.

The result is a funding recommendation: the monthly or annual amount the board should be collecting in reserve contributions to reach adequate funding by the time each component needs replacement.

States have different requirements for how often the study must be updated: every three years in California and Washington, every five years in Colorado and Nevada. Florida requires the Structural Integrity Reserve Study specifically for condos 3+ stories every ten years.

If your community has never had a reserve study, the first one is the most important. It establishes the baseline that all future planning builds from.

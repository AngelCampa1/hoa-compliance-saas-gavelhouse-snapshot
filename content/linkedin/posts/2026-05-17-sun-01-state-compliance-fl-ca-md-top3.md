Three states, three penalty structures. If your community is in Florida, California, or Maryland, here is what you are exposed to.

Florida. FL Stat. §718.111(14) (condos) and §720.303 (HOAs) prohibit commingling. Penalty: $5,000 per violation. Reserve waivers for structural components are banned under HB 913 (2025).

California. CA Civ. Code §5510 prohibits commingling. Penalty: $100-$500 per day. Davis-Stirling Act §5550-5560 requires a reserve study every three years with annual visual inspections and 30-year funding plan disclosure.

Maryland. Maryland HB 292 (2025, Chapter 519, effective Oct 1, 2025) requires associations to fund reserves per the most recent reserve study under Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2. HB 107 (2022) mandated the reserve study itself. Enforcement runs through the Maryland AG Consumer Protection Division.

All three states require the same structural element: separate reserve and operating accounts. The penalty for getting it wrong varies, but in all three states the penalty is significant enough that the cost of compliance is trivially small by comparison.

A separate bank account and accounting software that enforces the separation. That is the baseline. Everything else in reserve compliance builds from there.

Your HOA board can unintentionally make it harder for homeowners in your community to sell their homes.

Starting January 4, 2027, Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03 requires that communities meet a 15% reserve allocation for Full Review loan applications. If a community doesn't meet the threshold, the buyer's conventional mortgage application is flagged as non-conforming.

Lenders who can't sell loans into the secondary market either decline to lend, price in a risk premium, or require the buyer to put more down.

For homeowners trying to sell in an underfunded community, this shrinks the buyer pool. Buyers who need conventional financing can't get approved. The community becomes harder to transact in. Property values are affected.

The board didn't intend this outcome. They just never funded reserves adequately. From the buyer's lender's perspective, that's the same outcome either way.

The 2027 deadline is under a year away. Communities that aren't at 15% allocation today need a plan. A reserve study that documents the current state and a contribution schedule that reaches compliance is the starting point.

Boards that do nothing have made a decision that affects every homeowner trying to sell.

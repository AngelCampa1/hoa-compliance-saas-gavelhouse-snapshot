Hawaii HRS §514B-148 requires condominium associations to maintain replacement reserves of at least 50 percent of the estimated replacement reserves assessment, or fund 100 percent under a cash-flow funding plan.

Act 62 (2022) updated the 30-year horizon and reserve study framework. Act 296 (2025, SB 1044) established a state-level Condominium Loan Program to help associations finance essential repairs and deferred maintenance. It does not itself set the 50 percent floor; that obligation lives in §514B-148.

50 percent funded means the association holds at least half of the estimated replacement reserves on its component schedule.

For context: the 50 percent floor sits well above what most self-managed associations currently maintain. In Hawaii, falling below the §514B-148 threshold is a statutory violation, not a planning choice.

If your Hawaii condo board has not updated its reserve study and funding plan recently, the plan may be out of compliance with §514B-148. Act 296 also opens a financing path for communities that need to catch up.

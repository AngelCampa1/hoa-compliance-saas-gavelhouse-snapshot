Pennsylvania requires reserve information in every public offering statement for a planned community.

The Uniform Planned Community Act (68 Pa.C.S. §5304) mandates that reserve fund information appear in the public offering statement for any new or resale unit in a planned community. A buyer receiving a public offering statement is entitled to see the reserve picture before they close.

Pennsylvania is a disclosure state, meaning there is no statutory mandate for a formal reserve study. But the disclosure obligation means that whatever reserve information the board has, accurate or not, becomes part of the legal transaction document.

A board that has not done a reserve study, or has not updated one recently, will disclose a reserve position that may not reflect actual component conditions or replacement cost estimates. That creates liability exposure if a buyer later claims the disclosed information was materially misleading.

The path of least exposure is having a current, credible reserve study that backs up whatever numbers appear in the public offering statement.

The Fannie Mae 15% floor, effective January 4, 2027, will also affect Pennsylvania communities. Disclosure of a low reserve position in the offering statement, combined with a Fannie Mae shortfall, creates compounding problems for community sales.

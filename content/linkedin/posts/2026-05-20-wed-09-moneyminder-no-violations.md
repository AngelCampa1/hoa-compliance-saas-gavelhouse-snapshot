MoneyMinder is low cost. It also has no violation tracking.

For some boards in states with minimal reserve requirements and simple financials, a low-cost basic accounting tool may be appropriate. Not every community is in California with a 30-year reserve plan requirement.

But here's what "no violation tracking" means in practice for boards that need it:

No flag when an account balance drops below the statutory reserve floor. No alert when the reserve study cycle deadline is approaching. No record of whether the board reviewed reserve fund status at each meeting. No audit trail that connects financial decisions to board approvals.

When something goes wrong, the board needs to demonstrate that it exercised reasonable diligence. A tool that doesn't track whether the board was tracking compliance can't produce that evidence.

Low-cost tools are fine for what they do. The problem is when a board in Maryland, California, or Florida uses a basic accounting tool, assumes it covers their compliance obligations, and discovers the gap during an audit.

The question isn't whether the tool is cheap enough. It's whether the tool covers what your state actually requires.

Know your state requirements before selecting your software. gavelhouse.app/free/ has the state-by-state reference.

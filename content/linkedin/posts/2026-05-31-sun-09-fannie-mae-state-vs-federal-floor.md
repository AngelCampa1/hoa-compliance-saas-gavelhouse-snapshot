State reserve law and Fannie Mae's rule are different requirements. Both apply after January 4, 2027.

State reserve study mandates typically require an association to periodically commission a reserve study, maintain a plan for funding components over their useful lives, and in some states, document what percentage of full funding the community has reached.

Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03 does not care about your reserve study or your percent-funded score. It asks a simpler question: are you allocating at least 15% of your budget to reserves right now?

These two tests can give different answers for the same community.

A community with a comprehensive, state-compliant reserve study showing 75% funded could still fail the Fannie Mae check if this year's budget only allocates 10% of assessments to reserves. Maybe contributions were reduced during a tight budget year. Maybe the study was done when the reserve balance was high and the board reduced the allocation as a result.

A community without a state-mandated reserve study could pass the Fannie Mae check simply by having a budget that directs 20% to reserves.

After January 4, 2027, boards need to know both numbers: their state compliance status and their Fannie Mae allocation percentage. They are not the same question.

HOALife relies on QuickBooks for accounting.

So does Vinteum. Several other HOA platforms work the same way: they manage communications, documents, and portals, then hand off financials to a QuickBooks integration.

Here's what that means in practice:

You're paying for two subscriptions. And you still have the commingling problem.

QuickBooks does not enforce separation between operating and reserve funds. It lets you create accounts with different names. It does not prevent a transaction from being posted to the wrong fund. It does not reject a journal entry that would commingle funds. The separation is entirely dependent on whoever is entering data and whether they know the rules.

A board that switched from spreadsheets to "HOA software plus QuickBooks" made an improvement in organization. They did not fix the compliance gap. The liability exposure for commingling exists whether the mistake happened in a spreadsheet or a QuickBooks account.

State statutes don't ask what tool you used. They ask whether the funds were separate.

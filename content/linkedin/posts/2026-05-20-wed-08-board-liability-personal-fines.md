Personal liability means the fine comes to you. Not to the association. To you, personally.

This is the part that surprises many volunteer board members when they first understand it.

When a state imposes a commingling penalty, the question of who pays it depends on the circumstances. If the violation is organizational, the association pays. If it's personal misconduct, the individual board members who authorized the improper transactions may pay.

Maryland's $10,000 fine under §11B-112.2, California's $100-$500 daily penalty under §5510, Florida's $5,000 per violation under §720.303: these are statutory penalties that can attach to the individuals responsible, not just to the association.

D&O insurance may cover some of this. "May" and "some" are the operative words.

Volunteer boards often treat liability as an abstract concern. It becomes concrete when a homeowner files a complaint, when an attorney sends a demand letter, or when a state regulatory body opens an inquiry.

The board members who signed the financial reports during the period of the violation are the ones who need to explain what they knew and what they did about it.

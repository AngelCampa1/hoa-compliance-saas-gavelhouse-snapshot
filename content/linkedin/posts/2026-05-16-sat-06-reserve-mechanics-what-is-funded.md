100% funded does not mean you have enough money for everything at once. It means you have saved the right proportion for where each component is in its life cycle.

This is the most common misconception about percent-funded status.

A 100% funded community does not have enough money to replace every roof, every elevator, and every parking lot today. It has saved the right fraction of each component's replacement cost, proportional to how far that component is through its useful life.

A 10-year-old roof with a 20-year life that costs $200,000 to replace should have $100,000 in reserves allocated to it: 10/20 of the total cost, because it is halfway through its useful life. A 100% funded community has exactly $100,000 saved for that roof, not $200,000.

This is why California's annual percent-funded disclosure is meaningful. A community at 50% funded has saved about half of what it should have at this point in each component's life cycle. On average, it is behind by one half of the reserve requirement.

A community at 30% funded is significantly underfunded. It has saved less than a third of what the reserve study says it should have at this point.

The Fannie Mae 15% floor is a completely different metric (allocation as a percentage of budget), but communities at 30% funded are usually allocating well above 15% of their budget to reserves. The problem cases are the communities close to zero.

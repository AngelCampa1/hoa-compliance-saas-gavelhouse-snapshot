Your state doesn't mandate a reserve study. Your community still has a January 2027 deadline to think about.

Some states allow HOA boards to opt out of reserve requirements with an owner vote, or simply have no reserve statute at all. In those states, the board has discretion to fund reserves at whatever level it chooses, including zero.

That discretion doesn't extend to Fannie Mae.

Lender Letter LL-2026-03 sets a 15% reserve floor effective January 4, 2027, and it applies to every community where buyers seek conventional financing. It's not a state rule. It's a secondary mortgage market rule.

For communities in permissive states that have never formally tracked their percent-funded status, 2027 creates a new exposure that didn't exist before.

The "50-State HOA Reserve Fund Requirements Guide" includes a section on Fannie Mae's requirements and what they mean for communities with no existing state mandate. It's the clearest explanation of how the federal layer works in the absence of state requirements.

If your board hasn't discussed reserve funding because "our state doesn't require it," this is the document to bring to the next meeting.

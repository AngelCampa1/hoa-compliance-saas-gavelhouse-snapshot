When you accept the treasurer role on a volunteer HOA board, you accept a fiduciary duty to the community's homeowners.

That duty has three components. The duty of care: act with reasonable diligence and inform yourself before making financial decisions. The duty of loyalty: put the association's interests above your own. The duty of obedience: act within the governing documents and applicable law.

For reserve fund management, the duty of care is most directly in play. A volunteer treasurer who fails to conduct a required reserve study, fails to fund the reserve account according to a plan, or allows operating funds to be commingled with reserve funds has failed the duty of care.

The volunteer defense has limits. Courts have held that "I didn't know" is not a shield when the information was available and the board member had an obligation to seek it out.

This is not hypothetical risk. Homeowners who suffer financial harm from an underfunded reserve, a special assessment that could have been avoided, or a commingling violation have standing to sue board members individually.

D&O insurance may provide coverage. It may not. What it cannot do is retroactively close the compliance gap that created the exposure.

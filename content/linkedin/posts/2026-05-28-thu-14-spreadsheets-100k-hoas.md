Over 100,000 self-managed HOAs still manage their finances on spreadsheets and general-purpose accounting software.

Spreadsheets do not enforce account separation. A formula error or a manual entry mistake can quietly move money between what is labeled "reserves" and what is labeled "operations." There is no system-level barrier.

Spreadsheets do not calculate percent-funded status. The board must build the model, maintain the component inventory, and update the remaining useful life figures every year. That maintenance rarely happens.

Spreadsheets do not track study deadlines. Colorado's five-year cycle, California's three-year cycle, and Nevada's five-year cycle are not calendar entries in a spreadsheet formula.

The financial complexity that flows from reserve fund compliance, component tracking, funded status calculation, commingling prevention, and study cycle management is one of the primary reasons volunteer boards hire professional management companies. Fifty-eight percent of boards that made the switch cited finances as the trigger. That transition typically costs $15,000 to $40,000 per year for a small community.

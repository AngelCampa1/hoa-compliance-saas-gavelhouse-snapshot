New Jersey and Nevada both mandate reserve compliance, but their approaches are different. Here is how.

Nevada mandates a reserve study every five years under NRS §116.31152. The study must be maintained and available for inspection. Nevada's approach is study-centered: the requirement is to perform and maintain the analysis.

New Jersey's approach under the Condominium Act (N.J.S.A. 46:8B) is balance-centered: separate accounts are required under §46:8B-19, and the balance can never drop below zero under S2760 (2024) and S3992 (2025).

New Jersey does not have a study mandate equivalent to Nevada's. It has an account structure mandate and a balance floor. A New Jersey community could technically comply with the letter of the law by maintaining a separate account with a positive balance and no formal study.

That approach leaves the community unprepared for the Fannie Mae 15% floor and unable to answer accurately what its reserve allocation percentage is, but it satisfies the current New Jersey statutory requirements.

Nevada's study mandate produces the documentation needed for Fannie Mae, but only if the study is current and the community is actually following the funding plan it contains.

Both states require the same end result: adequate reserves. They just regulate different pieces of the path.

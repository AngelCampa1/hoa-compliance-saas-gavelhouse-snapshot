A spreadsheet with two tabs is not fund separation under any state statute.

Over 100,000 self-managed HOAs still run on spreadsheets and QuickBooks. Both tools have the same architectural problem: there is no technical barrier stopping funds from being mixed. A formula error, a copy-paste into the wrong column, or a bank transfer to the wrong account creates a commingling violation.

Maryland's §11B-112.2 requires separate accounts. Florida's §720.303 prohibits commingling. California's §5510 prohibits it. Washington's RCW 64.34.364 requires separate reserve accounts.

"Separate account" means a separate bank account, not a separate spreadsheet column.

The violation is not tracked to when the mistake was discovered. It is tracked to when the commingling occurred. An audit that finds three years of mixed deposits finds three years of violations.

We built Gavelhouse because the enforcement needed to happen at the account structure level, before a transaction is posted, not after an accountant reviews the books.

Get the accounting architecture right first. Everything else follows from there.

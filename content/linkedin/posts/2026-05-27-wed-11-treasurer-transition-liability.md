When a new treasurer takes over an HOA board, they inherit the community's financial records, the reserve fund balances, and the compliance status of every obligation the prior board carried.

They do not automatically inherit personal liability for prior board decisions. But they do inherit the obligation to understand what those decisions were and what exposure the community now carries.

A new treasurer who receives a reserve study that is three years past its required update cycle has two choices. Commission the update and bring the community into compliance. Or continue operating from the expired study and now own the decision to do so.

The first action a new HOA treasurer should take is a review of the reserve fund status. Current study? Check. Percent-funded status? Calculate it. Commingling risk? Review the account history. Funding plan adopted? Confirm it exists and is reflected in the current budget.

This review protects the incoming treasurer from inheriting predecessor problems they could have identified at transition. If the review finds problems, the new treasurer can document the findings and the remediation plan.

What the new treasurer cannot do is assume everything was handled. Assumption is not a defense.

A clean financial transition requires documentation from both sides. The outgoing treasurer should produce it. The incoming treasurer should demand it.

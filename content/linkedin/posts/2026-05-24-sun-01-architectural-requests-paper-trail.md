The board approved a fence modification. There is no written record. Three years later, the homeowner sells and the new buyer asks the board to approve a different fence. Now what?

The board can't point to precedent. The original homeowner doesn't remember exactly what was approved. The board has to make a new decision, possibly inconsistent with the first one, and someone ends up unhappy.

This is why architectural request records matter beyond the individual transaction.

What every architectural request record should include:

- Homeowner name and unit
- Date of request and description of the modification
- Any plans or specifications submitted
- Board review date
- Decision: approved, denied, or approved with conditions
- Any conditions attached to the approval (materials, colors, setbacks)
- Signature or attestation from the board officer who reviewed it

The record protects the board against selective enforcement claims. If an owner argues that a similar modification was approved for a neighbor, the board can pull up the record and show exactly what was approved, under what conditions, and when.

Florida's §720.303(5) requires these records to be maintained and accessible. California's §4525 overlaps. But regardless of state, the paper trail is how the board proves it treated owners consistently.

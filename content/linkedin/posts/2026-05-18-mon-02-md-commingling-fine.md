Maryland HB 292 (2025) made reserve funding mandatory, and board members who let the reserve plan drift carry the fiduciary exposure.

HB 292 (Chapter 519, effective October 1, 2025) requires Maryland HOAs to adopt a funding plan in consultation with the reserve study author and to deposit reserve contributions matching the most recent reserve study's recommended amount by the end of each fiscal year. The reserve provisions live in Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2 (annual budget) and §11B-112.3 (reserve study).

"Commingling" means keeping operating and reserve funds mixed in the same account without structural separation. That undermines the §11B-112.2 budget alignment that HB 292 now requires. It happens when a board inherits a bad setup from the prior treasurer and doesn't know to fix it. It happens when accounting software doesn't enforce the separation. It happens when the board uses QuickBooks and no one set up separate funds correctly.

Enforcement runs through the Maryland Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division under the Maryland Condominium Act and Homeowners Association Act. Board members who approved a budget that doesn't fund reserves per the plan, or who let reserve funds drain into operating use, carry personal fiduciary liability.

If your board is in Maryland and you're not certain your fund accounts are structurally separated (not just labeled), and your funding plan is documented and on schedule, that's worth checking today.

Virginia is listed in Gavelhouse's compliance research under mandate states.

The cited statute is the Virginia Property Owners' Association Act section 55.1-1826. The study frequency is at least every five years, and the requirement says the reserve study must include component analysis and a funding plan.

That phrase should matter to every board member.

Component analysis connects the reserve account to real property obligations: roofs, roads, siding, mechanical systems, and other common elements the association must maintain.

A funding plan then asks whether the board is actually saving for those obligations.

If the board only watches cash balance, it can miss the bigger question: does the money match the component timeline?

That is where reserve software should help.

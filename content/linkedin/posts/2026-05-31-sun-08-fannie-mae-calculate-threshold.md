How do you calculate whether your association meets the Fannie Mae 15% threshold?

Under Lender Letter LL-2026-03, effective January 4, 2027, the check is straightforward: reserve contributions divided by total assessment income must be at least 15%.

Here is the math.

Take your current annual budget. Find the line that shows reserve fund contributions (sometimes labeled "reserve transfer" or "reserve deposit"). Find the line that shows total assessment income.

Divide the reserve contribution by the total assessment income. Multiply by 100 to get the percentage.

Example: a community collects $240,000 in annual assessments and contributes $28,800 to reserves. That is exactly 12%. Below the 15% floor by 3 percentage points. To close the gap, the community would need to contribute $36,000 to reserves. With 80 homes, that is $90 more per home per year, or $7.50 per month per home.

The dollar gap is often smaller than boards assume. The political challenge of raising dues is the real obstacle, not the math.

Fannie Mae's rule gives boards a concrete, external justification for the conversation. The choice is a $7.50 monthly dues increase or the risk that homeowners face mortgage rejection at closing after January 4, 2027.

What does that math look like for your community?

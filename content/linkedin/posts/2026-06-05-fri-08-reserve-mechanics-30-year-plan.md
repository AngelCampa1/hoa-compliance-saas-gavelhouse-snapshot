A 30-year funding plan can feel abstract.

Most volunteer boards are worried about this year's dues, this year's insurance, this year's projects, and this year's owner complaints.

But the common elements do not operate on board terms. Roofs, pavement, siding, structural components, and major systems age on their own schedule.

Gavelhouse tracks 30-year planning in multiple places, including Washington's reserve study requirement and California's annual disclosure framework.

The value of a long plan is not perfect prediction.

It forces the board to connect short-term dues decisions with long-term property obligations.

Without that connection, the board can look responsible for one budget year while quietly pushing costs onto future owners.

A 30-year funding plan is not optional in Washington State. It is a statutory requirement.

RCW §64.34.382 (condos) and §64.90 (common interest communities) require reserve studies at least every three years, and each study must include a 30-year funding plan.

The 30-year plan matters because component replacement costs are not evenly distributed across time. A community might face minimal expenditures for several years, then $400,000 in roof replacements all at once. The plan models the timing of those spikes so assessments can be smoothed.

Boards that manage reserves without a 30-year plan often discover the problem the hard way: a major component fails, the reserve account cannot cover it, and a special assessment goes out to every homeowner.

Washington is not alone. California requires the same planning horizon under Civil Code §5550. Colorado requires reserve studies every five years under §38-33.3-209.5.

The 30-year plan is also the document lenders and buyers increasingly want to see when evaluating a community's financial health ahead of a sale.

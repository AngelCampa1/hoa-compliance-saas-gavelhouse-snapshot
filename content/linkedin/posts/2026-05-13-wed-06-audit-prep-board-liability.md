An HOA audit does not just check the math. It checks whether your fund structure is legally compliant.

An auditor reviewing a self-managed community's books will look for: separate reserve and operating accounts, a reserve study that supports the current funding level, reconciliation between the bank statements and the ledger, and documentation for significant disbursements.

Finding commingled funds in an audit is a statutory violation in every mandate state. It is also a finding that board members will need to explain, because the auditor's report goes to the full membership.

The personal liability exposure for board members increases when an audit finds violations, because the audit creates a documented record that the violation existed and was known. A board member who receives an audit finding and takes no corrective action is in a worse position than one who receives the finding and immediately moves to correct it.

Maryland's $10,000 penalty, Florida's $5,000 penalty, and California's daily fine schedule are all penalty structures that become more likely to be enforced once a formal finding exists.

Clean financials before the audit is the only strategy that reliably works.

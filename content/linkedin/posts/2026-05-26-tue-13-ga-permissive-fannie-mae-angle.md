Georgia HOA law authorizes reserve funds. It does not require them.

There is no Georgia statute mandating a reserve study, a funding plan, or a minimum contribution level. The fiduciary duty that attaches to Georgia board members through common law and governing documents is the primary accountability mechanism.

Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03, effective January 4, 2027, sets a 15 percent reserve allocation requirement for Full Review loan applications. It applies to Georgia HOAs on the same terms as it applies to HOAs in states with formal reserve mandates.

Georgia communities where reserves are chronically underfunded, or where the budget does not allocate at least 15 percent to reserves, will affect mortgage qualification for buyers in those communities. Lenders will pull the financials. The reserve percentage will be below the floor. The Full Review path will be unavailable.

This is a property value issue as much as a compliance issue. Georgia HOA boards that want their community to remain attractive to buyers using conventional financing have until January 4, 2027, to get reserve allocations to 15 percent or above.

The state not requiring it does not mean the market will ignore it.

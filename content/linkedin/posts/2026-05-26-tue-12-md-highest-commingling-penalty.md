Maryland HB 292 (2025) made reserve funding mandatory under the HOA Act. Account separation is part of meeting that obligation.

HB 107 (2022, Chapter 664) required Maryland associations to commission reserve studies. HB 292 (2025, Chapter 519, effective October 1, 2025) added a mandatory funding plan. Together they amend Md. Real Prop. §11B-112.2 (annual budget reserves) and §11B-112.3 (reserve study).

Maryland Homeowners Association Act §11B-112.2 now requires associations to fund reserves per the most recent reserve study's recommended amount, not merely to disclose the balance.

For Maryland boards managing community finances in general-purpose accounting software, the risk is structural. QuickBooks and similar tools do not enforce account separation. There is no system-level barrier to transferring money between accounts labeled "reserves" and "operations." The funding obligation exists in the statute. The enforcement gap exists in the software.

A mandatory funding plan under §11B-112.2, combined with the reserve study requirement under §11B-112.3, means Maryland HOA boards have two distinct compliance obligations to track: account separation and the recommended funding level. Enforcement runs through the Maryland Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division.

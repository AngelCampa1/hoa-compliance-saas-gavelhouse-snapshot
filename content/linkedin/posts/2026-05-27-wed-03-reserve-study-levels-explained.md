Reserve studies come in three standard levels, defined by the Community Associations Institute and widely recognized by reserve study professionals.

Level 1, Full Study with Site Visit: The reserve professional physically inspects the community, measures and evaluates each component, and produces a funding plan. This is the most comprehensive and the most expensive. California's three-year requirement and Florida's SIRS requirement are satisfied by Level 1 studies.

Level 2, Update with Site Visit: The prior study is updated based on a new site visit. Useful life and replacement cost estimates are refreshed. No new component list is built from scratch, but the existing list is validated. Less expensive than a full Level 1.

Level 3, Update without Site Visit: The prior study is updated based on new financial data provided by the board, without a site visit. The lowest cost option. Useful for maintaining current funding calculations between full studies, but relies on the accuracy of the information the board provides.

State statutes specify which level satisfies the requirement. Most mandate states require at minimum a Level 2 update on renewal cycles and a Level 1 on the initial or periodic full-cycle study.

A board that purchases a Level 3 when the state requires a Level 1 has not satisfied the statutory requirement, regardless of what the study document says.

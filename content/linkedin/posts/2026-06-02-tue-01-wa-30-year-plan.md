Washington is one of the states where reserve planning has real statutory structure.

Gavelhouse tracks RCW 64.34.382 for condos and RCW 64.90 for common interest communities. It also says the reserve study must be updated at least every three years and must include a 30-year funding plan.

That last part matters.

A study that names the roof, pavement, siding, and major mechanical systems is only half the job. The board also needs a plan for how money gets into reserves over time.

For a volunteer treasurer, the hard part is usually not understanding the idea. It is keeping the study date, component list, funding plan, and annual budget tied together after the meeting ends.

That is the operating problem Gavelhouse is built around.

The Surfside collapse changed what legislators and lenders think about reserve compliance. It changed what we built.

After Champlain Towers South collapsed in 2021, Florida passed SB 4-D in 2022. The law mandated structural integrity reserve studies (SIRS) for condos three stories and above, every 10 years. It banned reserve waivers for structural components. HB 913 in 2025 extended those requirements further.

Fannie Mae updated its reserve requirements. Lenders started scrutinizing reserve fund disclosures more carefully. The conversation shifted from "is this a nice-to-have" to "reserves are a structural safety issue."

We were building Gavelhouse during this period. The legislative response made one thing clear: reserve fund compliance was moving from a self-policing obligation to a regulated one with enforcement teeth.

That shaped how we prioritized the product. We didn't build reserve compliance as a feature category alongside dues collection and document storage. We built it as the foundation the rest of the product sits on.

Whether that was the right call from a product growth standpoint, I genuinely don't know yet. What I know is that the legislative environment for reserve requirements is tightening, not loosening, and boards need tools that keep up with it.

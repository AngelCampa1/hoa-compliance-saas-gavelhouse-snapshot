Underfunded reserves do not just affect your mortgage eligibility. They affect what your home is worth.

When Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03 takes effect January 4, 2027, communities below 15% reserve allocation will face a narrower pool of eligible buyers. Buyers using conventional financing who go through Full Review will encounter a lender check that the community cannot pass.

Fewer eligible buyers means less competition for units. Less competition means lower sale prices.

This is the property value channel that boards rarely model when they are making the case to homeowners that a dues increase is necessary.

The case is not "we need to save money for future repairs." The case is "if we do not fund reserves at 15% of budget, your home becomes harder to sell and worth less on the open market."

That argument lands differently in a homeowner meeting.

Some boards have been trying for years to get homeowners to approve a reserve increase and failing. LL-2026-03 changes the framing from financial prudence to immediate transaction risk.

Whether that changes the vote depends on the community. But the argument exists now in a way it did not two years ago.

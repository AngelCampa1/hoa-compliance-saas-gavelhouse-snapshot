When board members change, reserve fund liabilities do not reset. Incoming boards inherit the problems they did not create.

A self-managed HOA with commingled accounts, an outdated reserve study, and three years of underfunding does not become compliant when the treasurer changes. The compliance obligations belong to the association, and the current board is responsible for the association's current state.

The transition period is when problems most commonly surface. An incoming treasurer requests the financial records and finds: one bank account for everything, QuickBooks with no reserve tracking structure, and a reserve study from 2017.

At that point, the incoming board has a choice: document the findings, begin corrective action, and create a record that shows the new board identified the problems and addressed them; or continue without acknowledgment, which converts a pre-existing problem into a current board problem.

The documentation choice is the correct one, even if the corrective action takes time. A board that discovers pre-existing violations and documents them as part of a transition review is in a fundamentally different legal position than one that was informed of problems and took no action.

For incoming boards: request a full compliance review in the first 90 days. The reserve fund structure is the first thing to check.

New Jersey says your reserve balance can never drop below zero.

N.J.S.A. 46:8B-19 establishes a baseline reserve funding requirement that cannot be waived. The balance cannot go negative. This is not a recommended target. It is a statutory floor.

S2760 (2024) and S3992 (2025) extended these requirements further. The legislative trend in New Jersey is toward more stringent reserve requirements, not less.

For boards in New Jersey, this means a budget that shows reserves drawing down to zero is already approaching a compliance line. A special assessment that depletes reserves to fund operations crosses it.

The board members who approved that budget bear fiduciary responsibility for maintaining the floor.

Most self-managed boards in New Jersey inherited their financial structure from a prior board or a developer turnover. They're running on whatever system was in place when they took over. Whether that system tracks the reserve balance against the statutory floor in real time is a question worth asking.

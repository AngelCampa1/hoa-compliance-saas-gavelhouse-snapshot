N.J.S.A. 46:8B-19 requires separate reserve accounts. New Jersey's reserve fund cannot go negative.

The Condominium Act in New Jersey has two distinct requirements that work together: separate accounts (§46:8B-19) and a minimum balance floor that cannot drop below zero (established by S2760 (2024) and S3992 (2025)).

The zero floor is unusual in state law. Most states require adequate funding but do not set an absolute floor. New Jersey went further, recognizing that communities that empty their reserves entirely are in a structurally different and more dangerous position than communities that are underfunded but positive.

For New Jersey boards, the practical compliance checklist has two items: (1) a separate bank account for reserve funds, with no operating deposits, and (2) a reserve balance above zero at all times.

Both requirements are independently enforceable. A board that maintains a separate account but draws it down to zero is in violation of the floor requirement. A board that keeps a positive balance but pools it with operating funds is in violation of the separate account requirement.

The New Jersey framework is a useful model for understanding what adequate reserve compliance looks like at a structural level.

I chose flat pricing for Gavelhouse because HOA boards have to get software approved by a vote.

That's not a marketing angle. It's a real constraint.

When a treasurer wants to switch to new software, they present it to the full board, sometimes at an annual meeting, sometimes in a regular meeting agenda item. The board votes. The cost comes out of the operating budget. It has to make sense to people who are not software buyers.

Per-unit pricing creates a problem in that meeting. "It depends on how many units we have" is a hard answer to defend when board members are asking what they're approving. The conversation shifts to debating the formula instead of evaluating the software.

Flat pricing is: $27/month billed annually with Y80OFF. That's it. For up to 200 homes.

The board can approve or reject that in one meeting. There's no follow-up question about growth. There's no risk that the price changes if the community adds a new phase.

I also wanted to build a product that volunteer boards on tight budgets could actually afford. Flat pricing makes that easier to predict, easier to budget, and easier to justify.

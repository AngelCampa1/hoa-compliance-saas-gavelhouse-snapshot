If your HOA is in California, Florida, Maryland, Washington, or New Jersey, your state has a specific law against commingling reserve and operating funds.

"Commingling" means putting reserve money and operating money in the same account, or routinely moving reserve funds to cover operating shortfalls without a proper vote and documentation.

It sounds like an accounting technicality. It isn't. Boards that commingle expose themselves to personal liability claims from owners. The board members, not the association, can be held responsible.

The statutes vary by state. California's Civil Code §5510 addresses it. Florida's §720.303 is even more explicit. Washington's RCW 64.34.364 covers condos.

If you're in one of the states above, your exact statutory language is in there. If you're in a state with no specific reserve law, the Fannie Mae requirements that take effect January 4, 2027 still apply.

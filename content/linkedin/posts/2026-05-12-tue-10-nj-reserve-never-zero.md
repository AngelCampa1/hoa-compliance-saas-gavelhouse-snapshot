New Jersey: your reserve balance can never drop below zero. That is now state law.

S2760 (2024) and S3992 (2025) established baseline funding rules under the Condominium Act (N.J.S.A. 46:8B). A New Jersey HOA or condo board cannot draw reserves down to nothing, even if the board votes to do so.

N.J.S.A. 46:8B-19 also requires separate reserve accounts. Commingling operating and reserve funds is a violation regardless of whether the balance is positive.

The zero-floor rule exists because communities that emptied their reserves often could not recover without significant special assessments or loans. The legislature decided that particular outcome was worth preventing by statute.

For New Jersey boards, this means the question is no longer "should we fund reserves?" The question is "how far above zero are we, and is that enough?"

If the answer to the second question is "barely above zero," the board has a planning problem even if it does not technically have a legal violation yet.

Utah Community Association Act §57-8a requires associations to disclose the reserve balance to homeowners and to conduct a reserve study at least every six years.

The disclosure obligation is annual. The study obligation is every six years.

That gap creates a problem. If the reserve study is conducted in year one, the disclosed reserve balance in years two through six is a current number from the association's accounts. But the context for that balance, the percent-funded status, the component list, the funding plan adequacy, is based on a study that is one, two, three, four, or five years old.

A reserve balance of $300,000 means something different when the study shows the fully funded balance should be $310,000 versus when it should be $900,000. The disclosure of the balance alone does not tell homeowners which situation they are in.

Utah boards that are mid-cycle on their six-year study obligation should consider whether their annual reserve balance disclosures are accompanied by enough context for homeowners to understand the funded status, not just the account balance.

A six-year study cycle is a long time in construction cost terms. Boards that are at year four or five since the last study are working from cost estimates that may be significantly out of date.

Per-unit pricing means your HOA software gets more expensive as your community adds homes.

A 75-unit community at $3/unit pays $225/month. When Phase 2 adds 50 homes and you're at 125 units, you're at $375/month. Same software. Same features. More money because the community got bigger.

The work the software does didn't increase proportionally. The compliance obligations didn't increase. The reserve fund doesn't become more complex because there are 50 more homes. The reporting requirements didn't change.

The vendor's costs to serve your account didn't meaningfully increase either. Modern SaaS doesn't have significant marginal cost per additional user or unit.

Per-unit pricing exists because it's a pricing model that grows revenue automatically as communities scale. It's good for the vendor. It's a perverse incentive for the customer.

EasyHOA charges $3/unit/month. Effortless HOA charges $3/home/month. Vinteum charges $0.79 to $1.99/unit/month.

Gavelhouse charges about $27/month billed annually with Y80OFF for up to 200 homes. If your community grows from 80 to 150 homes, your software cost stays at about $27/month billed annually with Y80OFF.

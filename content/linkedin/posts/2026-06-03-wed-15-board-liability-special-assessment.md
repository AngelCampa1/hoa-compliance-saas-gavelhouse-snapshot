A special assessment may be necessary. It may even be the responsible choice.

But boards should be honest about what it usually signals: the association has an obligation that current cash and current reserve funding cannot absorb smoothly.

The compliance research frames reserve planning around study requirements, 30-year plans, percent-funded disclosures, baseline funding, and Fannie Mae's 2027 reserve floor.

Those are all ways of forcing the same conversation earlier.

What repairs are coming? What will they cost? What has the board already saved? What has the board told homeowners? What record shows the directors reviewed the risk?

When those questions are delayed, the special assessment becomes the meeting where everyone finally pays attention.

That is a rough way to govern.

QuickBooks can label accounts whatever you want. It cannot prevent a transaction from posting to the wrong one.

This is the core problem with using general-purpose accounting software for HOA reserve fund management.

Create an account called "Reserve Fund" in QuickBooks. Create another called "Operating Fund." Label them clearly. Train your treasurer to post transactions correctly.

None of that stops a transaction from being entered in the wrong account. None of that enforces the structural separation that state law requires. None of that generates an alert when reserve funds fall below the statutory minimum. None of that produces a compliant reserve fund disclosure report.

QuickBooks is an excellent accounting tool for businesses where the accounting requirements are general. HOA reserve funds have specific statutory requirements that a general accounting tool doesn't know exist.

California, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey, and Washington all have statutory requirements around fund separation. The requirement is not "label your accounts correctly." It is that the funds are structurally separate and demonstrably so.

A QuickBooks setup that has been working for years may still have a compliance gap that only becomes visible during an audit or a homeowner dispute.

Replacement cost estimates in a reserve study are snapshots. They reflect labor and materials costs at the time the study was conducted.

Construction costs, roofing materials, concrete, and skilled trades have all experienced significant price increases in recent years. A reserve study from 2021 that estimated component replacement costs using 2021 prices is now materially understating what replacements will actually cost.

This does not require any unusual inflation assumption. Normal construction cost escalation of three to five percent per year compounded over four years produces a 13 to 22 percent gap between a 2021 estimate and today's replacement cost.

For a community with $500,000 in reserve components, that gap is $65,000 to $110,000.

Boards that are still operating from a 2021 reserve study are running a funding plan that is systematically underfunded relative to today's actual replacement costs.

This is one reason reserve professionals recommend updating the cost estimates even in years when no full study is required. The funding plan is based on the cost estimates. If the cost estimates are wrong, the plan is wrong.

Some states, including California and Washington, require a study update every three years. For communities in states with longer cycles, more frequent informal cost reviews make sense.

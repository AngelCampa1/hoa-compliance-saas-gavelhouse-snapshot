Boards often treat the reserve study like an outside report.

Someone orders it. A specialist sends a PDF. The board files it. Then the budget conversation goes back to dues, vendors, and whether there is enough operating cash.

That breaks the point of the study.

The study names the major components the association is responsible for and ties those obligations to a funding plan. In Washington, the requirement says that plan must cover 30 years. In California, percent-funded status and a 30-year plan are part of the annual disclosure framework.

The board's job is to connect the document to monthly financial operations.

If the study lives outside the finance workflow, it becomes advice the board can accidentally ignore.

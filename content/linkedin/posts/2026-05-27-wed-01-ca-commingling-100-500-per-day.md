California Civil Code §5510 is explicit: reserve funds cannot be commingled with operating funds.

The penalty is $100 to $500 per day. Not per incident. Per day that the violation continues.

A California HOA that transfers reserve funds to the operating account on January 1 and corrects it on March 31 has accumulated 90 days of potential daily penalties. At $500 per day, that is $45,000.

The Davis-Stirling Act's commingling prohibition exists alongside the reserve study requirement (§5550), the percent-funded disclosure requirement, and the three-year study cycle. These are separate obligations. Meeting the study requirement does not satisfy the commingling prohibition, and vice versa.

California boards using general-purpose accounting software that allows free movement between accounts are operating with a structural compliance gap. The software does not know about §5510. It will not stop a transfer. The board member who makes that transfer will still have made it, regardless of whether the software flagged anything.

The three laws passed in 2024 and 2025 (SB 900, AB 2114, SB 410) did not remove the §5510 commingling prohibition. They layered on top of it.

California HOA boards should verify, with their accounting records, that reserve and operating funds have remained fully separated.

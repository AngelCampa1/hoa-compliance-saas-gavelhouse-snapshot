The component inventory in a condominium reserve study typically looks very different from the component inventory in a single-family HOA.

Condo components often include: building exterior (roof, facade, windows), elevators and mechanical systems, common area HVAC, fire suppression systems, plumbing within common areas, parking structures, and shared amenities.

HOA components in a single-family community typically include: street pavement and curbs, community entrance features, amenity buildings, fencing, pools and equipment, exterior painting for maintained structures, and shared landscaping infrastructure.

The distinction matters for compliance in several ways.

Florida's SIRS requirement applies to condo and co-op buildings three stories or taller. It does not apply to single-family HOA communities in the same way. The component focus for a Florida high-rise is structural in ways that a Florida single-family HOA reserve study is not.

A reserve study template designed for a single-family HOA may miss critical components for a condominium, and vice versa. Boards that use generic templates without professional guidance risk an incomplete component analysis.

The funding implications compound over time. Missing components today means missing contributions today. By the time the omitted component needs replacement, the shortfall may not be recoverable without a special assessment.

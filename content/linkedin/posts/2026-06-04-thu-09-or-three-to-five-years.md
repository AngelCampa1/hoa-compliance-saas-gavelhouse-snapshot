Gavelhouse tracks Oregon under mandate states.

It cites ORS section 100.175, says the reserve study frequency is every three to five years, and says the study must include a funding plan.

For boards, that turns reserve work into a recurring cycle.

Study. Funding plan. Budget. Review. Update. Repeat.

The weak point is usually the handoff between those steps. The study gets completed, the budget gets passed, and the update date gets buried somewhere the next board will not find until too late.

Oregon boards should treat the reserve study date as an operational deadline, the same way they would treat insurance renewal or tax filing.

If the date matters, it belongs in the system.

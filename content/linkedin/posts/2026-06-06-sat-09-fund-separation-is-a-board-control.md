Reserve and operating separation protects more than the chart of accounts.

It protects the board conversation.

When funds are separated, a board can see what is available for regular expenses and what is committed to long-term components. When they are blended, every decision gets softer. A repair feels affordable because the balance looks healthy.

Several states in Gavelhouse's compliance research call out commingling directly, including California, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey, and Washington.

The practical lesson is broader than any one statute: do not let short-term cash hide long-term obligations.

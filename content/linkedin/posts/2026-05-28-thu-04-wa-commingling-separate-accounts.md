Washington Revised Code §64.34.364 requires associations to maintain reserve funds in an account separate from the operating fund.

Separate accounts means separate bank accounts. A single bank account with a column in a spreadsheet labeled "reserves" does not satisfy the RCW requirement. The funds must be held in distinct accounts at the financial institution.

Washington's three-year reserve study cycle under RCW §64.34.382 and the 30-year plan requirement both assume the reserves being analyzed are held in appropriately segregated accounts. If the reserves are commingled, the study is analyzing a pool that cannot be reliably attributed to reserve purposes.

Self-managed Washington HOAs that maintain a single operating account and track reserves as a spreadsheet line item are not in compliance with RCW 64.34.364, regardless of whether the numbers in that spreadsheet are accurate.

The fix is straightforward. Open a dedicated reserve account at the association's bank. Transfer the reserve balance. Maintain separate records. Going forward, reserve contributions flow to the reserve account and reserve expenditures come from it.

The complication is that doing this correctly requires reconciling the actual reserve balance against what the study says the balance should be. Many boards discover the reserve balance when they segregate it is lower than expected.

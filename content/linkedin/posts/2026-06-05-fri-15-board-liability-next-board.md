Most volunteer board terms are shorter than the life of the assets the board is responsible for.

That mismatch is the heart of reserve governance.

A board can defer a transfer this year, keep dues lower, and leave office before the roof, pavement, siding, or structural issue becomes urgent. The next board inherits the math.

That is one reason reserve study cycles, funding plans, percent-funded status, separate accounts, and board records matter.

They make long-term obligations harder to hide inside short-term politics.

The current board does not have to solve every future problem. It does have to make decisions with a clear view of the obligation and leave behind a record the next board can understand.

That is basic stewardship.

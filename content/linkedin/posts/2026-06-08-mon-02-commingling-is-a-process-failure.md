Boards rarely set out to mix reserve and operating funds.

It happens because the system lets the distinction fade.

One bank balance gets discussed in meetings. One spreadsheet gets updated. One volunteer knows which line items are really reserve obligations. Then a project arrives, and everyone realizes the records were too loose.

Gavelhouse calls out states where commingling is prohibited, including California, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey, and Washington.

But even outside those examples, the operating lesson is the same: if the system does not separate funds clearly, the board has to do it from memory.

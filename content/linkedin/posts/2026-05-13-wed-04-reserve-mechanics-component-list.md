A reserve study starts with a component inventory. Most boards are missing half the list.

The study identifies every major common element with a useful life and a replacement cost. Roof. Parking lot. Pool and pool equipment. Elevators. HVAC systems. Exterior painting. Fencing. Drainage systems. The list for a typical community is longer than most boards expect.

Once the component list is complete, the study estimates: current replacement cost, remaining useful life, and the amount that should be saved each year to cover replacement when it arrives.

The percent-funded status is the ratio of what you have saved to what the study says you should have saved. A community with $200,000 in reserves against $1,000,000 in anticipated replacement costs is 20% funded, even if that $200,000 feels like a lot of money.

California's Civil Code §5550 requires the full component inventory and the funding analysis as part of the mandatory study, along with a 30-year plan. The study is not just a number. It is a document that must hold up to regulatory scrutiny.

If your community's reserve study is more than three years old, the component costs in it are already outdated.

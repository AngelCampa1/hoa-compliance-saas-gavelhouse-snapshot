Your HOA's reserve shortfall can block your neighbor's mortgage application.

Starting January 4, 2027, Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03 requires Full Review loan applications to confirm that the association allocates at least 15% of its budget to reserves.

If the association fails that check, the lender cannot approve the conventional loan. The buyer does not close. The seller's deal falls through.

The board made a financial decision. A homeowner pays for it at closing.

This is the dynamic that self-managed volunteer boards often do not see coming. Reserve funding is typically viewed as a long-term capital question. LL-2026-03 turns it into an immediate transaction-blocking problem.

Boards have roughly 7 months before the effective date. For communities already underfunded, the options are: raise dues to hit 15%, apply a special assessment to reserves, or watch property marketability decline.

None of those are easy conversations. But the conversation your board needs to have this quarter is simpler: what is our current reserve allocation percentage?

If you do not know that number, start there.

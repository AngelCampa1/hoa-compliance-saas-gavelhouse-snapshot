Volunteer HOA boards are a different product category than professional management companies. We built for one of them.

The distinction matters more than it sounds.

Professional management companies have dedicated accounting staff, compliance teams, and software vendors who provide training, implementation support, and account management. Their software can be complex, expensive, and require onboarding weeks because there are people whose job it is to manage that process.

Volunteer boards have a treasurer who does this in their evenings and weekends. The onboarding window is measured in hours, not days. The user interface needs to be clear enough that a homeowner who volunteered for the role can do the job without compliance expertise.

The compliance requirements are identical. The operational capacity is completely different.

Every platform decision we make at Gavelhouse goes through this filter: will a volunteer treasurer who has never used HOA software before be able to do this correctly without training? If the answer is no, the design is wrong.

That's why we built reserve compliance into the workflow rather than into a documentation sidebar. The person using the software shouldn't need to know California's Davis-Stirling requirements to get California-compliant outputs.

Volunteer boards deserve software built for their reality. Not enterprise tools scaled down and re-priced.

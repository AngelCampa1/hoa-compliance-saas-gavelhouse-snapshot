Arizona authorizes reserve funds for HOAs but does not mandate them. No reserve study requirement. No minimum funding level. No commingling prohibition baked into statute.

Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03, effective January 4, 2027, requires a minimum 15 percent reserve allocation for associations under Full Review.

Arizona HOAs that have never funded reserves, or that fund reserves at a token level, will affect mortgage availability for every homeowner who tries to sell after that date.

The buyer's lender will ask for the association's financial package. The reserve allocation percentage will be in that package. If it falls below 15 percent, the loan does not qualify for Full Review under the updated Fannie Mae guidelines.

The seller's deal falls apart. The seller's neighbor's deal may also fall apart. The whole community becomes harder to sell into.

Arizona boards that treat reserves as optional are not wrong under current Arizona law. They are building a problem that will surface at closing tables across their community starting in January 2027.

Fiduciary duty is active in Arizona regardless of whether the state mandate exists.

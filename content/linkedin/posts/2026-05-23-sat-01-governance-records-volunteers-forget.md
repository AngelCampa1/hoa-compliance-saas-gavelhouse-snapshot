Three things volunteer boards consistently forget to document, and why each one matters when the board turns over.

**1. Vendor selection rationale.**
The board got three bids and chose the middle one. Why? The reasoning existed during the meeting. Six months later, when an owner asks why the association is paying $18,000 for landscaping, nobody can reconstruct it. Document the bid comparison in the meeting minutes.

**2. Rule interpretations.**
The board decided that the fence rule applies to front yards but not side yards facing common areas. That decision isn't in the CC&Rs. It was a board ruling. If it's not written down somewhere, the next board will make a different ruling and the homeowner who built under the old interpretation has a legitimate grievance.

**3. Reserve study adoption.**
Did the board formally adopt the reserve study? Did they vote to waive or reduce the recommended contribution? That vote needs to be on record, because if a special assessment becomes necessary five years later, owners will ask whether the board followed the study's recommendations.

Volunteer boards move fast and trust each other. That's actually appropriate for a small community. But the records need to exist for the people who come after.

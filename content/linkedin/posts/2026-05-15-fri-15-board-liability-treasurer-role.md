The HOA treasurer role carries direct personal exposure for financial mismanagement. Volunteer does not mean immune.

The HOA treasurer has direct responsibility for financial record-keeping, fund management, and reserve compliance. In most state statutes, the treasurer is the officer accountable for ensuring accounts are properly structured and funds are not commingled.

When an audit finds commingled accounts, the treasurer is the first officer named in the finding. When a homeowner brings a claim for financial mismanagement, the treasurer is typically the first board member named as a defendant.

The fiduciary duty standard applies to all board members, but the treasurer's functional responsibility for the accounting makes the exposure more direct. A treasurer who delegates accounting to a bookkeeper still has the obligation to confirm the bookkeeper's work product is compliant.

In Florida, that means confirming the reserve and operating accounts are separate. In California, it means confirming the reserve study is current and the annual disclosure is accurate. In Maryland, it means confirming the account structure satisfies §11B-112.2.

The practical risk management: know your state's requirements, confirm the accounting structure satisfies them, document that you reviewed and confirmed compliance at each budget cycle. If the structure is wrong, fix it and document the correction.

D&O insurance is your backstop. Clean financials are your primary protection.

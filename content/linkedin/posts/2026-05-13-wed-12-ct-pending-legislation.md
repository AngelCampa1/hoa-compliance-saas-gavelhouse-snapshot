Connecticut currently requires reserve balance disclosure. SB 212 would mandate the study itself.

The Common Interest Ownership Act §47-261 requires Connecticut associations to disclose reserve fund balance in annual financial reports. That is a disclosure requirement, not a study mandate.

SB 212 is pending legislation that would change Connecticut's status from a disclosure state to a mandate state. If it passes, Connecticut boards would be required to commission a full reserve study, not just report the current balance.

The difference matters for boards that are currently in compliance with the disclosure requirement but have not done a study. Disclosing a balance is straightforward. Producing a study with a 30-year funding plan is a different level of obligation.

Connecticut boards that have not done a reserve study should be watching SB 212. If the bill passes, the window to get the study done on your own timeline, before a regulatory deadline, gets compressed.

Even if SB 212 does not pass this session, the Fannie Mae 15% floor applies to Connecticut communities starting January 4, 2027. That consequence is not contingent on state legislation.

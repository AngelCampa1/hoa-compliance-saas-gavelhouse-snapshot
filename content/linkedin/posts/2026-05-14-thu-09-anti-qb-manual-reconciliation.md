Every manual reconciliation step is a place where a commingling violation can be created.

When a board uses QuickBooks for HOA accounting, the typical workflow involves: the treasurer or bookkeeper manually coding transactions to the correct fund, monthly reconciliation between the bank statements and the QuickBooks ledger, and periodic reports that the board uses to confirm reserve and operating balances separately.

Each of those steps requires a human to make the correct choice. The bank does not know whether an incoming dues payment is operating or reserve. QuickBooks does not know. The human knows, and the human can be wrong.

In a mandate state, "wrong" is not just an accounting error. It is a statutory violation. In Maryland, it is a $10,000 statutory violation. In Florida, $5,000. In California, $100-$500 per day.

The manual step is the risk. Removing the manual step removes the risk.

Gavelhouse routes transactions to the correct fund at the time of posting. The treasurer does not need to remember which account. The system enforces the routing based on the transaction type.

That is not a convenience feature. It is a compliance architecture.

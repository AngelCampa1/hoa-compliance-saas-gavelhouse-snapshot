HOA Express is a website builder. It does not do accounting. It does not track reserves. It is not HOA management software.

It ranks in searches for HOA software. It's priced at Free to $79/month. Boards find it, set it up, and think they've solved their HOA management problem.

They've solved their website problem.

A community website is useful. Residents can find documents, announcements, and contact information. But a website does not produce a reserve fund compliance report. It does not enforce fund separation. It does not generate the documentation a board needs when an auditor reviews their records.

A board that has HOA Express and calls their HOA software situation "handled" has a gap they may not know about.

This is a product category problem, not a criticism of HOA Express specifically. When search results mix website builders, payment processors, and full HOA management platforms under the same category label, boards end up comparing tools that don't actually do the same thing.

Verify what the software actually does for reserve fund compliance before signing up. Ask specifically: does it enforce fund separation? Does it generate reserve study reports? Does it track percent funded against your state requirements?

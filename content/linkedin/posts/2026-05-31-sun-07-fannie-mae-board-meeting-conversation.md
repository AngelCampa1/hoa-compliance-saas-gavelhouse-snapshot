The Fannie Mae 2027 rule changes the conversation in HOA board meetings. Here is how.

For years, the reserve funding conversation in board meetings looked like this: the treasurer presents the reserve balance, a board member asks if it is "enough," someone mentions the reserve study, and the discussion ends without a clear resolution on whether dues need to go up.

After January 4, 2027, that conversation has a harder anchor.

Lender Letter LL-2026-03 sets a specific, verifiable threshold: 15% of total assessment income must go to reserves. If your community is at 11%, the gap is quantifiable. The consequence is specific: homeowners trying to sell or refinance may face lender rejection on Full Review applications.

That framing changes the dynamic for boards that have been unable to build consensus on a dues increase. "We should fund reserves at a higher level" is an abstract governance argument. "If we do not get to 15% by January, homeowners will face financing problems" is a transaction-risk argument.

Those arguments land differently with homeowners in a meeting.

Boards have roughly 7 months before the rule takes effect. For most communities with a Q4 budget cycle, the window to get a dues increase approved and implemented before January 4 is closing.

Is the Fannie Mae rule on your next board meeting agenda?

Volunteering for your HOA board does not insulate you from fiduciary liability.

Board members owe a fiduciary duty to the community. That means acting in the community's best interest, managing funds prudently, and following the governing documents and applicable state law.

When reserves are underfunded and a structural component fails, the question auditors and attorneys ask is: did the board know the reserves were inadequate? Did they have access to a reserve study? Did they act on it?

"We didn't know" is a weaker defense than it sounds. State statutes in California, Florida, Maryland, and elsewhere require boards to conduct reserve studies and disclose funding levels. Not knowing is sometimes itself the violation.

D&O insurance helps, but it has limits. Most D&O policies exclude acts that are clearly illegal or grossly negligent. A board that had a reserve study showing 20% funding and ignored it for three years has a harder time claiming coverage.

The strongest protection is documentation. A funded reserve, a current reserve study, and accurate financial records showing the funds are where they're supposed to be.

That's what compliance-first software is designed to produce.

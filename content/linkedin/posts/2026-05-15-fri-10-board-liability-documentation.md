Board meeting minutes are the primary evidence of fiduciary compliance. Most boards do not document reserve discussions adequately.

When a board member's reserve management decisions are challenged, the first document anyone asks for is the meeting minutes. What did the board know? When did they know it? What decisions did they make and why?

Adequate reserve documentation in minutes includes: the current reserve balance, any disbursements approved from reserves, comparison to the funding plan, acknowledgment of the current percent-funded status, and any discussion of needed adjustments.

Most self-managed board minutes say something like "treasurer's report presented." That is not documentation. It is a reference to a conversation that no one can reconstruct.

A board that can show, via minutes, that it reviewed reserve status at every meeting, acknowledged the funding plan, and made deliberate decisions about funding levels is in a strong position if those decisions are later questioned.

A board that cannot produce any reserve discussion in its meeting record is not.

The standard is not perfection. The standard is whether a reasonable person reviewing the minutes would conclude the board took reserve management seriously. That bar is achievable with basic documentation discipline.

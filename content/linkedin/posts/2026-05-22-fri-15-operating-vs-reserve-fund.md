Two funds. Different rules. Most boards confuse them constantly.

**Operating fund:** Money that comes in from regular dues and goes out for recurring expenses. Landscaping. Utilities. Insurance. The day-to-day cost of running the community.

**Reserve fund:** Long-term savings set aside specifically for major repairs and replacements. Roof replacement. Repaving the parking lot. Replacing pool equipment. Things that cost a lot and happen infrequently.

The mistake boards make is treating the reserve fund as a backup account when operating costs run over. It isn't. It's a separate liability bucket with its own rules.

In California, Florida, and Washington, keeping these funds separate is a statutory requirement, not a best practice. CA Civil Code §5510, FL §720.303, and WA RCW 64.34.364 each address this.

Even in states without a specific commingling law, mixing the funds creates problems: your percent-funded calculation is wrong, your reserve study assumptions break down, and any owner who reviews the financials will ask uncomfortable questions.

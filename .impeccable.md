## Design Context

### Users
Volunteer HOA board members — treasurers, presidents, secretaries — running self-managed communities of up to 500 homes. They use the dashboard during working sessions (evenings, weekends) to manage compliance, finances, and governance workflows. They are not designers or tech workers; they're accountable people who need clarity, trust, and confidence from the interface. They need to find information fast and act with certainty.

The marketing site (`apps/web`) is the first-impression layer targeting the same people before they sign up. It must establish authority and trust quickly.

### Brand Personality
**Compliance-first, fiduciary, measured.** Three words: *authoritative, precise, trustworthy*.

Not startup-breezy. Not bureaucratic. The tone is closer to a well-run law office or a state regulator's portal — serious, capable, and honest.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Marketing site (`apps/web`):** Editorial authority. Libre Baskerville headings (serif for gravitas) over a warm parchment surface (#f6efe6). Gold accent (#cb8a2e) for calls to action. Dark navy (#163a5f) as the anchor color. Feels like a well-produced compliance guide, not a SaaS landing page template.

**Dashboard app (`apps/app`):** Clean data utility. Public Sans for all text — no serif, keeps information legible and scannable. Navy primary (#163a5f) for active nav and primary actions, matching the brand color from the marketing site. Warm off-white background (not pure white). Generous radius (14px) for a modern, accessible feel.

**Shared values:**
- Navy + gold palette (never replace with generic blues or grays)
- Public Sans as the universal body/UI font across both apps
- No Inter (overused, zero brand identity)
- No gradient text
- No glassmorphism as decoration
- No side-stripe accent borders on cards/alerts
- No identical card grids of icon + heading + text

### Button Geometry

Buttons are pills. Use fully rounded button geometry (`border-radius: 9999px`, `rounded-full`, or equivalent) for every button and button-styled CTA, including icon buttons, segmented/toggle controls, and link-buttons. Cards, inputs, dialogs, tags, and content containers may keep their own radii; button-like controls should not use square, `rounded-md`, or `rounded-lg` corners unless the user explicitly asks for that exception.
### Design Principles
1. **Compliance authority before beauty** — the interface should feel trustworthy before it feels pretty. Data accuracy, clear hierarchy, and predictable layouts beat decorative flourishes.
2. **Navy is the anchor** — every screen should read navy as the dominant brand signal. Gold is the accent, used sparingly for primary CTAs only.
3. **Public Sans everywhere in product** — no font switching between marketing and app means one less source of disconnect. Marketing earns serif authority; product earns utility clarity.
4. **Spacing creates trust** — generous consistent spacing signals a well-organized system. Cramped layouts signal rushed work.
5. **Text must fit** — no wrapping overflow, no truncated labels, no 60-character lines without a max-width. Readability is non-negotiable.
